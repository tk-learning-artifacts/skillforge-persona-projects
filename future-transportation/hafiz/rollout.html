Still on the road / Rollout plan

Rollout plan

Test it at three crossings. Compare with three that stay the same. Only then scale. 18 months, January 2027 to June 2028.

  1. January to February 2027. 20 real interviews with older residents, through the Active Ageing Centre. Ask about the last time they crossed, not what they want. Pick 6 crossings: 3 for the trial, 3 similar ones as controls.
  2. February to April 2027. Baseline counts at all 6 crossings over 4 weeks. This shows the normal ups and downs.
  3. March to May 2027. LTA reviews the proposal, picks a detector and surveys the poles.
  4. June to July 2027. Install at the 3 trial crossings. Run in shadow mode for 4 weeks. The detector logs what it would do, but does not touch the lights. My learners count at the same time, so we can check the detector against people.
  5. August 2027 to January 2028. Live trial, 6 months. Signs go up on day one.
  6. October 2027 and January 2028. Follow-up counts, same hours and weekdays as the baseline.
  7. February to March 2028. Analysis and a public report in plain English, shared with residents.
  8. April to June 2028. If the trial passes, LTA adds the detector to its upgrade list. Crossings near Silver Zones and Friendly Streets come first.

4.3How we count, fairly

This follows the module's article on fair before and after measuring. It covers a baseline, the same conditions, numbers per person and normal variation.

  • Two volunteers per crossing, 7:30 to 9:30 am and 4:30 to 6:30 pm. Baseline: 6 count days spread over 4 weeks, on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays. Each follow-up: 4 count days.
  • For each cycle we note three things. How many crossed? How many looked 65 or over? How many were still on the road at car green?
  • We report per 100 crossers. Then a busy market day does not look worse just for being busy.
  • We also note near misses: a car brakes hard or swerves for a walker.
  • We log rain and school holidays, and compare like with like.
  • We compare the change at trial crossings with the change at control crossings. If both improve, the sensor did not cause it.
Safety for volunteers Count from the pavement only. Never step onto the road. Wear a high-visibility vest. Work in pairs. Stop if there is lightning.

4.4Go or no-go

I am writing the test before the trial, so I can't move the goalposts later. Scale up only if all four are true:

  1. Older walkers still on the road at car green fall by at least half at trial sites. The fall is clearly bigger than at control sites.
  2. The detector agrees with the volunteers in at least 95 of 100 cycles in shadow mode.
  3. Cars and buses wait no more than 3 extra seconds per cycle on average.
  4. Near misses do not go up.

4.6Risks

Drivers get impatient and jump the lightThe hold is short. Red-light cameras still apply. We count red-light running.

My rubric check

  • Clear grasp of the problem. Real Traffic Police data, read with care about small numbers. The walking speed gap is shown with real studies.
  • Creative, fitting technology. A proven sensor, used for a gap Singapore's current fix leaves. I chose it over AI on purpose.
  • Serves diverse mobility needs. It helps anyone still on the road, with no card and no tap.
  • Realistic to launch. Partly. The plan is careful, but it depends on LTA saying yes. My costs are guesses.
