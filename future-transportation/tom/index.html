Walk the Last Bit

Every school morning, cars queue outside the primary school next to mine. The children who walk pay for it. This is my plan to move the cars back 400 metres. Then we walk the children in together.

I'm Tom. I teach sustainability and social topics through projects, at a secondary school. A primary school shares our road. My Sec 3 project class walks past its gate at 7:15 every day. They picked this problem, and I did the module project alongside them.

My question for any transport idea: who does it help, and who does it leave out?

The problem, with data

I started with national data, then looked at our own gate. The national data comes from the Census of Population 2020, published on data.gov.sg. It counts how resident students usually get to school.

How primary pupils get to school

One primary pupil in five arrives by car. Two in five need no transport at all. Those two groups meet at the gate every morning.

Most car trips to school are short

More than half of car trips to school take 15 minutes or less. Many of those are trips a child could walk, or finish on foot.

The same table shows the other side. 27.1% of students in HDB flats need no transport, against 13.7% of students in other homes.

This is the part my class argued about most. Condos and landed homes usually cost more than flats. So the families who drive tend to be better off. The families who walk past the queue are more often from the flats. The fumes and the risk land on the walkers. One student said: "So the people who make the jam aren't the ones who breathe it." I didn't correct her.

Children with disabilities are three times as likely to use "other modes". These include school buses and vans. They are much less likely to need no transport. Any plan that keeps vehicles away from the gate must still let them through. Otherwise I would be fixing the street for most children by shutting out a few.

What families told us

Made-up interviews These people are invented, like me. We asked about the last time, not "would you ever", as the module's interview article suggests.
  • "Last Tuesday I drove her because it's on my way to work. It's two minutes." A father who lives in a condo 600 m away.
  • "The road at 7:15 is the scary part. Cars everywhere." A mother who drives for that reason. Her car is part of what makes it scary.
  • "My son uses a wheelchair. We have to drop at the gate." A parent who must not be pushed out.
  • "I walk my two grandchildren. I hold both hands and still watch every car." A grandmother from the flats.
  • "I would let him walk if I knew he got there." A mother who works early shifts.
The problem in one paragraph Short car trips pile up at the primary school gate between 7:00 and 7:30. They block the bus stop, leave engines running and make children squeeze between cars. The cost falls hardest on the children who walk, who are more often from the flats. Parents drive partly because the road feels unsafe, which the driving causes. Any fix must keep access for children with disabilities. It must also give working parents what they asked for: knowing their child arrived.

What I'm proposing

Three parts, which only work together:

  1. Drop-and-Walk points. Two places about 400 m away where cars drop children off.
  2. Walking buses. Volunteers walk groups of children in on a timetable. They start at two HDB blocks and pick up at those points.
  3. WalkTap. A tap of a bag tag tells parents their child joined the walk and reached the gate. No tracking in between.

Plus a quiet Gate Zone: no drop-offs at the gate from 7:00 to 7:30. School buses and children who need door access are the exceptions.

See the design, specs and the working app prototype, then the rollout plan.

What I would change

The plan moves cars away from the gate. It does not take many cars off the road. A parent who drove 600 m still drives 400 m of it. Fewer car trips would need safer walking routes from home. That is a job for the town, not the school. My census numbers are also national and from 2020. They describe Singapore, not this school. Before launch I'd want real counts at our gate.
