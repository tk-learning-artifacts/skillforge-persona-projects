Walk the Last Bit / Rollout plan

The rollout plan

Start small with one walking bus in Term 1 of 2027. Grow it term by term. Measure before and after, the same way each time.

How we will know it works

We count before we change anything. Then we count again the same way, on the same weekdays, at the same times. We report numbers per 100 pupils, so a day with many absent pupils does not look like a success.

Risks, and what we do

RiskWhat we do
Volunteers drop outA rota with a backup for each morning. If a bus has fewer than 2 adults, it does not run, and parents get a message by 6:45.
The jam moves to the drop points, next to someone's blockTwo drop points, not one. Count there too. Move a point if residents complain.
Parents ignore the Gate ZoneFriendly marshals, a reminder on Parents Gateway, and the counts shared openly.
Heat and rainCovered walkways where they exist, ponchos, water bottles, and the cancel rule.
Privacy worriesTags hold a code only. 30-day deletion. Parents can opt out of messages and still walk.
A child leaves the groupThe back adult keeps everyone in front. Headcount at every stop, by eye.

My rubric check

  • Clear grasp of the problem. Strong on the national picture, from real census data. Weak on our own gate: those counts are made up until a class does them.
  • Creative, fitting technology. WalkTap is small, cheap and answers the one thing parents asked for. Some will find it too plain. I think plain is the point.
  • Serves diverse mobility needs. Gate Zone passes, step-free routes, SMS and four languages. The weak spot: families who live too far to walk get little from it.
  • Realistic to launch. Yes for Term 1. It needs no new law and little money. It depends on volunteers, and that is where it could fail.
