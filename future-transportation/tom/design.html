Walk the Last Bit / Design

The design

Move the drop-off 400 metres back. Walk the children in together. Tell parents when they arrive. Keep the gate for the children who need it.

The three parts, with specs

1. Drop-and-Walk points

SpecDetail
WhereTwo places with a legal place to stop, about 400 m from the gate. One on each side, so no family drives through the Gate Zone.
WhenChildren wait with a volunteer from 6:55. Walking buses leave at 7:04 and 7:05.
Marked byA folding sign and two volunteers in vests. No new paint or kerbs at first.
Check firstAsk the Town Council and car park owner. A stop must not block another bus stop or a lift lobby.

2. Walking buses

A walking bus is a group of children who walk to school with adult volunteers. It has a fixed route, with stops and times. The rules below come from Devon County Council's walking bus guidance in the UK.

SpecDetail
AdultsAt least 2 per bus, one in front and one at the back. 1 adult for every 8 children.
SizeNo more than 16 children on one bus.
VestsHigh-visibility vests for every adult and child, kept in a box at each start point.
RoutesTwo routes, each about 400 m from its drop point. Step-free, with signalled crossings only. My Sec 3 class audits each route first.
VolunteersParents, grandparents and residents. The school checks every volunteer before they start.
WeatherLight rain: ponchos. Lightning warning or heavy rain at 6:45: the walk is cancelled and parents get a message.

3. WalkTap

The tech part is small on purpose. Working parents told us they drive because they want to know their child arrived. WalkTap answers that, and nothing more.

SpecDetail
TagsOne NFC sticker tag per child, on the bag tag. It holds a random code only.
ReadersSix school-owned Android phones with NFC: one per walking bus leader, two at the gate, two spare. Families buy nothing.
TapsTwo a day: joining the bus, and reaching the gate.
MessagesApp message, SMS for any phone, or none. The parent chooses.
LanguagesEnglish, Chinese, Malay and Tamil, translated by the school's staff and checked by parents.
Sign-up and consentThrough Parents Gateway, MOE's app that schools already use for notices and consent forms.
Data keptTap times for 30 days, then deleted. Parent phone numbers until the family leaves. No location, no photos.
If it failsThe leader has a paper list too. A missed tap means a missing message, never a missing child: the headcount is done by eye.

Why not GPS watches or live tracking?

Some parents asked for a live map. I said no, for three reasons.

  • Who it leaves out: a GPS watch costs money. A free bag tag works for every family.
  • Who it watches: a live map of where children walk is sensitive data about children. Two tap times answer the real question.
  • What it fixes: parents wanted to know their child arrived. They did not need to watch the walk.

The Gate Zone

From 7:00 to 7:30, no drop-offs in front of the gate. Cones and two volunteer marshals mark it. School buses and vans still stop. So do families with a Gate Zone pass, for children with disabilities or injuries.

This is a light version of London's School Streets, where roads outside schools close to traffic at drop-off and pick-up. The Mayor of London reported in 2021 that almost 350 had been delivered since April 2020. A study at 18 primary schools found nitrogen dioxide fell by up to 23% during morning drop-off. 81% of parents and carers supported them, and 18% said they drove to school less.

"Up to 23%" is a best case, not an average. And our Gate Zone does not close the road. A full closure would need LTA and the Traffic Police. LTA's Friendly Streets already include streets near schools, such as near Teck Ghee Primary in Ang Mo Kio. That is the route I would use in phase two.

Who it serves

WhoWhat changes for them
Children who walk from the flatsA clear gate, a clear bus stop, and fewer cars to squeeze between.
Children who are drivenDrop at a calm point, then 400 m on foot with friends.
Children with disabilitiesGate Zone pass for door drop-off. Step-free walking routes for those who want to walk with friends.
Working parentsA message when the child joins and arrives.
Families without smartphones or dataSMS, or the leader's paper list. Nobody has to buy anything.
Grandparents who walk children inThey can join the bus, or volunteer. The ratio rule means they never walk alone with a big group.
Public bus ridersThe bus can pull into its stop.

Try the WalkTap prototype

Two phones, wired together. On the leader's phone, tap children's tags, start walking, then reach the gate. Watch the parent's phone and the message log change. Then try "Not walking today" on the parent's phone, or switch to SMS in Settings.
