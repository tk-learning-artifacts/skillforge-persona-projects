Where the demo fits: a 90-minute club session

My club is made up, like me. The timings are planned for a real after-school club.
  • Who: 20 pupils from Primary 5 and 6, in 10 pairs.
  • Devices: 10 school laptops with any browser. One teacher laptop on the projector, with a webcam.
  • Accounts: none for pupils. Nobody signs in to anything.
  • Printouts: 10 test sheets. Each has 5 empty boxes for "what we drew, what it said, how sure".
  • Goal: pupils can say what training data, labels and features are. They can show one drawing that fools the model, and say why.
  1. 0 to 10 min. Settle and log in to the school laptops. Open the doodle trainer. If a laptop won't start, that pair joins the next one. The page works with 3 pupils too.
  2. 10 to 20 min. Unplugged warm-up. I hold up 6 drawings on paper and ask "sun, fish or house?" Then a sun with no rays. Some say moon. We talk: how did you decide?
  3. 20 to 35 min. Pairs press "Run the test" on version 1. Each pair finds the 2 wrong ones and writes why they think it went wrong. Then they tap each one to see its 5 closest drawings.
  4. 35 to 55 min. Pairs press "Start empty". Each partner adds 4 drawings of each thing, so 8 per class. Then pairs swap laptops. They draw 5 new things for the other pair's model and fill in the test sheet.
  5. 55 to 65 min. Break, away from screens.
  6. 65 to 80 min. I show Teachable Machine on the projector. I train 3 classes with things from my table: a ruler, an eraser, a water bottle. Only the objects go on camera, never faces. Pupils call out new things to try, like a pencil case. I close the tab at the end without saving.
  7. 80 to 87 min. Talk. Pupils match the four words to what they did: training data, label, features, overfitting. Then: "When was it sure and wrong?"
  8. 87 to 90 min. Pack up. Exit line on a sticky note: "My model got confused when…"

Check: 10 + 10 + 15 + 20 + 10 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 90 minutes.

What I watch for

  • The early finisher: ask them to fool their own model on purpose, then fix it with 2 drawings.
  • The quiet pupil: they can be the "tester" in the pair. Testing is the real job here.
  • Pupils who say "it knows": I ask "what does it see?" and point at the 20 × 20 squares.
