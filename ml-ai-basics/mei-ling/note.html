Teacher note: Sun, Fish or House?

A doodle classifier for a 90-minute club with Primary 5 and 6 pupils. By Mei Ling Tan.

What it is

A web page with a small model that sorts drawings into sun, fish or house. Pupils teach it with their own drawings. They test it on new ones and see how sure it is.

Scope: what it is for

  • Showing that a model learns only from its training data.
  • The words training data, label, feature and overfitting.
  • Testing on new data, and fixing data when it fails.
  • A backup when webcams or log-ins fail. It needs no camera and no account.

It is not a drawing judge. Never use it to mark pupils' work.

How it works

Each drawing is cut out, shrunk and turned into 20 × 20 squares. To guess, it finds the 5 taught drawings with the most similar squares. They vote. 4 votes of 5 shows as 80%.

What it gets wrong

  • Version 1 scored 13 of 15 on new drawings. It called a sun with no rays a fish, at 100%. It called a tall house a sun, also at 100%.
  • Version 2 adds 6 drawings and scores 15 of 15. It is only 60% sure on those two.
  • It knows 3 things only. A cat will still be called sun, fish or house.
  • It sees dark squares, not colour or meaning.
  • My drawings come from one hand. Pupils' styles will fool it more.

Safety and privacy

  • No pupil accounts, no names, no faces. Drawings stay in the browser tab.
  • "Download this model" saves numbers, not pictures. Pupils keep it on the school laptop only.
  • For Teachable Machine, I use my own laptop. Only objects go on camera. I close the tab without saving to Drive.
  • Teachable Machine's FAQ says it needs no account and trains in the browser tab. Samples go to no server unless you save to Drive.
  • Pupils draw things, not classmates.

If it goes wrong

"It's broken!" usually means it was taught too few or too similar drawings. Ask: what did you teach it? What does it see?
