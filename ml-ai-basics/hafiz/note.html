Teacher note: the SMS spam model demo

For a 60-minute Sec 3 lesson, or a 45-minute adult class. By Hafiz Ismail.

What it is

A web page with a naive Bayes spam model. It was trained on 4,459 real texts from the SMS Spam Collection. Students check made-up messages and see which words pushed the score. Then they audit it and add training examples.

Scope: what it is for

  • Training data, labels and features: here the features are words.
  • Testing on unseen data, and why a score needs a baseline.
  • How bias comes from who supplied the data.
  • Why a few new examples fix words, not ideas.

It is not a scam checker. Never tell students to trust its answer on a real message.

How it works

It counts how often each word appeared in spam and in normal texts. For a new message, it adds up each known word's push. Unknown words count for nothing. It does not read meaning or check links.

What it gets wrong

  • 99.2% right on 1,115 unseen texts from the same collection. But "always normal" already scores 86.5%.
  • It caught 5 of my 10 made-up 2025-style scams. Job, investment and chatty scams got through.
  • Singlish lowers the score: one bill scam fell from 99.6% to 5.6%. Most normal texts were Singaporean. Most spam came from a UK forum.
  • The collection dates from 2011. Spam has changed since.

Safety and privacy

  • No accounts. Nothing typed leaves the browser.
  • Students type made-up messages only. Never paste a real text: it may hold a name, number or link.
  • Never open the links in a real scam text. The links in my audit are made up.
  • I removed rude words and first names from the model file.
  • If a student is worried about a real message: ScamShield helpline, 1799.

If it goes wrong

A student's fix "works" on one message only? That is the lesson. Ask them to write the scam a new way and test again.

Sources: SMS Spam Collection, UCI Machine Learning Repository (doi:10.24432/C5CC84); ScamShield, scamshield.gov.sg. Checked 24 October 2026.
