·· Where the demo fits: a 60-minute Sec 3 lesson

My class is made up, like me. The timings are planned for a real 60-minute lesson.

  • Who: 36 Sec 3 students in 18 pairs. They know what a model and training data are from an earlier lesson.
  • Devices: one Chromebook per pair, with the demo page open. No accounts, no sign-in.
  • Printout: one audit sheet per pair, with 8 blank rows: message, really, score, right or wrong.
  • Goal: students can say why a 99% score can mislead. They can name one group a model could fail.
  1. 0 to 5 min. Hook. "A scam filter is 99% accurate. Would you trust it on your mum's phone?" Hands up, yes or no. Keep the count on the board.
  2. 5 to 15 min. Each pair writes 4 scam and 4 normal texts on the audit sheet. Nobody has seen the model yet. Rules: made up, no real names, numbers or links. This is their test set.
  3. 15 to 25 min. Pairs try the model with the sample buttons. I show the test table and ask: what would "always say normal" score? Someone works out 86.5%.
  4. 25 to 40 min. Pairs type in their own 8 texts and fill in the sheet. Then they press "Run the audit" and compare with mine. Each pair reports caught, missed and false alarms.
  5. 40 to 50 min. Pairs pick one scam it missed. They add 3 new made-up examples to fix it. Then they write the same scam a new way and test again. Did it learn the idea or the words?
  6. 50 to 57 min. Class talk. The Singlish table goes on the screen. Who supplied the data? Who would this model protect least? Then the real defences: the SMS Sender ID Registry, and ScamShield's 1799 helpline.
  7. 57 to 60 min. Exit ticket: "A 99% score is only as good as… because…". Second vote on the hook question.

Check: 5 + 10 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

·· For adult learners

I run the same activity in my evening class for working adults, in 45 minutes. I drop the paper sheet and use a shared spreadsheet. Adults go straight to "who built this data?", so the class talk takes longer.
