The people, their words and the numbers are made up, like me. The methods are what I would run in a real school.

Who we asked, and how

  • 24 P1 and P2 pupils who wait in the corner. P5 pupils asked in pairs, in the P1 and P2 classrooms, with the form teacher there. Questions were about yesterday: "Where did you wait? What did you do? What happened when your bus came?"
  • Parents of pupils who wait: a paper slip sent home. 38 of 60 came back.
  • The adults who run dismissal: the bus attendant, the security officer, a P1 teacher and a cleaner. I asked, and two P5 pupils took notes.
  • Pupils across the school: a dot vote at recess on the three card models. Pupils who wait in the corner got yellow dots. Everyone else got red.

No names went on any sheet. Pupils could say no, and one did.

The dot vote

Three models sat on a table outside the canteen for one recess. Each pupil who stopped placed one dot.

  • Model A, shade first: a sail over the front, with benches under it.
  • Model B, bus bays: benches under the roof, bus bays and a bus board.
  • Model C, reading nook: cushions, a bookshelf and a quiet corner.

The reading nook won the most dots. But most of them came from pupils who never wait there. The pupils who do wait wanted shade and their bus. My class argued about this for 20 minutes. They decided the yellow dots count more, because those pupils use the corner every day. I didn't tell them that. The two colours of dots did.

How we reported back

The P5s drew a "you said, we changed" poster by hand. They pinned it in the corner. Two of them gave a two-minute talk at assembly. We also visited the P1 and P2 classes with the card model. The children who spoke could see their idea in it.

Who might lose out

Four sail posts take up floor. Children used to play catching games there while they waited. Cleaners have more to sweep around. The P5 on bus board duty misses 15 minutes of their own free time. We made it a rota across the class. Each pupil does it about once every six weeks.

What I would change

We didn't ask any pupil who uses a wheelchair or walking frame. Our school has none in P1 or P2 this year. So we tested with a card token instead. A token can't tell you the bench is too low to get out of. Next time I'd invite someone from a local disability group to look at the model.
