Checks

    The things you can place

    • Bench: 2 m long, 4 seats. It counts as shaded only if the whole bench is in shade.
    • Shade sail: 3 m by 2 m, with a post at each corner. Pupils can stand and sit under it, but not walk through the posts.
    • Planter: 1 m square. Green, but it takes up floor.
    • Bag rack: 1.5 m long. It gets bags off the floor.
    • Bus board: hangs on the back wall, so it does not block the floor.
    • Bus bay: a painted box on the floor where one bus group waits. You can walk over it.

    How the checks work

    • The path: the tool slides a 1.5 m square from the gate to the door. BCA's 2025 accessibility code asks for 1.5 m on the main routes of school buildings. We used the same width.
    • Turning: it looks for an empty space 2 m across. The code says a wheelchair needs 1.8 m of clear floor to turn freely. Our squares are 0.5 m, so 2 m is the nearest fit.
    • Shade: the roof shades the back 2 m at 1.40 pm. A sail shades the floor under it. Real shade moves with the sun. This tool only shows 1.40 pm.
    • Standing room: about 0.5 m² of shaded floor for each pupil who stands. That is our class's rule of thumb, not an official number.

    What the groups found

    • Group B's bag rack blocked the way to the door. In the card model, the wheelchair token got stuck right there. They had not seen it on paper.
    • Group A got close on shade, but one bench stayed in the sun. Nobody would sit on it at 1.40 pm. The floor count told us so.
    • The final layout takes the sail from Group A and the bus board and bays from Group B. The benches along the back wall leave the path in front of them clear.
