The scale

At 1:25, 1 m in the corner is 4 cm on the model. The corner is 8 m by 6 m, so the base is 32 cm by 24 cm. That fits on one A3 sheet of card with a margin.

I picked 1:25 because 4 cm per metre is easy for P5 maths. 1:20 is tidier, but the base would be 30 cm deep, wider than A3.

Parts list, per group

  • 1 sheet of A3 card for the base. Draw the walls, gate, door, pillars and a 2 cm grid in pencil.
  • 1 strip of blue paper, 32 cm by 8 cm, for the roof shade (2 m deep).
  • 2 sheets of A4 card for benches, bag rack, bus board and test tokens.
  • 1 sheet of tissue paper for the sail, and 4 paper straws for its posts.
  • Scissors, glue sticks, a ruler, a pencil, masking tape.
  • The plan printed from the layout tool, if you want a start.

Safety: adults handle craft knives and hot glue guns. Pupils use scissors and glue sticks only. Straws are used instead of skewers, so there are no sharp points.

Cutting plan

Solid lines are cuts. Dashed blue lines are folds. All sizes are on the model, in cm. On a phone, swipe the plan sideways.

Build it

  1. Draw the base: an outline 32 cm by 24 cm. Mark the gate on the left side, 12 cm to 20 cm from the back. Mark the door on the right side, 2 cm to 8 cm from the back.
  2. Glue the blue strip along the back. That is the roof shade at 1.40 pm. Glue two 1 cm card squares at its front edge for the pillars. Put them 10 cm and 20 cm from the left.
  3. Fold each bench net into a low table shape. The top is 2 cm wide (0.5 m) and the legs are 1.8 cm tall (0.45 m).
  4. Tape a straw at each corner of the tissue sail. Cut the straws to 10 cm, so the sail is 2.5 m up on the model.
  5. Place your layout. Don't glue anything down yet. You will move things after the tests.

Test it before you glue

  1. Path test: slide the path gauge from the gate to the door. If it hits anything, the path is under 1.5 m wide.
  2. Turning test: find a spot where the turning disc lies flat without touching anything.
  3. Reach test: move the wheelchair token to every bench. Can it park beside each one?
  4. Shade test: count the benches fully on blue paper or under the sail.
  5. Write each result on a sticky note on the base. Change one thing, then test again.

The tokens follow BCA's 2025 accessibility code. A wheelchair going straight needs 0.9 m by 1.2 m. Turning freely needs 1.8 m. The main routes in school buildings need 1.5 m.

The micro:bit logger

This runs on a micro:bit V2 only. The V1 has no microphone and cannot log data. Add the Data Logger extension in MakeCode first: click Extensions and search for "datalogger".

// Button A: change the spot number, 1 to 6.
// Button B: start or stop logging.
// Every 10 seconds, while logging, save spot, sound and temperature.
let spot = 1
let logging = false
datalogger.setColumnTitles("spot", "sound", "temp")
basic.showNumber(spot)

input.onButtonPressed(Button.A, function () {
    spot += 1
    if (spot > 6) {
        spot = 1
    }
    basic.showNumber(spot)
})

input.onButtonPressed(Button.B, function () {
    logging = !logging
    if (logging) {
        basic.showIcon(IconNames.Yes)
    } else {
        basic.showIcon(IconNames.No)
    }
})

loops.everyInterval(10000, function () {
    if (logging) {
        datalogger.log(
            datalogger.createCV("spot", spot),
            datalogger.createCV("sound", input.soundLevel()),
            datalogger.createCV("temp", input.temperature())
        )
    }
})

datalogger.onLogFull(function () {
    logging = false
    basic.showIcon(IconNames.Sad)
})

Getting the data out

  1. Plug the micro:bit into a laptop. It shows up as a drive called MICROBIT.
  2. Double-click the MY_DATA file. It opens in the browser as a table.
  3. Download it as a CSV file, or copy it into a spreadsheet.
  4. Sort by spot. Average the sound and temperature for each spot and each time.

Downloading a new program hides the old data. Copy MY_DATA off before you change the code. The log also keeps a time column, which starts from 0 each time the micro:bit restarts.

The card shade box

Fold a strip of white card into a box with a roof and two open sides. The micro:bit sits inside, so the sun never shines on the board but air still flows through. Every group used the same box, so the readings are fair to compare.

How the six sessions ran

Each session was 60 minutes in the makerspace, for 32 P5 pupils in 8 groups.

  1. Session 1. 10 min: what is wrong with the waiting corner? 20 min: code the logger and test it in the room. 20 min: build the card shade boxes. 10 min: plan who logs where.
  2. Session 2. 15 min: log at recess, on a rota. 30 min: tally and dot map at the corner, with adults outside. 15 min: put the data in one class sheet.
  3. Session 3. 20 min: chart the averages and write one finding with numbers. 40 min: interview P1 and P2 pupils, with their teachers there. See the feedback report.
  4. Session 4. 10 min: the scale and the tokens. 45 min: build card models. 5 min: pack up.
  5. Session 5. 25 min: test the models with the tokens and change them. 35 min: the dot vote at recess, with all three models on a table.
  6. Session 6. 20 min: trial week results. 30 min: write the pitch to the principal, with costs. 10 min: each group says one thing it would change.

The trial week ran between sessions 5 and 6, at dismissal. The P5 pupils and I were on duty.
