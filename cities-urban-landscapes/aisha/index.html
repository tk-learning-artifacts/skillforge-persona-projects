The Waiting Corner

After school, up to 46 pupils wait in one corner by the side gate. They wait for a bus or a parent. There are 8 seats, all in the sun. So 17 children sit on the floor in the shade. My P5 makers measured it and built it in card. They tested a change for a week and pitched the fix.

I'm Aisha. I teach design and making in our school makerspace. I wanted a space the pupils walk past every day, so they could touch, measure and change it. The waiting corner was perfect. Everyone in the class had waited there.

How the class ran it

  • Who: 32 P5 pupils, in 8 groups of 4.
  • When: 6 makerspace sessions of 60 minutes, in Term 3, plus one trial week.
  • Kit: 8 micro:bit V2s with battery packs, tally sheets, a 10 m tape, card, scissors and glue sticks.
  • Rules: we stayed inside the school compound, away from the driveway and car park. No photos of faces. An adult was with every group outside.

"Went to the gate to look" counts pupils who walked past the bend to check for their bus. We counted for 20 minutes. That is a safety problem. The gate opens onto the driveway.

The micro:bit survey

Each group took a micro:bit to one spot. It logged sound and temperature every 10 seconds for 10 minutes. We did it three times: at recess, in a lesson and at dismissal. Each micro:bit sat in a small card box with open sides. That kept the sun off the board without blocking the air.

Two things my pupils had to learn first

  • Sound is a number from 0 to 255, not decibels. It lets you compare spots measured with the same micro:bit setup. It does not tell you how loud a place is in real units.
  • The temperature sensor is inside the micro:bit's processor. It gives a rough idea of the air temperature. So we compared spots with each other, not with the weather report.

The code is on the card model and code page.

What the site analysis says

  • At dismissal, the front of the corner is the hottest spot we logged. It read 35.5 against 32.0 under the roof, in the same 10 minutes.
  • It is also the loudest spot at dismissal: 131 out of 255. My pupils said it is loud because children shout across to friends at the gate.
  • Children choose shade over seats. 17 sat on the floor under the roof while the benches stayed in the sun.
  • Children can't see their bus from the corner, so 12 walked out to the gate to look.

We scored the corner with the Place Diagram. Comfort was the worst of the four. It is too hot, with too few seats, so you end up on the floor.

2. A one-week trial

Before asking for money, we tested a cheap version. For one week we put out 12 stools from the makerspace under the roof. We chalked two "bus bays" on the floor. A P5 pupil on duty held up a card bus board when each bus came in. The security officer told them by walkie-talkie.

The stools emptied the floor. The bus board kept most children inside. The chalk bays faded after two days of rain, which told us paint was worth paying for.

3. The redesign

Each group built a card model at 1:25 and tested it with a wheelchair token. You can try our layouts and your own in the layout tool. The cutting plans are on the model page.

The final layout

  • A shade sail, 3 m by 2 m, over the front of the corner, where the benches are.
  • Four benches: two under the sail, two under the roof. That is 16 seats, all in shade at 1.40 pm.
  • A bus board on the back wall, made in the makerspace. Each bus has a slot. A pupil on duty slides in a card when the bus arrives.
  • Two painted bus bays on the floor, so each bus group waits in one place.
  • A low bag rack, so bags come off the floor.
  • A bright arrow sign at the bend by the gate, so new P1s find the corner.
  • A small book box on the back wall, for the pupils who voted for a reading nook.
  • A clear path at least 1.5 m wide from the gate to the door. One space 2 m across, where a wheelchair can turn.

What I would change

We only measured on dry days in Term 3. A rainy afternoon pushes everyone under the roof, and our plan may not cope. I'd also like a temperature reading we can trust more. The micro:bit gives a rough reading. So the 3.5 degree gap between spots is useful, but the exact numbers are not. A cheap thermometer next to each micro:bit would help pupils check it.
