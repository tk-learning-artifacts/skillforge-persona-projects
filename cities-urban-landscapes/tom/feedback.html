Gate to Stop · community feedback report

Who uses the walk, and what they told us

We asked two groups who share one footpath at different hours. Then we went back with three options and let them choose.

The people, their words and their numbers are made up, like me. The methods are the ones I would use with a real class.

Who we asked, and how

Round 1: what happened last time

  • Students: a short anonymous form for 4 Sec 3 classes. 131 of 148 replied.
  • Residents: 11 short interviews at the Block 2 void deck and coffee shop. We went on a Saturday, from 9 to 11 am. Students worked in pairs, with me beside them. We took no names, only the age group each person gave us. Anyone could say no, and 6 people did.
  • Others: the coffee shop owner, our school's operations manager, and a town council officer by email.

Every question asked about the last time, not about wishes. "Think of the last time you walked to the bus stop after school. What happened?" works. "Would you like a shelter?" gets a polite yes from everyone.

Round 2: pick one option

  • Residents: a poster on the Block 2 noticeboard for one week, with the town council's permission. Each person placed one sticker dot. 46 dots.
  • Students: the same three options on a second form. 131 replies.

The gap between the two groups is the finding. Only 9% of students wanted a seat. 73% of residents did. If we had only asked students, the benches would never have made the plan.

Round 2: the three options

Each person picked one. Here is how the two groups split.

OptionResidents (46 dots)Students (131)
A. Move the bicycle racks and add a rest stop with 2 benches21 (46%)22 (17%)
B. Two exit times at dismissal, 10 minutes apart15 (33%)28 (21%)
C. A covered walkway along the whole route10 (22%)81 (62%)

Students wanted the roof. Residents wanted the bench and the space. Neither group was wrong. The walkway takes years and needs approval from outside the school. So the plan does all three, in order of what we can do soonest.

What we heard but didn't change

  • 12 students asked for a drinks machine at the bus stop. The stop is not ours to change, and the coffee shop sells drinks.
  • One resident asked for a traffic light crossing near Block 3. We passed it to the town council. Crossings are for LTA to decide, and we had no data on the road.

How we reported back

We put a one-page "you said, we changed" poster on the same noticeboard for two weeks. It had the vote results and the three phases. Students took the same page home in the school newsletter.

Who might lose out

Students who live far away and catch a later bus lose 10 minutes on exit days. Their parents may not like it. The coffee shop gets its rush in two smaller waves, which the owner thought was fine. Residents who work weekdays never met us. We interviewed on a Saturday morning, so we mostly heard from retirees.
