The dataset

Resident Students Aged 5 Years and Over by Planning Area of Residence and Usual Mode of Transport to School, Census of Population 2020. It comes from the Singapore Department of Statistics. I downloaded it from data.gov.sg. The original is SingStat table 17589. Every number on this page is from it, unchanged.

    What my class found

    • In Tampines, 22.2% of students need no transport. Most of them, I'd guess, walk. That is close to Singapore's 23.6%.
    • More than half of Tampines students go by public transport. That is 26.4% bus only, 6.7% MRT or LRT only and 22.2% both.
    • Tampines students rarely go by car: 10.8% car only. In Bukit Timah it is 34.1% car only. Only Tanglin is higher, at 36.4%. The chart above adds taxis too, so its numbers are a little higher.
    • Punggol tops the "no transport" list at 34.7%, then Sengkang at 31.7%.

    Most students in our town need no transport. Most of the rest take a bus for part of the trip. A bad walk to the bus stop hits the most common trips.

    The Bukit Timah numbers started the best argument of the unit. One student guessed it was about who owns a car. Another said some homes are far from any school, so walking is not an option. The data can't settle it. That was the point.

    What this data can't tell you

    • It is sorted by where students live, not where they study. A Tampines student may go to school in Bedok.
    • It counts all resident students aged 5 and over. That includes polytechnic and university students, not only school pupils.
    • It is from 2020. Punggol and Tengah have grown since. The census runs once every 10 years.
    • It says nothing about our footpath, heat, or older residents. That is why we counted and asked.
    • Some rows add up to 1 to 3 more or less than their total. I think this is rounding, but the table doesn't say.
