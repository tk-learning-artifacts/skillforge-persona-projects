Gate to Stop

A 180 m footpath runs from our side gate to the bus stop. At 2 pm it belongs to 300 students. At 9.30 am it belongs to older residents going to the market. My Sec 3 class measured it and asked both groups. Then they wrote a plan that works for both.

I'm Tom. I teach Geography and run project work on sustainability and fairness. I picked this space because it has two sets of users who never meet. The students rush through at 2 pm. The residents plan their day around avoiding the rush. A redesign that only asks students would miss half the story.

How the class ran it

  • Who: 38 Sec 3 Geography students, in groups of 4 or 5.
  • When: 6 lessons of 60 minutes, over 3 weeks of Term 3.
  • Fieldwork: counts on 2 weekdays, inside the school's usual fieldwork rules. Groups stayed on the footpath, never on the road, with a teacher in sight.
  • Kit: tally sheets, a 30 m measuring tape, a phone stopwatch, clipboards. No photos of faces.

Other measurements

  • Length, gate to bus stop: 180 m, measured with the tape in 30 m lengths.
  • Footpath width: 1.8 m. At the bicycle racks and lamp post it drops to 1.1 m clear.
  • Shade at 1.30 pm: 42 m of the 180 m, all from four trees. That leaves 138 m in the sun.
  • Seats along the walk: none. The nearest bench is in the void deck of Block 2, off the route.
  • Bus stop shelter: 9 m long. We counted about 20 people standing under it before the rest spill out.
  • Most people waiting at the stop at once: 64 at 1.52 pm, 14 at 7.10 am.

Reading the space with the Place Diagram

We scored the walk on the four qualities from Project for Public Spaces' Place Diagram. Each group gave 1 to 5, and we took the middle score.

  • Access and linkages: 3. Short and direct, but the pinch point blocks wheelchairs at 2 pm.
  • Comfort and image: 1. Hot, no seats, and the stop overflows.
  • Uses and activities: 2. People only pass through. The coffee shop is the one reason to stop.
  • Sociability: 2. Students greet each other. Residents told us they avoid the students.

What the site analysis says

The path is narrow, but the bigger problem is timing. From 1.40 to 2.00 pm, 41 people stepped off the path at the pinch point. At 9.30 am, 18 older residents and 2 people with walking aids used the same spot. Nobody stepped off then, but nobody sat down either. There is nowhere to.

So my design question became this. How can the same 180 m work for the 2 pm crowd and the 9.30 am walkers?

What I would change

We counted on 2 dry weekdays only. A rainy day would change everything, because students bunch under the trees. We also never counted on a Saturday, when the market is busiest. And "older" was a guess by eye. Next time I'd ask residents to tell us their age group in the interview instead.
