Lesson plan: Stop, Check, Tell

60 minutes, a double period. Mostly unplugged, with one screen station.

  • Class: 30 P5 pupils, aged 10 to 11, in their usual classroom.
  • Needs in this class: 4 pupils read well below their age, 2 have dyslexia, 1 autistic pupil finds sudden changes hard, and 3 are learning English.
  • Devices: 4 tablets or Chromebooks at one station, with the message sorter open. No pupil logs in to anything.
  • Printouts: the three worksheets (12 of each), 8 sets of clue cards, 15 role-play scam cards, a "No" card for each table, and the rules poster in A3.
  • On the board: a picture timetable of the eight parts of the lesson. I tick each part as we finish it.

By the end, pupils can

  • Name 3 clues that a message may be a scam.
  • Say the three steps: Stop, Check, Tell.
  • Say no to a request for a password or a code, out loud.
  • Say why using someone else's account is wrong, even if they gave you the password.

Success criteria, in pupil words

  1. I can point to 3 clues in a scam message.
  2. I can tell you what I do: Stop, Check, Tell.
  3. I said "No, I will ask an adult" in the role-play.

Before the lesson

  • Read the voice note message (number 5) and think about your class. If a pupil has a grandparent who is ill or lives far away, swap it for another message. Tell the class before you show it, either way.
  • Give the autistic pupil the picture timetable the day before. Tell them about the role-play and that it is pretend.
  • Set the screen station to "fewer words" on one device. Leave "read it to me" ready on another.

Timings

  1. 0 to 5 min. Settle and preview. I go through the picture timetable. I say: "Today you are detectives. Some messages are tricks. You will learn to spot them."
  2. 5 to 12 min. Hook. I read the GemQuest message aloud, twice, and show it on the board. Thumbs up for safe, down for scam, sideways for not sure. I ask three pupils why. I write their clues on the board in their words.
  3. 12 to 17 min. Rules and the law. I read the short talk from the rules page. We read the poster together. Then the Jun Wei question: "If he tells you his password, may you use it?"
  4. 17 to 25 min. Teach the clues and the steps. I hand out clue cards to each table. I show the Jun Wei message. Tables hold up the clue cards they see. Then I teach Stop, Check, Tell with the poster, and act it out once.
  5. 25 to 45 min. Practise. Pupils choose a worksheet: circle, square or triangle. They work in pairs. Every 5 minutes, 4 pairs move to the screen station, one pair per device. Four turns of 5 minutes fit all 15 pairs. I sit with the circle pairs first. My co-teacher or the allied educator, if there is one, sits at the station.
  6. 45 to 53 min. Role-play: saying no. Pairs take a scam card. One reads it. The other answers, using the script on the card if they need it: "No. I will check with an adult first." Then they swap. Two pairs show the class.
  7. 53 to 58 min. Show what you learnt. Exit task, in any of three ways: tell me, draw it, or write it. "A message asks for the code on your phone. What do you do?"
  8. 58 to 60 min. Pack up. I collect the role-play cards so no fake messages leave the room. Tick the last box on the timetable.

Check: 5 + 7 + 5 + 8 + 20 + 8 + 5 + 2 = 60 minutes. A pair at the station leaves its worksheet and picks it up again after.

Role-play cards

Print one card per pair. Each card has the message on one side and the "no" script on the back. The scripts:

  • "Send me the code on your phone." Answer: "No. Codes are secret. I will ask my mum or dad."
  • "Tell me your game password, I'll get you free coins." Answer: "No. My password is only mine."
  • "It's me, your cousin. Don't tell anyone, just send money." Answer: "No. I am telling an adult."
  • "Click this link fast or you lose your prize." Answer: "No. I will stop and check first."

For different pupils

  • Reads well below age: the circle sheet has 3 messages, big print and clue pictures to tick. I read the messages aloud to the group.
  • Learning English: clue cards have a picture and one word. They can answer in the role-play with the card's script.
  • Finds noise hard: the role-play happens at their own table, not in front of the class, unless they choose to show.
  • Finished early: the triangle sheet asks them to write their own made-up scam message, on paper, for a partner to spot. Then they write the reply that says no.
  • Would rather not speak: they can hold up the "No" card from their table instead of saying the script.

How I check learning

The exit task tells me criterion 2 for every pupil, in the way they chose. I note criterion 3 during the role-play. The worksheets show criterion 1. I mark each as "yes", "nearly" or "not yet" on a class list. Anyone with "not yet" on criterion 2 gets five minutes with me next lesson.
