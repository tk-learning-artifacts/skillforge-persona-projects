Our online safety rules

Six rules for P5, with a picture for each. Pin it up in A3. We read it together at the start of the lesson.

  1. My passwords and codes are mine.I never share them, not even with my best friend.
  2. I only use my own accounts.Using someone else's account without their say-so is against the law in Singapore. Even if they told me the password.
  3. I stop before I tap.No tapping links, replying or sending until I have checked.
  4. Not sure? I ask a safe adult.Asking is always allowed. It is never silly.
  5. I tell.If something online feels wrong, I tell a safe adult. Telling is not getting anyone into trouble.
  6. Practice scams stay in class.The fake messages we write are for learning. We never send them to anyone.

The short talk on the law

I read this in about 3 minutes, slowly, with the poster on the board. I point to the picture for each rule as I say it.

"Before we look at tricks, we need to talk about what is fair and what is the law."

"Your account is like your locker. Only you should open it. If someone opens your locker without asking, how do you feel?" (Take two answers.)

"Singapore has a law about this. It is called the Computer Misuse Act. It says you must not get into someone else's computer or account without their permission. People who break it can be fined or even go to jail."

"Here is the tricky part. What if Jun Wei tells you his password? May you use his account?" (Take answers. Most will say yes.)

"Knowing a password is not the same as being allowed. Jun Wei might have told you by mistake. If he did not say 'yes, please use my account now', it is not yours to open. And the kind thing is to tell him to change it."

"Last rule. Today we will write pretend scam messages. They are only for this room. Sending a trick message to a real person is not a joke. It can hurt and scare people."

If a pupil uses or shares a password

At this age, it is usually a friend's game account, shared on a dare or out of kindness. My steps:

  1. Talk in private, calmly. Ask what happened. Most pupils don't know it was wrong.
  2. Point to rules 1 and 2 on the poster. Use the locker idea again.
  3. Make sure the owner changes the password, with a parent's help.
  4. If a school account was used, tell the school's ICT staff and follow the school's process. Let the parents know.

Why I kept the law talk so simple

The Act has many sections and penalties. P5 pupils don't need the numbers. They need one idea: permission matters more than knowing the password. I left out the amounts. Big numbers frightened one of my pupils last year and taught nothing new.
