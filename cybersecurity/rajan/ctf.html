Six challenges. Each one hides a flag that looks like sf{something}. Find it, type it in, score the points.

You may attack these six challenges and this site's own files, including its code. I own the site and I give you permission. That permission stops at this site. Read the ground rules first. Challenge 1 makes sure you do.

1. Read the rules 50 pts

Every real CTF starts with rules. The people who run it say what you may attack and what you may not. Read ours to the end.

Open the ground rules

The flag is in the last box on the rules page.

2. Where does this link go? 100 pts

A message arrives in your class chat. Merlion Bank is made up. Pretend its real site is merlionbank.com.sg. The links below are plain text, so you can't click them. Good. Read them instead.

Unknown number, 9:41 pm

Your Merlion Bank account is locked. Log in within 1 hour to unlock it. Use any of these mirror links:

  1. https://merlionbank.com.sg.acct-unlock-sg.net/login
  2. https://www.merlionbank-sg.com/login
  3. https://merlionbank.com.sg@203.0.113.7/login
  4. https://www.rnerlionbank.com.sg/login
  5. https://login.merlionbank.com.sg/reset

Only one link goes to a server Merlion Bank controls. The flag is that link's hostname, wrapped as sf{hostname}.

The message is still a scam, by the way. A real bank would not send "mirror links". The point is that you can read where a link goes before you tap it.

Find the hostname: it sits after // and before the next /. Anything before an @ is not the host. Then read the hostname from the right. The owner is the last two or three parts, like example.com.sg. Look closely at every letter.

3. Caesar's homework 100 pts

I found this note on a desk. Every letter has been moved along the alphabet by the same amount. I don't know the amount.

TLLA TL HMALY JJH. AOL MSHN PZ ZM{AYF_HSS_25_RLFZ}

Use CyberChef: drag in ROT13, then change Amount. Or use the shift tool here.

There are only 25 shifts to try. Trying every key is called a brute-force attack. Real ciphers have so many keys that this would take longer than anyone can wait.

A short word repeats at the start: TL. Two-letter words in English are few. Watch for "ME" or "IS" as you slide.

4. Encoded is not encrypted 150 pts

A student "hid" the flag so nobody could read it. Here it is:

c2Z7ZW5jb2RlZF9pc19ub3RfZW5jcnlwdGVkfQ==

There is no key. Name the format, decode it, and you have the flag. CyberChef will do it in one step if you pick the right operation.

Only letters, digits, and == at the end. That padding is a giveaway for Base64. In CyberChef, use "From Base64".

5. The club page 150 pts

The made-up Coding Club put up a page. The leader says the flag is "hidden where students can't see it".

Open the club page

Look at what the browser received, not only what it shows.

On a laptop, press Ctrl + U (Cmd + Option + U on a Mac) to view the page source. Search it with Ctrl + F for sf{. If view source is blocked on your school device, put your hand up. I have a printout.

6. Build a passphrase the checker accepts 200 pts

Now defend. Two of its checks come from NIST, the US standards body. The others are mine. Make up a passphrase that passes every check, and the checker gives you the flag.

Make one up for this lesson. Never type a password you really use, here or on any checker.

    The checker runs in your browser. Nothing you type leaves this page or is saved.

    Pick three or four words that have nothing to do with each other, like things on your desk. Put a dash between them. Don't use your name, your pair name or the word "password".
