Build guidePrint it, fill it, hang it

Everything you need to make one case. It runs from the tolerance test to the pin on the wall. The steps marked "pupils" suit a P6 group of 4.

Parts for one case

  • The printed body and lid, in PLA. About 38 g of plastic.
  • One micro:bit V2. The sound level block needs a V2.
  • One 2 × AAA battery holder with a switch and a JST-PH plug. Mine is 63 × 26 × 16 mm. Measure yours: the bay is 64 × 26.8 mm and 16 mm deep.
  • Two AAA batteries.
  • One drawing pin or small hook, with a head under 8 mm across.
  • For the teacher: digital callipers and a plastic scraper.

Safety first

  • The nozzle runs at about 200°C for PLA. The bed is hot too. Pupils never touch the nozzle, bed or moving parts while the printer is on.
  • The printer sits away from desks, in a room with good air flow. Washington State's health department gives one more rule. Keep the room or printer cover shut for 20 minutes after a print.
  • A teacher is in the room whenever it prints. No prints left running overnight.
  • Let the bed cool before you lift a part. Use a scraper, never fingers or a craft knife.
  • Batteries go in the right way round. Don't mix old and new ones.

Steps

Before the class (teacher)

  1. Print the tolerance test from SkillForge's fit article. Find the snug hole. Write the number on a sticky label on the printer.
  2. Use the lid maker to make a lid with your gap. Or use my lid if your snug hole was between 10.0 and 10.2 mm.
  3. Open both STLs in Cura. Load the profile settings above.
  4. Check the preview: a whole first layer, no supports, and a time you can supervise.
  5. Print one case first. Test the lid before you print nine.

Check the print (pupils)

  1. Run a finger round the lip. Feel for blobs or strings. Tell the teacher if you find one.
  2. Look at the four posts on the lid. Each should be straight and whole.
  3. Push the lid on with no parts inside. It should press in and stay.

Fill it (pupils)

  1. Put the batteries in the holder. Switch it off.
  2. Lay the holder in the bottom bay, switch facing up. The lead goes towards the notch in the wall.
  3. Feed the plug through the notch, into the hollow under the board.
  4. Plug it into the micro:bit's battery socket.
  5. Lower the micro:bit into its pocket, LEDs facing up. It should drop in by itself. Don't force it.
  6. Press the lid on, starting at the battery end.
  7. Switch on. The board should light up through the window.

Code it (pupils)

Make this in MakeCode for micro:bit. In blocks, it is one "forever" with a "plot bar graph" inside. The sound level goes from 0 (quiet) to 255 (loud).

basic.forever(function () {
    led.plotBarGraph(input.soundLevel(), 255)
})

Download it to the board with the USB cable. Lift the board out to plug in. Then put it back and close the lid.

Hang it (teacher)

  1. Push a pin into the pinboard at pupils' eye level. Leave 3 mm sticking out.
  2. Put the round part of the keyhole over the pin head. Slide the case down.
  3. Tug it gently. The pin should sit at the top of the slot.

When it goes wrong

What you seeLikely causeFix
The lid won't go onYour printer runs tightMake a lid with a gap 0.05 mm bigger.
The lid falls offYour printer runs looseMake a lid with a gap 0.05 mm smaller.
The board stands up at one endThe lead is under the board's edgeRoute the lead through the notch and the hollow.
Body corners curl upWeak bed gripClean the bed. Switch the skirt to a brim.
Thin strings across the windowsPlastic oozing on travel movesLower the temperature by 5°C. Pull the strings off once cool.
The bar graph never movesA V1 boardCheck the back of the board. Sound level needs a V2.

Taking it further with pupils

Pupils under 13 should not make their own Tinkercad accounts. A teacher can set up a Tinkercad class with seats instead. SkillForge's printing article explains how. Then each group can remix the lid. They could add their group name, or a raised ring round the window. The lip and posts must stay the same, or it won't fit.
