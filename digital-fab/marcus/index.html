A 3D-printed bracket for the HC-SR04 distance sensor on our practice robots. In our crash tests, version 3 took 50 hits and did not crack.

My team, our robots and our test log are made up, like me. Every print time and test result on this page comes from that log. The bracket sizes are the real sizes in the STL files.

I'm Marcus. I coach a Sec 3 robotics team. Our robots follow walls using an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor. We used to cable-tie the sensor to the chassis. After one hard crash it pointed at the floor, and the robot drove in circles. So my "mount for a classroom device" had to survive crashes. It also had to print fast and go back on in a minute.

Short rationale

  • Crashes happen every practice. The bracket has to take them, so strength drove the design.
  • Speed matters too. A broken part must be reprinted before the next session. So it stays small: about 26 minutes and 6 g.
  • Two M3 screws in slots, not cable ties. The slots slide 5 mm, so one bracket fits all six of our chassis.
  • The sensor's two cans push through the front plate. The plate takes the hit, not the sensor.
  • One cable tie through a slot between the cans holds the board in.
  • Gussets at both ends stop the plate folding back.

Fit

The Pi Hut lists this sensor at 43 × 20 × 15 mm. Boards from different makers vary. So we measured our own boards with callipers.

Version 1 had 16.0 mm holes, the same size as the cans. They jammed. One student pushed hard and bent a can's legs. That sensor never read right again. Never draw a hole the same size as the part.

Printability

  • V3 prints on its side, so it stands 50 mm tall on a small footprint. A 5 mm brim stops it tipping.
  • On its side, the can holes are horizontal. A round hole's top would sag. So the holes are teardrops, pointed on top. Every edge is 45° or steeper.
  • Cura's base default support angle is 50°. Our 45° tops are under that, so no supports.
  • The gussets at each end are flat on the bed or at the top. No overhangs.
  • The point of each teardrop sits beside the can, not under it. The can still touches the hole all round its lower half.
  • Safety at the printer: the nozzle runs at about 200°C and the bed is hot. Lift the part only when the bed is cool. Trim the brim with flush cutters and safety glasses. The pit sheet has the full list.

Strength vs time

This is where I was wrong. I think speed wins, so I start with 0.28 mm layers. That part was right. But I ignored which way the part lay on the bed. That turned out to matter most, and it cost almost no time.

Side view of the bracket. Layer lines are drawn wider apart than real ones.

A printed part is weakest across its layers. Printed upright, the layers stack across the bend. The crash pulls them apart there. Printed on its side, each layer is a whole L. The walls run round the bend without a break.

What we kept

More walls beat more infill, as Prusa's guide says. Gussets helped. Turning the part on its side cost 2 minutes and did the most. Fine 0.12 mm layers cost 35 more minutes and changed nothing we could measure.

Three versions

V1: the jam

  • 2 mm thick, printed upright. 16.0 mm holes.
  • The cans jammed. One sensor was damaged getting it in.
  • Change: holes to 16.6 mm.

V2: the snap

  • Same shape, 16.6 mm holes, and a cable-tie slot. Fit was good.
  • It snapped clean along a layer line at the bend, after 6 crashes.
  • We tried more walls and more infill first. Neither got past 11 crashes.
  • Change: thicker, gussets, and a new way up on the bed.

V3: the keeper

  • 3 mm thick, with a 12 mm gusset at each end. Each gusset is 2 mm thick, so the board fits between them.
  • Printed on its side, with teardrop holes and a brim.
  • 50 crashes, no crack. Our robots now wear these at every practice.

STL and slicer profile

What I would change

The bracket is strong, but the robot is now the weak point. In crash 38, the chassis plate under the screws bent. A part that never breaks just moves the damage somewhere else. Next time I would add a thin "fuse" that snaps first. It would be a cheap part that prints in 5 minutes. Also, the 26 mm can spacing is our boards' number. A team with different boards must measure and edit the file. I have not made that easy yet.
