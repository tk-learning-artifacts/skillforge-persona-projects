Pit sheetPrint, fit, test

The one page my team keeps by the printer. A pair can go from STL to a mounted sensor in under 40 minutes.

Kit for one robot

  • One printed V3 bracket, in PLA. About 6 g.
  • One HC-SR04 sensor.
  • Two M3 screws, 10 mm long, with two M3 nuts. Check the length against your chassis plate.
  • One small cable tie, under 4 mm wide.
  • Tools: a screwdriver, flush cutters, a plastic scraper and digital callipers.

Safety

  • The nozzle runs at about 200°C for PLA, and the bed is hot. Hands off while it prints.
  • A teacher is in the room whenever the printer runs. Keep it in a room with good air flow.
  • Let the bed cool. Lift the part with the scraper, not fingers.
  • Cut the brim off with flush cutters, pointing away from you. Wear safety glasses: small bits fly.
  • In the crash test, keep hands and feet off the track. Whoever holds the controller calls "clear" first. Everyone wears safety glasses, because a snapped part can flick.

Print it

  1. Open ping-mount-v3.stl in Cura. Do not rotate it. It is already on its side.
  2. Set the profile above.
  3. Check the preview. Look for a brim all round and no supports. Each hole's teardrop point should be at the top.
  4. Check the time. Ours says about 26 minutes. Up to four fit on one plate.
  5. Print. Stay in the room.
  6. When the bed is cool, lift it off and trim the brim.

Fit it

  1. Measure your sensor's cans and the gap between their centres. If the centres are not 26 mm apart, stop. The file needs editing first.
  2. Push the cans through the front plate from behind. They should slide in. Never force them: the legs bend.
  3. Thread the cable tie through the slot between the cans. Loop it round the board. Pull it snug, not tight.
  4. Bolt the foot to the chassis with two M3 screws and nuts. Slide it in its slots until the sensor faces dead ahead.
  5. Plug in the sensor's four wires. Run your distance code and check a reading against a ruler at 30 cm.

Crash test

Run this on every new version before it goes to practice. It takes one CCA session.

  1. Set up a plywood board as a wall at the end of the track. Mark a start line 1 m away.
  2. Mount the bracket on a test robot. Put a strip of tape across the bend, so a crack shows.
  3. Call "clear". Drive into the wall at full speed.
  4. Check the bend and the screws. Note any crack, wobble or change in the sensor reading.
  5. Repeat. Stop at the first crack, or at 50 crashes.
  6. Log the build, print time and crash count. Compare with the table on the main page.

Printing for the whole team

We have 6 practice robots, and keep one spare bracket each. That is 12 brackets, 4 to a plate. SkillForge's planning rule adds 5 minutes per print for heating and clearing.

  • One plate: 4 × 26 = 104 minutes, plus 5, so 109 minutes.
  • Three plates: 109 × 3 ÷ 60 is about 5.5 hours.
  • Our CCA runs two 2.5-hour sessions a week. So the full set takes just over a week.

A single broken bracket is back on the robot in about 40 minutes: 31 for the print and heat-up, then trim and bolt on.
