Back to Soft Landing

Lesson plan, 60 minutes

Variables that change variables

Sec 2 students finish the Soft Landing starter. They learn how a frame loop updates speed and position. Then they share it, or choose not to.

  • Class: 36 Sec 2 students. Most did one Scratch unit in Sec 1.
  • Time: one 60-minute double period in the computer lab.
  • Devices: one lab PC each, with a keyboard. Scratch in the browser. The Scratch app is installed as a backup.
  • Accounts: student accounts from my Scratch teacher account, in one class. The starter is shared in the class studio.
  • Printouts: 36 task cards. The sharing rules on one slide.
  • On the projector: Soft Landing on this page, for its slow motion and frame trace.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain how a loop updates speed, then position, once per frame.
  • Use a condition with "and" to guard a variable, so fuel can't go below 0.
  • Place a decision after a loop, and explain why it is not inside.
  • Share a remix with credit and no personal details, or hand in a file instead.

Success criteria

  1. With no keys pressed, my lander speeds up as it falls.
  2. Holding up slows it, and fuel stops at 0.
  3. Landing slower than -2 on the pad chimes. Anything else crashes.
  4. My Notes and Credits name the starter and say what I changed.

The lesson

  1. 0 to 5 min. Settle. Log in with class accounts. Open the class studio and remix the starter.
  2. 5 to 12 min. Hook. One student flies Soft Landing on the projector, in slow motion. The class watches the trace table. Question: speed y is -0.5 now. What is it 30 frames later, with the engine off? (Answer: -2.)
  3. 12 to 17 min. I build task 1 live. Two blocks, one question: what does each one change? I don't build task 2.
  4. 17 to 40 min. Tasks 1 to 3 on their own. After each task, show your neighbour it working, then move on. Finished early: the stretch.
  5. 40 to 48 min. Sharing. Go through the 5 rules below. Everyone writes Notes and Credits. Then each student picks a route: Share to the class studio, or File, then Save to your computer, and upload the file to our class folder.
  6. 48 to 55 min. Play 2 classmates' games in the studio. Leave 1 comment each, in the format below.
  7. 55 to 60 min. Exit ticket on paper: gravity becomes -0.1. With no engine, is the crash faster, slower or the same? Why? Log out.

Check: 5 + 7 + 5 + 23 + 8 + 7 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Discussion question if time allows: does it matter if "change speed y by gravity" comes after "change y by speed y"? It does, slightly. The new speed takes effect one frame later. The lander still falls, so most students won't notice until they look at the trace.

Sharing rules

  1. Shared means public. Anyone on the internet can see your project and comments. A class account does not change that.
  2. No personal details anywhere: not your real full name, school, class, phone, address, or social media and chat usernames. That includes titles, notes, sprite names and costumes.
  3. Remix with credit. Make a meaningful change, and name the original in Notes and Credits. Scratch adds a "Thanks to" line, but say what you changed.
  4. Comments: one specific thing that works, and one suggestion. Be kind, not critical. No spam or chain messages.
  5. Report, don't reply. If something is mean or inappropriate, click Report and tell me.

All 5 rules come from the Scratch Community Guidelines. Rule 2 follows the guidelines' own list of what to keep private.

The comment format

"Your [thing] works because [reason]. Could you try [idea]?" Example: "Your exhaust fades nicely because of the ghost effect. Could you try a fuel warning at 10?"

Why there is a no-share route

The teacher account FAQ is clear on three points:

  • There is no private class. Anything shared within a class is open to the Scratch community.
  • A teacher account can only see shared student projects.
  • Comments can't be switched off for the whole site. The FAQ points to the offline Scratch app, which has no online community.

So sharing is the easy way for me to mark, but it is public. A student who doesn't want that saves the .sb3 file and hands it in. Both routes are marked the same way.

Beginners and fast finishers

Students new to coding

  • The live demo of task 1 is for them. I keep it on the projector during work time.
  • The task card shows every block they need. If they are stuck, I ask them to read the speed y monitor aloud while it runs. That usually shows which block is missing.

Fast finishers

  • Stretch 1: exhaust clones. Then: how many Puff clones can exist at once? (8.)
  • Stretch 2: a "Missed the pad" message, with one more if-else.
  • Stretch 3: write the loop as 3 lines of Python-style text on the back of the card. For example: speed_y = speed_y + gravity.

How I mark it

I fly every project once, with no keys pressed. Then once more, burning early. That checks criteria 1 to 3 in under a minute each. Criterion 4 is in Notes and Credits, or in the file I'm handed. Each criterion gets "met", "nearly" or "not yet". The exit tickets tell me whether to reteach frame order next lesson.
