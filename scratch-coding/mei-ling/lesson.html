Club session: make Blue Bin Catch work

One 90-minute after-school session. Pairs finish the starter project, then share it safely.

  • Who: 20 P5 pupils in my coding club, in 10 pairs. Most have done 3 or 4 sessions of Scratch.
  • Time: 90 minutes after school, with a 5-minute break.
  • Devices: 10 school iPads with Scratch in the browser. Mine is on the projector.
  • Accounts: student accounts I made with my Scratch teacher account. One account per pair this week.
  • Printouts: 10 task cards, 20 login cards, 10 red "stuck" cards, the sharing rules on A3.
  • Link to science: it revises which waste can be recycled.

By the end, pupils can

  • Make clones fall with a loop, and delete them at the bottom.
  • Use an if block and a variable to keep score.
  • Stop a game with a countdown and a broadcast.
  • Share a project the safe way, with credit and no personal details.

Success criteria, in pupil words

  1. Our rubbish falls and disappears at the bottom.
  2. Our score goes up when we catch something.
  3. Our game stops by itself after 30 seconds.
  4. Our Notes and Credits say whose starter we used. They have no real names.

The week before

  1. Request a teacher account early. The Scratch Team checks that you are an educator first.
  2. Make a class. Add 10 student accounts. Pupils need no email. Scratch uses yours.
  3. Usernames: an animal and a number, like "otter4821". No real names. Print each one on a login card.
  4. Share the starter from your account. Add it to the class studio.
  5. Charge the iPads. Log in to one account on the school Wi-Fi to test it.

My rule for login trouble: a pair whose login fails joins another pair as a three. I don't fix passwords in club time. I reset them afterwards from My Classes.

The session, minute by minute

  1. 0 to 10 min. Arrive, water, sit in pairs. Login cards out. Log in and open the class studio.
  2. 10 to 20 min. Warm up. Two pupils play Blue Bin Catch on the projector. Everyone else shouts "blue bin" or "general bin" for each item. Ask: which one surprised you? (It is always tissue.)
  3. 20 to 25 min. Open the starter and click Remix. Scratch adds a "Thanks to" line that links back to my starter. Press the flag and Start. Rubbish piles up at the top. Ask: what is missing? Hand out task cards. Driver holds the iPad, navigator reads the card. Swap at each whistle, every 8 minutes.
  4. 25 to 45 min. Tasks 1 and 2. I walk round. A stuck pair puts up the red card. I help red cards first.
  5. 45 to 50 min. Break. iPads face down.
  6. 50 to 65 min. Task 3. Pairs who finish try the stretch.
  7. 65 to 75 min. Sharing rules. We read the 5 rules below together. Then a quick game: I read 4 made-up Notes and Credits. Pupils show thumbs up or down for each. Then pairs write their own Notes and Credits.
  8. 75 to 82 min. Pairs who agree click Share and add it to the class studio. Pairs who don't want to share show me on the iPad instead. Both are fine.
  9. 82 to 87 min. Play 2 other games in the studio. Leave 1 kind comment on each.
  10. 87 to 90 min. Log out. iPads back in the trolley. Login cards back to me.

Check: 10 + 10 + 5 + 20 + 5 + 15 + 10 + 7 + 5 + 3 = 90 minutes.

Our 5 sharing rules

  1. Use your Scratch name only. Never your real name, school, address or phone number.
  2. Shared means anyone in the world can see it. Share only when you and your partner agree.
  3. Used someone's work? Change something real, and say thank you in Notes and Credits.
  4. Comments: say one thing you like and one idea. Be kind.
  5. See something mean or scary? Don't reply. Tell Ms Tan and click Report.

Each rule comes from the Scratch Community Guidelines, in shorter words. The guidelines ask you to credit everyone whose work you used, and to make a meaningful change. Rule 3 says that in P5 words.

The 4 notes for the thumbs game

  • "Remix of Ms Tan's starter. We added the countdown." Thumbs up.
  • "By Jia Hui Lim and Arjun, 5B, from our school." Thumbs down: real names and class.
  • "Our game. We made it all." Thumbs down: no credit for the starter.
  • "Thanks to Ms Tan. Comment if you got more than 10." Thumbs up.

The names in these practice notes are made up.

What the teacher account can and can't do

I checked these in the teacher account FAQ. They changed how I run sharing.

  • There is no private class. Anything a pupil shares can be seen by the whole Scratch community.
  • You can only see projects that pupils have shared. So I check unshared work in person.
  • You can remove your pupils' comments and projects. You can't turn comments off for the whole site. They can be turned off one project at a time.
  • A class holds up to 150 students. A pupil can be in only one class.
  • If comments don't suit a group, the Scratch offline app has no online community at all.

Pupils new to coding, and fast finishers

New to coding

  • Pair them with a calm partner, and give them the navigator job first. Reading the card is a way in.
  • Task 1's blocks are already in the project. If they are lost, I point at the yellow note, not the blocks.

Finished early

  • Do the stretch: only recyclables score.
  • Then make the rubbish fall faster when time left is under 10. Or add a new item and decide which bin it goes in. They must check it on the Recycle Right site first.

How I check it

I play every pair's game once, on their iPad or in the studio. Each success criterion gets a tick, "nearly" or "not yet". Criterion 4 I read in Notes and Credits. Most pairs get 1 to 3 the first time. Criterion 3 is the one that needs a second session.
