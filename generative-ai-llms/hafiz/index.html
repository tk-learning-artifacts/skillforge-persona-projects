Claude, checked

A guide for trainers who teach data to working adults, and let them use Claude in class. Every prompt in it was run 3 times and checked against a real source.

I'm Hafiz. I teach data and AI to secondary students and adult learners. This guide is for my evening course, Data Basics for Working Adults. It runs for 6 weeks, with 20 learners aged 25 to 60 on their own laptops. Two other trainers teach it with me. They asked me one question: should our learners use a chatbot? My answer is yes, if they learn to check it. This guide shows how.

My course, my learners and my co-trainers are made up, like me. The test runs are real. Every Claude answer quoted here is copied from those runs.

Why Claude, and why adults

Claude is a chatbot made by the company Anthropic. You type a question and it writes an answer. I picked it for two reasons.

  • My learners are all adults. Anthropic's terms say you must be at least 18 to use Claude. So they can make their own free accounts. I would not do this with my secondary classes.
  • It flags its own limits. Prompts 5 and 6 ask for facts that could be old or wrong. In all 9 of those runs, Claude told me to check. That gives learners a habit to copy.

The second reason has a catch. A warning is useful. It does not make the answer right. The findings show where the warnings missed.

How I tested the prompts

  • Date: 24 October 2026. AI tools change often, so a test has a date.
  • Model: Claude Sonnet 5.5. I ran each prompt in a fresh session through Anthropic's own command-line tool, with web search off. The Claude app adds its own instructions, so its answers may differ a little.
  • Runs: 3 for each prompt. One run can be lucky. Three show whether a problem repeats.
  • Checks: I wrote down what "good" meant before I ran each prompt. Then I checked every number, date, formula and citation against a real source.
  • Prompts: 8, plus a second version of the sources prompt. That makes 27 runs.

Three things I did not expect

  1. The sums were right every time. Six runs of maths and formulas, no errors. I expected at least one slip.
  2. It was a year behind on Singapore's population. All 3 runs said about 6.11 million, as at June 2025. SingStat now gives 6.21 million, as at end-June 2026. To be fair, all 3 runs gave the date and told me to check SingStat.
  3. The studies were real, but one was retracted. Claude named 4 different papers across 6 runs. Every DOI was real when I looked it up. One paper, a meta-analysis by Wang and Fan, was retracted on 22 April 2026. Claude offered it twice as evidence. My "fixed" prompt did not stop that.

Point 3 surprised me most. In 2023, Walters and Wilder checked citations from ChatGPT. They found 55% of GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of GPT-4 citations were made up. My 6 runs are a tiny sample, so I won't claim a rate. The fake-paper problem looked smaller here. A real but retracted paper is harder to spot. Checking that a DOI works is no longer enough.

How it meets the brief

  • Accurate about what AI can do today. Every claim about Claude here comes from a dated test or from Anthropic's own pages. Where it did well, I say so. Where it failed, I show the run.
  • Prompts tested and classroom-ready. 27 runs, scored against checks I wrote first. Each prompt card says how to use it in class and what to check.
  • Covers risks and how to reduce them. Page 2 of the guide lists 6 risks. Each has one step you can take in your next lesson.
  • Clear without a technical background. No code. The one formula is explained part by part. Two of my made-up co-trainers teach Excel and business writing, not computing.

What I would change

Three runs a prompt is better than one. It is still a small sample. I tested on one day, with one model, with web search off. Many learners will have search on, and that changes the answers, for better or worse. I tested the quiz prompt for one turn only, not a whole conversation. Long chats drift, and I did not measure that.

The bigger gap: I tested the prompts, not the learners. I don't yet know if adults who catch one retracted paper keep checking in week 6. Next run of the course, I would count how many check without being told.
