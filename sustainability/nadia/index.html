Right Bin, Every Time

A six-week recycling project for a P4 class. We cut the wrong things in our blue box from about 4 in 10 to under 1 in 10.

I'm Nadia. My P4 class has 30 pupils with a wide range of needs. Some read well below P4 level. Some find noise and crowds hard. One pupil has low vision. A bin poster full of words reaches about half of them. So I built every part of this project to work three ways: with pictures, with words and out loud.

Why the class bin

Every MOE school has blue recycling bins. In Singapore, paper, plastic, glass and metal all go in the same blue bin. They are sorted later, at a recovery plant. That only works if the wrong things stay out.

  • NEA says about 40% of what goes into blue bins can't be recycled. Food, liquid, tissue and Styrofoam spoil the rest.
  • Singapore's household recycling rate was 11% in 2025.
  • Most rubbish is burned. The ash goes to Semakau Landfill. The government expects it to be full around 2035.

Those are big numbers. A P4 pupil can't change them. But they can change one box by the classroom door. And they can see the change in a week.

Project plan

Question: If every pupil can find the right bin in their own way, will our blue box get cleaner and our general bin get lighter?

Links to the UN goals: Goal 12, responsible consumption. Target 12.5 is to cut waste through "prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse". Target 12.8 is that people everywhere have the information they need.

Six weeks, step by step

  1. Week 0I asked our operations manager where the blue box goes. The cleaners tip it into the school's big blue bin, not the rubbish. If they didn't, I would stop here. I got a luggage scale, tongs, gloves and a tray.
  2. Weeks 1 and 2Baseline. The bins stay as they always were. I count and weigh every day. Each pupil does the sort check once.
  3. Weeks 3 and 4The four lessons, two a week. The new bin station goes up after lesson 2.
  4. Weeks 5 and 6After. Same counting, same time, same scale. Each pupil does the sort check again.

How we measure, the same way every day

  1. At 1.15 pm, I put on gloves. I lift each item out of the blue box with tongs, onto a tray. Pupils never touch the waste.
  2. I say each item out loud: "bottle, right", "tissue, wrong". The two bin checkers mark the picture tally sheet.
  3. Wrong items go into the general bag. Right items go back in the box.
  4. I tie the general waste bag and hang it on the luggage scale. The weigh helper reads the number and writes it down.
  5. I divide by the pupils present that day, from the attendance list.

Every item counts as one: a sheet, a bottle, a packet. The sort check uses the 10 printed cards. Each pupil picks how to answer: point to a bin card, say it, or write it.

Pictures. Every sign, card and sheet has a drawing. A pupil can show what they know by pointing.

Words. Short words in big type on an off-white page. No word is the only clue.

Out loud. I say each item when I count. The game can read itself out. Pupils can answer by talking.

Before and after

My class and these numbers are made up, like me. They show how the measuring and the analysis work. The facts about Singapore's recycling are real and linked below.

Wrong items in the blue box

  • Before: circle
  • After: diamond
  • Goal

Before, 36.4% to 42.9% of the items were wrong each day. That is close to NEA's figure of about 40%. After, every day was between 5.9% and 12.1%. Even the worst day after was far below the best day before.

The box also got fuller. It held 25.1 items a day before and 33.5 after. Right items went from 15.3 a day to 30.6. So pupils did not just stop putting things in. They moved paper and bottles from the general bin to the blue box.

General waste per pupil

  • Before: circle
  • After: diamond
  • Goal

The general bag fell from 95.9 g to 86.1 g per pupil a day. That is about 10% less. All 10 after days were below the lowest before day, 93 g. So the drop is bigger than the normal day to day change.

It only just met the goal. That makes sense. About 15 extra items a day moved to the blue box. Most were sheets of paper and bottles, which are light. Recycling right moves waste. It does not make less of it.

What I can and can't claim

  • I can claim the blue box in this class got much cleaner. The before and after days don't overlap at all.
  • I can't claim the lessons alone did it. The new bin station and my daily counting out loud changed too. Pupils knew someone was watching.
  • Two weeks after is short. I plan one more count in week 10, with no warning.
  • After day 5 was the day before a public holiday. Only 28 pupils came. I kept it in, because the per-pupil number allows for it.

How pupils take part

The full plan is on the four lessons page. In short:

  • Four 60-minute lessons in weeks 3 and 4. Pupils sort real clean items, play the Which bin? game, and make the bin signs.
  • Four class jobs that rotate each week: two bin checkers, a weigh helper and a reminder. Each job has a picture job card.
  • A five-minute bin routine before dismissal, every day for six weeks.
  • Pupils see their own chart each Friday. They choose the next week's tip.

Checking it against the rubric

  • A measurable drop in waste. Wrong items fell from 39.0% to 8.6%. General waste fell about 10% per pupil.
  • Clear learning outcomes. Each lesson has outcomes in pupil words. The sort check measures them, in three ways.
  • Works within school limits. It costs a luggage scale, tongs and gloves. Counting takes me about 8 minutes a day. The lessons fit in four 60-minute slots.
  • Other teachers could adopt it. The signs, cards and sheets print on A4. The game runs on a school Chromebook or iPad.

What I would change

Counting by hand takes me 8 minutes every day. That is fine for six weeks, but no teacher keeps it up all year. Next time, I'd train two pupils to count with me from week 3. I'd still do the tongs myself.

The project also says little about using less. Our general bag is still about 2.5 kg a day. Most of it is tissue and snack wrappers. A second project on reuse would move that number more than better sorting will.

And I missed goal 3 by one pupil. A catch-up lesson for pupils who were away would have fixed it. I'd plan it from the start.
