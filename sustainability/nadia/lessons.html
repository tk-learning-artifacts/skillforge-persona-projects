Back to Right Bin, Every Time

Student engagement plan: four lessons and a daily routine

Four 60-minute lessons in weeks 3 and 4. Then an 8-minute bin routine every day.

  • Who: my P4 class of 30. Groups of 5, chosen by me, mixing strong and weaker readers.
  • When: two lessons a week, in our 60-minute Science and form teacher slots.
  • Per group: a tray of 11 clean items, one of each game item except the jar. I wash and dry them first. The one glass jar stays on my desk.
  • Printouts: the sort cards, bin signs, job cards and tally sheet.
  • Devices: 7 Chromebooks for lesson 1, one per group and a spare.
  • Safety: no real rubbish, ever. Pupils handle only clean, dry items I have prepared. I carry the glass jar. Batteries are dead ones with taped ends, and I keep them.

Learning outcomes

By the end, each pupil can:

  1. Put 8 of 10 common items in the right bin: blue, general or somewhere else.
  2. Give one reason why a wrong item spoils the blue bin.
  3. Read our class chart and say if we got better.

In pupil words, on the board: "I can find the right bin. I can say why. I can read our chart."

Each pupil shows outcome 1 in the sort check, by pointing, saying or writing. Outcome 2 is one sentence, said or written, on an exit card. Outcome 3 is checked in lesson 4, when pupils read the chart.

Lesson 1: What's in our box? (60 min)

  1. 0 to 5 min. Settle. Show our week 1 tally as a big bar of drawn items. "4 in 10 of our things were wrong."
  2. 5 to 15 min. I tip out a clean, prepared box on the front table. Pupils guess "right" or "wrong" for each item with thumbs up or down. No words needed.
  3. 15 to 35 min. Groups sort their tray into three bin mats. I visit each group. I ask "why?" about one item.
  4. 35 to 50 min. The Which bin? game on a Chromebook, one per group. Pupils take turns. "Read it out" is on.
  5. 50 to 57 min. Talk: which item surprised you? Most groups say tissue or drink packets.
  6. 57 to 60 min. Exit card: draw or write one wrong item.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 2: Make the signs (60 min)

  1. 0 to 5 min. Recap with three sort cards. Pupils point to the bin.
  2. 5 to 15 min. Look at my sample sign. What makes it easy? Pupils notice: a picture, a shape, a short word.
  3. 15 to 45 min. Each group makes one big sign. Two groups per bin. They glue on the printed bin picture, then draw or stick three item cards. Round-tip scissors only.
  4. 45 to 55 min. Gallery walk. Each group checks another group's sign: is any item in the wrong place?
  5. 55 to 60 min. We put up the bin station: the best sign for each bin, at pupil eye level.

Check: 5 + 10 + 30 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 3: Jobs and the routine (60 min)

  1. 0 to 10 min. I show the four job cards. Each has a picture and three steps.
  2. 10 to 25 min. Practice run. I play the counter with an empty, clean box. Bin checkers mark the tally sheet as I call items.
  3. 25 to 30 min. The weigh helper practises reading the scale with a bag of books.
  4. 30 to 50 min. Pairs play the sort cards as a matching game. Pupils who finish early make a sign for the canteen. I ask the canteen vendor first.
  5. 50 to 60 min. Choose this week's jobs. Pupils who don't like crowds can be the recorder, at their own desk.

Check: 10 + 15 + 5 + 20 + 10 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 4: Read our chart (60 min)

  1. 0 to 10 min. The class chart so far: dots for each day. Pupils say what they see. "The dots went down."
  2. 10 to 25 min. Why per pupil? I ask: on a day with 25 pupils, is the bag lighter? Pupils share out 30 cubes to show it.
  3. 25 to 40 min. Each pupil picks one tip for next week. They show it as a drawing, a sentence or a short spoken clip on the iPad.
  4. 40 to 55 min. The sort check for 10 pupils. The rest play the game or make tip posters. The other 20 do the check in weeks 5 and 6.
  5. 55 to 60 min. Vote on next week's tip. Thumbs or a show of hands.

Check: 10 + 15 + 15 + 15 + 5 = 60 minutes.

The daily bin routine (8 min, weeks 3 to 6)

  1. The reminder says "bin time" and holds up the bin card.
  2. I count the blue box with tongs and gloves. I call each item.
  3. The two bin checkers mark the tally sheet.
  4. The weigh helper reads the scale. I write the number on our chart.

On Fridays, pupils see the week's dots on the chart. It takes another two minutes.

For different pupils

  • A pupil who can't read yet: every card has a picture. They answer by pointing.
  • A pupil with low vision: big cards, printed at A5, with thick outlines. "Bigger words" in the game.
  • A pupil who finds noise hard: lesson 1's tip-out is loud. They can sit at the back with ear defenders, or be my helper at the front.
  • A pupil who finishes early: find an item at home that isn't on our cards. Bring a picture of it, and we look it up together.
  • A pupil who is away for a lesson: a 10-minute catch-up with the sort cards. I didn't plan this the first time, and I should have.
