Power Down Cup · deliverable 3

Engagement plan

12 CCA members run it. 8 classes play it. Here is who does what, week by week, and every sheet we printed.

Learning outcomes

Robotics CCA members (Sec 3, 12 students)

  1. Calibrate a light sensor against what really happened, and say how many minutes it gets wrong.
  2. Turn minutes of use into kWh, then into kg of CO2, and show the working.
  3. Design a scoring rule that is fair to a class that starts low, and defend it.
  4. Explain why we need a baseline and an "after" period, not just the cup weeks.

Check: each member gets one room's raw log and its power settings. They give the lights-on minutes, the kWh and the kg CO2. Then they pick a threshold from a lights-on and a lights-off reading. All 4 right is a pass.

Every Sec 3 class

  1. Name the things in their room that use power after school: lights, fans, the projector.
  2. Do a 30-second switch-off routine as the last one out.
  3. Read the leaderboard: why a "% drop" is fairer than raw minutes.

Who does what

  • Me: plan, safety, place and collect the sentinels, check the data.
  • CCA members: build, calibrate, read logs every morning, do the 3 pm patrol in pairs, make the leaderboard. There is at least one CCA member in every Sec 3 class.
  • Power captains: two per class, chosen by the class. They do the switch-off check and press A.
  • Form teachers: give 5 minutes for the launch pitch. Nothing else is asked of them.
  • Operations manager: told in advance. Agrees that nothing is wired in.

Four CCA sessions before launch (2 hours each)

Session 1: Why, and build version 1

  1. 0 to 15 min. The question. How many rooms do you pass after school with the lights on? Guess, then I show the patrol count from my walk last Friday.
  2. 15 to 35 min. kWh in 20 minutes: power × time. Work out one room's lights for one afternoon.
  3. 35 to 95 min. Pairs code version 1 in MakeCode and test it under a desk lamp.
  4. 95 to 110 min. Race: whose sentinel reads closest to a light meter app on my phone? Guess first, then measure.
  5. 110 to 120 min. Pack up. Each pair labels a micro:bit.

Check: 15 + 20 + 60 + 15 + 10 = 120 minutes.

Session 2: Test day

  1. 0 to 10 min. Plan the test: which rooms, who sits in, what they write down.
  2. 10 to 70 min. Two rooms, lights on then off, blinds open then shut. Sitters record the truth.
  3. 70 to 100 min. Compare logs with the truth. The calibration simulator for anyone who finishes.
  4. 100 to 120 min. What failed? Each pair proposes one fix. The hood wins the vote.

Check: 10 + 60 + 30 + 20 = 120 minutes.

Session 3: Version 3 and the rules

  1. 0 to 50 min. Build hoods, add button A and the nudge. Teacher cuts the slots.
  2. 50 to 80 min. Scoring debate. Version 1 of the rules ranked raw kWh. Then I show 3C and 3F, who start low. Teams argue for a fairer rule. We land on "% drop from your own baseline, ties go to zero days".
  3. 80 to 110 min. Calibrate each room's threshold with the room's own reading, lights on and off.
  4. 110 to 120 min. Pack up.

Check: 50 + 30 + 30 + 10 = 120 minutes.

Session 4: Launch prep (after the baseline weeks)

  1. 0 to 30 min. Read the baseline logs. Each pair makes one room's baseline card.
  2. 30 to 60 min. Build the first leaderboard poster, all zeros.
  3. 60 to 100 min. Rehearse the 5-minute pitch in pairs. The rest of the team heckle with real questions.
  4. 100 to 120 min. Print captain cards and switch stickers. Assign pitch pairs to classes.

Check: 30 + 30 + 40 + 20 = 120 minutes.

The six weeks

  1. Weeks 1 and 2: baseline. The CCA member in each class presses A at dismissal. Silent mode. Patrol at 3 pm.
  2. Week 3, Monday: 5-minute pitch in every class during form time. Classes choose two captains.
  3. Weeks 3 and 4: the cup. Captains do the switch check and press A. Leaderboard on the noticeboard every Monday, and one line in the morning announcements.
  4. End of week 4: the winning class gets a trophy that the CCA designs and 3D-prints. Their class name goes on the robotics lab door.
  5. Weeks 5 and 6: after. Silent mode again. No leaderboard. CCA members press A again.
  6. Week 7: results to all classes, with the "after" numbers. That is where the habit question gets asked out loud.

Printables

Each box prints cleanly on its own.

Power captain card

You are the last one out. Every day, 30 seconds:

  1. Lights: all switches off.
  2. Fans: all switches off.
  3. Projector and screen: off.
  4. Press A on the sentinel. Tick: done. Cross and beep: something is still on.

Only use the wall switches. Never open a switchboard. If a switch is broken, tell your form teacher.

Switch sticker

LAST ONE OUT? Lights off. Fans off. Then press A.

Why %? A class that already switched off can't drop as many minutes. So we compare each class with itself.

Pitch script, 5 minutes, two CCA members

  1. "For two weeks, there was a micro:bit on your cupboard. It counted how long your lights stayed on after you left." Show the class's own baseline minutes.
  2. "Every afternoon your room used about this much power for nobody." Show it in kWh.
  3. "For the next two weeks, it's a league. Every class against its own baseline."
  4. Show the captain card. Ask for two captains.
  5. Take two questions. Leave the sticker on the switch.
