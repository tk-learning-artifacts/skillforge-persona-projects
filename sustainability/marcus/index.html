I'm Marcus. I coach a robotics team for competitions. My team likes a scoreboard, and so does every Sec 3 class I know. So I asked: what if saving energy had one?

Our classrooms run on lights and ceiling fans. After the last lesson, a lot of rooms stay lit and the fans keep spinning, for hours, with nobody inside. That waste is easy to see, easy to measure and easy to stop. It suits a competition.

Deliverable 1Project plan

Question: Will a class-against-class league cut the energy our empty classrooms waste after school? And will the habit stick once the league ends?

What we measure: two things, in the 4 hours after each room empties.

  • Lights-on minutes. A micro:bit "sentinel" reads the light level every minute. How it works.
  • Fans left on. Two CCA members patrol the 8 rooms at 3 pm and count spinning fans.

How we turn that into energy: lights minutes × lights power, plus fans × fan power × hours left on. Then kWh × 0.402 kg CO2 per kWh, Singapore's grid average for 2024.

Links: UN Goal 7, target 7.3: "double the global rate of improvement in energy efficiency". MOE aims for a two-thirds cut in net carbon emissions from schools by 2030. Our school has to start somewhere.

Timeline

  1. Weeks 1 and 2: baseline. Sentinels log silently. Classes know a CCA project is measuring light. They don't know about the cup yet.
  2. Weeks 3 and 4: the cup. Launch in every class. Power captains. Leaderboard every Monday.
  3. Weeks 5 and 6: after. No leaderboard, no announcements. Sentinels keep logging. Does it stick?

Goals and results

1. Cut energy in cup weeks

Goal: 50% less than baseline, all 8 rooms.
Result: 84% less Met

2. Keep it after the cup

Goal: still 60% less in weeks 5 and 6.
Result: 56% less Missed

3. CCA can measure

Goal: all 12 members pass the calibration and kWh check.
Result: 12 of 12 Met

4. Captains do the check

Goal: every class does the switch-off check on 8 of 10 cup days.
Result: 7 of 8 classes Missed

What the numbers say

  • Every class dropped in the cup weeks. The mean room went from 85 lights-on minutes per afternoon to 14. Fans spinning at 3 pm, across all 8 rooms, went from 18.5 to 2.8.
  • The drop is real, not noise. In the 3 biggest rooms, 3A, 3D and 3G, even the worst cup day was far below the median baseline day.
  • Then it faded. After the cup, lights crept back to 37 minutes. That is still 56% less energy than baseline, but it missed my 60% goal.
  • 3G is the warning. Its captains pressed A on the way out but skipped the switch check on four days. The sentinel beeped, and they left anyway. It finished last, just behind 3B. After the cup it was only 41% below baseline.
  • The CO2 is small. About 300 kg a year is a rounding error on a school bill. What matters is that 300 students now switch off, and 12 can prove it with data.

Moving the sliders changes the kWh a lot. It barely changes the ranking. 3E wins at every setting I tried, because it cut both lights and fans to almost zero. That told me the scoring rule was fair enough.

Deliverable 3Student engagement

Two groups take part. The full plan, the sessions and the printables are on the engagement plan page.

  • 12 Sec 3 robotics CCA members build, test and run it. Four 2-hour CCA sessions before launch. 20 minutes a week after.
  • 8 Sec 3 classes compete. A 5-minute launch pitch by two CCA members. Two power captains per class. A leaderboard poster every Monday.
  • Materials: a power captain card, a switch sticker, a patrol sheet, a leaderboard poster and the pitch script.

Against the rubric

  • Measurable drop. 84% less empty-room energy in the cup, 56% after. Both measured against a two-week baseline in the same rooms.
  • Clear learning outcomes. CCA members calibrate a sensor and convert minutes to kg CO2. Classes learn what uses power and a switch-off routine. See the outcomes.
  • School limits. 9 micro:bit V2s, which many schools already have. Cardboard, Blu-tack, batteries. No wiring into anything. No touching the switchboard.
  • Others can adopt it. The code is one short file. The build page has every step. A school without micro:bits can run the 3 pm patrol alone.

What I'd change

The cup worked because of pressure, and the pressure ended. The after weeks lost a third of the gain. Next time, I wouldn't stop the league cold. I'd run a monthly "spot check" round for the rest of the term, with no warning.

The measurement is also partial. We only cover after school. Rooms are empty during PE, lab lessons and recess too, and we never saw those hours. The fan number is the weakest: one patrol at 3 pm and a guess at how long fans then run.

And the sentinel's beep changed behaviour on its own, in the cup weeks. So I can't split the effect of the league from the effect of the beep. Next time I'd keep the beep off in half the rooms.
