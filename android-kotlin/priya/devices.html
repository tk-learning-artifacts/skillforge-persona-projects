Device plan

One app, three routes

Four Sec 3 classes in three schools. Each school's devices decide its route. The learning goals and the task card stay the same.

The schools and their devices are made up for this example. The requirements are Google's, checked in October 2026. Replace my three columns with one device from each of your schools.

School A: Class 3A, Studio with the emulator

  • 36 students, one Mac each, 40 Macs in the lab. Pairs still share for the TODOs. Pairing makes them talk about the code.
  • IT creates one emulator, a Pixel phone image, on every Mac before week 3. The first boot is slow, so they start it once.
  • Risk: 36 emulators start at once and the lab network is busy with downloads. That is why IT does the first sync, not the class.

School B: Classes 3B and 3C, Studio with a loan phone

  • Students use their own learning devices: Windows laptops with 8 GB. Studio runs, the emulator would not.
  • MOE set out for every secondary student to own a personal learning device by the end of 2021, with a Device Management Application installed. So installing Studio is a request to the school's IT team, weeks ahead. Ask early.
  • The school lends 12 Android phones, all Android 13. Developer options and USB debugging are turned on before week 3. Pairs plug in for each run, so 18 pairs share 12 phones.
  • Students do not use their own phones. USB debugging lets a laptop install apps and read logs on the phone. That is not something to switch on for a personal phone in class.
  • While waiting for a phone, pairs use the Preview pane. It shows a composable without running the app.

School C: Class 3D, half the class can't run Studio

The class has Chromebooks with 4 GB. The school has a trolley of 10 Windows laptops with 8 GB. So the class splits.

Studio group: 18 students

  • 9 pairs on 9 trolley laptops. One spare.
  • 5 loan phones from School B, shared.
  • Same task card as Schools A and B.

Blocks group: 18 students

  • 9 pairs on Chromebooks with MIT App Inventor in the browser.
  • They build the blocks version: a filtered list, a second screen, a start value.
  • They test on the Chromebook itself, or with the Companion app on a phone or tablet.

How the lesson runs with two groups

  1. The whole class watches the same opening: the replay, and "where does search live?".
  2. Pairs are mixed by confidence, not by who sits together. A keen Chromebook pair can ask for the trolley next week.
  3. The last 6 minutes: each Studio pair shows a Blocks pair their app, and the other way round. Both answer: "where is the list, and how does the second screen know which word?"
  4. Week 6 swaps the groups if the trolley is free. If not, the Blocks group reads and annotates the Kotlin of Word Wall on paper.

If the App Inventor site is blocked, or the Companion can't connect on school Wi-Fi, ask IT. App Inventor warns that some school firewalls block the Wi-Fi link. Its USB option works only with Android devices.

Every group answers the same exit ticket. So the team can compare learning across the three routes.

IT checklist, two weeks before week 3

  • All schools: allow kotlinlang.org and appinventor.mit.edu. Weeks 1 and 2 run in the browser.
  • Schools A and B, and the School C trolley: install Android Studio and the SDK. Open the starter once on each device so Gradle downloads the libraries.
  • School A: make one emulator on each Mac, and boot it once.
  • School B: approve Studio on the learning devices. On Windows, some phones need their maker's USB driver.
  • Loan phones: turn on Developer options by tapping Build number seven times. Turn on USB debugging. No personal accounts on them.
  • After week 6: turn USB debugging off on every loan phone.

Why no student app goes on Google Play

Students ask this in week 6, every time. Here is the short answer.

  • A Play Console account costs US$25, once.
  • You must be 18 or older to sign up. Our Sec 3 students are about 15.
  • A new personal account must first run a closed test with at least 12 testers, opted in for 14 days in a row.

So in this course, apps stay on school phones and emulators. If a school wants to publish a student app one day, a staff member owns the account, and the school agrees on it first.
