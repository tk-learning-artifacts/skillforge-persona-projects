Device plan: Studio on the lab PCs, the app on club phones

For a club of 20 Sec 2 and Sec 3 members, two hours on Friday afternoons, in the school computer lab.

The club, the lab PC and the phones are made up for this example. The system requirements are Google's, checked in October 2026. Do this check with your own lab.

Step 1: check one lab PC

I opened Settings, then System, then About on one lab PC. Then I held it against the Android Studio system requirements for Windows.

Step 2: pick the route

Studio with the emulator

Not on these PCs. I tried it once on 8 GB: the emulator took minutes to boot and Studio froze while it ran.

Studio with a phone: our route

Studio runs on the PCs. The app runs on a phone over a USB cable. The PC does not have to pretend to be a phone, so 8 GB is enough.

App Inventor in the browser

Our backup. A member whose PC fails that day builds the same word list in App Inventor, and tests it on a club phone with the Companion app.

The Preview pane

While a pair waits for a phone, they check their screens in Studio's Preview. It draws a composable without running the app.

Step 3: phones

  • The club owns 6 Android phones. 10 pairs share them, so a pair runs, tests and hands the phone on. Most testing is quick.
  • Developer options are on. You tap Build number seven times in Settings, then turn on USB debugging. I do this once, before term.
  • USB, not Wi-Fi. Wireless debugging needs Android 11 or newer, and the school Wi-Fi may block it. Cables just work.
  • Members never plug in their own phones. USB debugging lets a computer install apps and read logs. I don't want that on a personal phone with their accounts and photos. The club phones have no personal accounts on them.
  • After the term, I turn USB debugging off on all 6.

Step 4: what I ask IT to do, the week before

  1. Install Android Studio and its SDK on all 20 PCs.
  2. Install the USB driver, if the phones need one. On Windows, some phones need their maker's driver for adb.
  3. Open the starter project once on each PC and let Gradle sync. The first sync downloads the libraries. That way the club doesn't wait for 20 downloads at once.
  4. Let student accounts save in one project folder, and let Studio run adb.

If something breaks

  • Phone not found: try another cable first. Some cables only charge. Then check that USB debugging is on and that someone accepted the debugging prompt on the phone. It asks to trust this computer.
  • Gradle sync fails: the PC was missed in the pre-sync. That pair joins a neighbour today.
  • Studio freezes: close other programs. Chrome with 20 tabs and Studio on 8 GB do not mix.
  • Half the PCs fail: everyone switches to App Inventor for the day. Same word list, same two screens. The Kotlin waits a week.

Where this fits in the club term

  1. Week 1: the Kotlin tour in the browser. Nothing to install.
  2. Week 2: a Compose counter, run on a club phone.
  3. Week 3: this starter session.
  4. Week 4: members add their own game's words and playtest each other's apps.
