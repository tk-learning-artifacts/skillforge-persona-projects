    What I noticed, watching it back

    I watched it three times. First with sound, then with the sound off for my body. Last, I only listened, with my eyes shut. Each time stamp jumps the player to that moment.

    1. I rushed the first 30 seconds. My pace was close to 170 words a minute. I sounded keen, but the back row was still sitting down.
    2. The five-second wait worked. Nobody answered at three seconds. At five, a father at the back put his hand up. I nearly filled the gap.
    3. My first "okay". It came where I changed topic. A pause would have done the same job.
    4. Hands close together, then wide apart. With the sound off, you can still see a thousand become a million. My best gesture of the day.
    5. I caught the frowns on the right. Turning my whole body to that side helped more than the new words did. Two parents nodded at "one big dial".
    6. My back was to the room for about 4 seconds while I opened the calculator. Next time I open it in a second tab before I start.
    7. Only about half the room said it with me. "Say it with me?" sounded optional. "Everyone, say it with me" is better.
    8. After 30 seconds of pair talk, the room was louder and warmer. More people asked questions after this point than before it.
    9. My answer to the bank question was nearly as fast as my opening. I felt caught out, and I sped up. Stepping towards him was right. Speeding up was not.
    10. I ended on what they could do, and on their questions. No "that's all". I stayed still for two seconds, and they clapped.

      How I would film your talk

      1. Put a phone at the back, on a tripod or a pile of books. Landscape, at chest height.
      2. Point it at you and the screen. Keep the audience out of the shot. Tell the room you are filming yourself before you start.
      3. If students or parents could appear, check your school's rules first. Ask before you film anyone.
      4. Put a second phone near you, recording sound only. Room sound from the back is weak.
      5. Watch it three times: with sound, sound off, then eyes shut. Note one time stamp for each rubric line.
