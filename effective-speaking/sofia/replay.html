My aim was half and half. I talked for well over half, and the campers spoke aloud for under a minute in all. Most of their talk was one-word answers in chorus. The long yellow stretch in the middle is buddy time. That is where the learning happened, and it is the part I'd make longer.

What I noticed, watching it back

I watched it twice: once for my voice, once with the sound off for where I stood. Tap a time to jump there.

  1. Clap, then "Pixels". The circle was quiet in under ten seconds. The call and answer did the shouting for me, so I never had to.
  2. "Before snack time" works better than "in 12 minutes". Campers know when snack is. Nobody under ten knows what 12 minutes feels like.
  3. I walked over to Wei Ling. My back was to half the circle for about ten seconds. In the middle of a circle, someone always sees your back, so I try to turn every two or three sentences. Here I forgot.
  4. Too many numbers in one breath. Ethan loved 1,920 by 1,080. Mia's face went blank. One number, "two million", would have been enough.
  5. 22 seconds of handing out cards. The talk stopped dead. Next time the cards wait on the tape squares before anyone arrives.
  6. I crouched next to Mia, so we were the same height. The smallest camper got the biggest moment, and her shortcut became a joke we used all week.
  7. Two campers were poking each other at the back. I didn't name them. A stand-up, sit-down check got everyone moving and back with me.
  8. Five seconds of silence. Aryan's hand went up at two. I kept waiting, and three more hands went up. It felt like forever. On the replay it's nothing.
  9. Buddy time. I talked least here, and walked the outside of the circle so I never stood over anyone.
  10. The big reveal dragged. Rows five to eight took over half a minute, and most campers had guessed it by row four. Next time, Jun colours them while we talk, and we turn the mat round.

    If you film your own camp talk

    1. Check the camp's consent forms first. Only film if parents have agreed, and keep the video private.
    2. Point the camera at you, from just outside the circle. Campers' backs, not faces.
    3. Stand the phone on something steady, at sitting height. That is the campers' view of you.
    4. Watch it once with the sound off. Count how often your back faces each side of the circle.
    5. Delete the video once you have your notes, unless the camp needs it.
