Deliverable 4

Build the vote board

One double period. Pairs play the vote board and find its parts. Then they finish it in React Pad with their own games.

  • Class: 32 Sec 2 students, aged 13 to 14, in 16 pairs. They know some JavaScript from earlier lessons: variables, functions, arrays and objects.
  • Time: a 70-minute double period, two periods of 35 minutes.
  • Devices: one school device per pair. iPads with keyboard covers or Chromebooks both work.
  • Editor: React Pad, in the browser. No account, no install, nothing sent anywhere.
  • Teacher: a laptop on the projector with the finished board open.
  • Printed: one task card per pair. For plan B, one printout of the starter's App.jsx per pair.
  • Before the lesson: each pair writes down one game they made last term, with a one-line pitch.

By the end, students can

  • Point to the components on a screen, and say which one holds the state.
  • Fix a missing key warning, and say why React wants keys.
  • Update state from a click by copying and changing a pattern.
  • Change the data in a JSON file and see the app change with it.

The lesson

  1. 0 to 5

    Settle. One device per pair. The React Pad link is on the board as a short link.

  2. 5 to 15

    Play first. Four volunteers vote on the projector board, using last term's games. Reveal. Cheer.

    Ask: what did the board have to remember between players? Collect answers on the whiteboard: tokens, totals, whose turn. That list is the state.

  3. 15 to 22

    Find the parts. Pairs sketch the board on paper and draw a box round each part: the coins, the filter, one game card, the list. Name each box.

    Compare with the tree on the project page. Most pairs miss that every card is the same component.

  4. 22 to 27

    Step 0: predict, then run. Pairs open the starter, read App.jsx, and write two guesses on the card.

  5. 27 to 37

    TODO 1: the key. Read the red console line out loud first. Then fix it.

    Show the leaderboard re-ordering on the projector. Ask: how does React know which row is which game after they swap?

  6. 35

    Bell between periods. Drivers swap. Students stay seated.

  7. 37 to 52

    TODO 2: voting. Read takeBack together, then copy its pattern into vote. Test by spending all 3 tokens.

    Walk the room. A pair with "NaN tokens left" gets hint 2, not the answer.

  8. 52 to 60

    Make it yours. Pairs put their own game into games.json. Vote for it.

  9. 60 to 66

    Swap and play. Pairs swap devices with the next pair and vote on their board. Pairs who finish early try the stretch, sorting the leaderboard.

  10. 66 to 70

    Exit card: "Name one piece of state in your board, and the click that changes it." Download the project if you want a copy. Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 7 + 5 + 10 + 15 + 8 + 6 + 4 = 70 minutes. The bell at 35 falls inside TODO 1. It is a short task and easy to pause.

Device check, the week before

  • Open the starter on one student iPad and one Chromebook, on the student Wi-Fi. The preview should show the games with a red key warning in the console.
  • Type a letter in the editor, then reload the page. Your change should still be there. If it is not, the browser is blocking storage. Students then press Download before they leave.
  • On an iPad, press Download once. The .zip should land in the Files app.
  • React Pad loads once from GitHub Pages, about 0.7 MB. After that, it runs on the device. Check the school filter allows tk-learning-artifacts.github.io.

Plan B

If the page won't load, pairs work on paper. They do step 0 and TODO 2 on the card and the code printout. They write the new line for vote by hand. I type the best three into React Pad on the projector. The class votes on which works.

For different students

  • New to JavaScript objects: pair them with a confident partner, and start them on Step 3, editing the JSON. It needs no code, and they see their own game appear.
  • Done early: the stretch. Then a new genre, a fourth token, or a second category such as "Best sound".
  • A student who takes over the keyboard: drivers swap at the bell and at every step. The card says so.
  • A student who needs less on screen at once: React Pad works at phone width. They can zoom in and see one file at a time.

How I check it

I play each pair's board once during the swap. Three questions: does +1 spend a token, is the key warning gone, and is their game on the list? The exit cards tell me who can link a click to the state it changes. That is the idea the next lesson builds on.
