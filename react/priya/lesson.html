Deliverable 4

Use it, then build it

A 60-minute lesson for Week 5 of our Sec 3 web elective. Students take the Checkpoint quiz, then finish its React starter in GitHub Codespaces.

  • Class: about 30 Sec 3 students, aged 15. Four trainers teach four classes from this one plan.
  • Time: one 60-minute lesson.
  • Devices: the school's Chromebooks, one per student. Nothing to install.
  • Editor: GitHub Codespaces in Chrome, from our template repository.
  • Accounts: every student has a GitHub account, made in Week 1 with the school's consent form. GitHub accounts need users aged 13 or over.
  • Seating: students sit in pairs. Each student types on their own Chromebook, but pairs talk through every prediction.
  • Printed: one task card per student, and the hint strips, cut up, in a tray.

By the end, students can

  • Say where state lives in a small React app, and why the score is not stored.
  • Load a JSON file in an effect, using a path with no slash at the start.
  • Update state from a click, and test it with one right and one wrong answer.
  • Save their work with a commit.

The lesson

  1. 0 to 5

    Settle. Chromebooks open, Chrome only. Post the Checkpoint link and the template link on the class page.

  2. 5 to 10

    Entry check. Students take the Checkpoint JavaScript set and paste their exit line into the class form.

    Trainer: scan the form. Note who must revisit J2 or J3. Those students get the hint strips first.

  3. 10 to 15

    Make a copy. Use this template, then Create a new repository, then Code, Codespaces, Create codespace on main.

    While it starts, students do step 0 on paper: two predictions about the starter.

  4. 15 to 20

    Run. Type npm run dev. The preview opens beside the code. Check both predictions. Two pairs share theirs.

  5. 20 to 30

    TODO 1, fill the gap. Load the set the student picked.

    At 27 minutes, show the blank page diagram from the module's "Why my deployed site is a blank page" guide. Ask: what would /questions/css.json do on GitHub Pages?

  6. 30 to 42

    TODO 2, finish the idea. Keep score. Test with one right and one wrong answer.

    Trainer: sit with the students who flagged J2 or J3 at the start.

  7. 42 to 50

    Stretch or catch up. Pairs who are done start TODO 3. Others finish TODO 2 with a hint strip.

  8. 50 to 54

    Save. Source Control, a message, Commit, Sync Changes. Pairs check each other's repository shows the commit.

  9. 54 to 58

    Exit question in the class form: "Where does your TODO 1 path go if you add a slash at the start?"

  10. 58 to 60

    Stop the codespace at github.com/codespaces, then pack up.

Check: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 10 + 12 + 8 + 4 + 4 + 2 = 60 minutes.

Device check, one week before

One trainer runs this on a student Chromebook, on the student Wi-Fi, in the actual lab.

  • A test student account can open a codespace from the template, and npm run dev opens the preview.
  • The school network lets through the addresses Codespaces needs. GitHub lists them in its meta API, under codespaces: *.github.com, *.api.github.com, *.github.dev, *.visualstudio.com, *.vscode-webview.net, *.windows.net, *.azureedge.net, *.msecnd.net and *.microsoft.com. GitHub's connection guide says to check that list.
  • Every student can sign in to GitHub. Collect the usernames in a class list.
  • Chrome is not blocked from opening the preview tab.

Codespaces stops after 30 minutes with no activity, by default. A codespace also has a maximum life of 12 hours. Neither matters inside one lesson. The commit at minute 50 keeps the work.

Each lesson uses about 2 of a student's 120 free core hours a month, on a 2-core machine. A student who forgets to stop the codespace loses at most another half hour of quota.

Plan B, if Codespaces won't load

  • Students open the starter running on its own page, and do step 0 there.
  • They do TODO 1 and 2 on the printed code, in pen. Pairs swap and check with the hint strips.
  • Next lesson starts with 10 minutes in Codespaces to type in what they wrote.

For different students

  • Flagged J2 or J3 at the start: hint strips first, and the trainer sits with them during TODO 2.
  • Done early: TODO 3, then add a fourth set of their own, with 5 questions and a source for each.
  • Slow to type: their partner reads the TODO aloud while they type. They swap for TODO 2.
  • Absent in Week 1, so no account yet: they pair with a partner on one Chromebook, and make the account after the lesson.

How the team checks it

Each trainer opens five repositories at random. TODO 1 and 2 done, with one commit, means the lesson worked. The exit question shows who understood the slash. I collect the four classes' results in one sheet. That tells me whether Week 6 starts with tic-tac-toe or with a fetch recap.
