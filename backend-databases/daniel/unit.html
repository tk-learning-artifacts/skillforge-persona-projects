How a sign-up app works

Three CCA sessions. Students follow a sign-up from browser to database. They write SQL and break my version 1 on purpose. Then they design the data for an app of their own.

  • Who: 18 Infocomm Club members, Sec 1 and Sec 2. Most have done some Python, a few have not.
  • Time: 3 CCA sessions of 2 hours, one a week. Each has a 10-minute break.
  • Devices: school Chromebooks. Nothing to install.
  • Tools, all with no sign-up: my app page, SQL Online IDE set to SQLite, and the Open-Meteo weather API. Students paste their SQL into a class Google Doc to keep it.
  • No hosting accounts. PythonAnywhere's terms need users old enough to sign a contract, which is 18 in Singapore. Only my app goes online.
  • Printouts: request slips for session 1, and the v1 and v2 login code for session 2. Also a design sheet per group.

By the end, students can

  • Say what the browser sends, what Flask does and what the database stores when someone signs up.
  • Write SELECT, INSERT and JOIN queries in SQLite.
  • Explain why ? placeholders stop the attack that breaks version 1.
  • Choose the fields an app needs, and leave out the rest.

Success criteria, in student words

  1. I can tell the story of one sign-up in 4 steps: request, SQL, redirect, page.
  2. My SQL makes the tables, adds fake rows and answers 2 questions, one with a JOIN.
  3. I can show where version 1 went wrong and how version 2 fixes it.
  4. Our design follows our 4 data rules.

Session 1: Follow one sign-up

  1. 0 to 10 Arrive, attendance, Chromebooks out.
  2. 10 to 25 Unplugged. Three volunteers play browser, Flask and database. The browser hands over a request slip: "POST /signup, Ali, 1E1, Robotics". Flask writes the SQL on a slip. The database adds a row on the whiteboard. Flask hands back "302: go to /".
  3. 25 to 45 Pairs use my app page. They sign up three fake students and fill a session. They copy one "What just happened" story onto their sheet.
  4. 45 to 55 A real API. Students open api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=1.29&longitude=103.85&current=temperature_2m. They note the status code in DevTools and 3 JSON keys.
  5. 55 to 65 Break.
  6. 65 to 100 SQL Online IDE, set to SQLite. Students paste my schema.sql, then write: all sessions; only Robotics; an INSERT for a fake student; the sign-ups ordered by class.
  7. 100 to 115 Share. One thing that surprised you. I go first: I forgot commit in my own app.
  8. 115 to 120 Pack up. Paste your SQL in the class doc.

Check: 10 + 15 + 20 + 10 + 10 + 35 + 15 + 5 = 120 minutes.

Session 2: JOINs, and breaking version 1

  1. 0 to 10 Arrive. Open last week's SQL from the class doc.
  2. 10 to 40 JOINs. First a JOIN of sign-ups with sessions. Then places left, with LEFT JOIN and COUNT. Why does Robotics on Friday vanish with a plain JOIN?
  3. 40 to 55 Break version 1. I read the rules out first. Then pairs switch my app to version 1 and log in with ' OR 1=1 --. They copy the SQL it built.
  4. 55 to 65 Break.
  5. 65 to 85 On paper, the v1 and v2 login code side by side. Circle every difference. Then open the teachers table in both versions. What would a leak give away in each?
  6. 85 to 110 Groups of 3 pick an app to design: a canteen pre-order, a library, or a sign-up for something else. They list every field and why. Then they cross out one.
  7. 110 to 120 Pack up. Design sheets go in the club folder.

Check: 10 + 30 + 15 + 10 + 20 + 25 + 10 = 120 minutes.

Session 3: Build the data for your app

  1. 0 to 10 Arrive. Get your design sheet.
  2. 10 to 45 In SQL Online IDE, each group writes CREATE TABLE for their design. They add 5 fake rows, and write 2 queries. One must be a JOIN.
  3. 45 to 60 Each group writes 4 data rules for their app, using mine as a model.
  4. 60 to 70 Break.
  5. 70 to 95 Gallery walk. Groups run another group's SQL and read their rules. They leave 2 sticky notes: one thing that works, one rule that was broken.
  6. 95 to 110 Fix one thing from your sticky notes.
  7. 110 to 120 Exit ticket: tell the story of one sign-up in 4 steps. Pack up.

Check: 10 + 35 + 15 + 10 + 25 + 15 + 10 = 120 minutes.

Data rules, and how I check them

These go up in session 1, before anyone types a name.

  1. Fake names only. First names from the list I hand out, or made up. Never a classmate. Check: I read every INSERT in the group's SQL in the class doc.
  2. No passwords in any table. If an app needs logins, the column is called pw_hash, and the sample rows say "hash". Check: I search their SQL for "password".
  3. Every column has a reason. One line each on the design sheet, and one field crossed out. Check: I hold the sheet next to their CREATE TABLE. A column with no reason gets a sticky note from me.
  4. Nothing that finds a person. No phone, email, address, NRIC or photo. Check: I read their CREATE TABLE. These words should not be in it.

And one behaviour rule for session 2. The ' OR 1=1 -- trick is only for my version 1 page. Trying it on any other site is not a joke. I told our IT team before the session, too.

How I mark it

Each success criterion gets "yes", "nearly" or "not yet", with one comment. Rule breaks are "not yet" until fixed. A group that breaks a data rule fixes it before the gallery walk ends.

For different students

  • New to code: they work with a partner who has done Python. In session 3 they own the design sheet.
  • Done early: add a teachers table with pw_hash, and write the JOIN that shows each teacher's sessions.
  • Struggling with JOIN: draw the two tables on paper, and connect matching ids with a pencil line first.
