Stall Board: running it with your class

For a Sec 3 class of 32 in 8 teams of 4. Students are 14 or 15. Three lessons of 60 minutes. Each student needs their school learning device and a browser.

Before lesson 1 (teacher)

  1. Make your template from the template page. Copy it once per team.
  2. On each copy: Pages source GitHub Actions, the ruleset on main, 4 collaborators, 5 issues.
  3. Students sign up for GitHub at home, with a parent's okay. Everyone must be 13 or older. Check your school's rules on student accounts too.
  4. Account names must not be real names. A colour and an animal works.
  5. Test that github.com and github.dev open on a student device at school. Some networks block them.

Team rules

  1. Main is locked. Every change goes on a branch and through a pull request. The ruleset enforces it.
  2. One task, one branch. Name it after the task, like add-drinks. Delete it after the merge.
  3. Someone else reviews. Open the page in the preview before you approve. You can't approve your own.
  4. Green before merge. The author merges, after the check passes and a teammate approves.
  5. Nothing personal, nothing secret. No real names, emails, photos, class numbers, keys or passwords. The check looks for some of these. You look for the rest.

When the check goes red

  1. Open the pull request. Click the red check to open its log.
  2. Find the first line that starts with FAIL. It names the file.
  3. Fix that one thing on the same branch. Commit. The check runs again.
  4. Still red after two tries? Ask your team, then me.

If a real key gets committed

Tell me straight away. Deleting the line is not enough, because Git keeps old commits. The key has to be cancelled where it was made.

Assessment

  • Your merged pull request, with a clear title and checklist.
  • One review you gave, with a reason.
  • Your team's live site works on a phone.
