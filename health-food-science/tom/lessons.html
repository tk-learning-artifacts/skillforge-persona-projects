The curriculum

Four lessons

Four 60-minute lessons for a Sec 2 FCE class, then a two-week campaign in the canteen.

  • Class: 36 Sec 2 students, in 9 groups of 4.
  • Room: the food room, with 3 sinks and 9 benches.
  • Time: four 60-minute lessons, one a week. The campaign runs in weeks 5 and 6.
  • Devices: one Chromebook per group for the drink checker. The paper table on the student sheet works too.
  • Before lesson 1: I collect 10 empty, rinsed drink packs that students buy. I ask the canteen vendor if students may count sales for two weeks.

Learning outcomes

By the end, each student can:

  1. Find sugar per 100 ml on a drink label and work out its Nutri-Grade.
  2. Turn the sugar in one pack into grams and teaspoons.
  3. Explain why a can of cola sinks in water and a diet cola floats.
  4. State two rules for drinks sold in school canteens.
  5. Plan one change to the canteen and say how they would measure it, and who it might leave out.

Syllabus links, from the 2024 Lower Secondary FCE syllabus: 2.3 on My Healthy Plate ("Choose water", "Limit intake of sugar"). 2.6 on budget, and section 6 on needs and wants. And 9.1, "Interpret information found on food and nutrition labels".

The evidence each lesson uses

  • Nutri-Grade cut-offs and label rules, from HealthHub.
  • The WHO limit of about 50 g of free sugars a day, from the WHO healthy diet fact sheet.
  • Singapore's sugar intake and its sources, from MOH's 2023 release on the National Nutrition Survey 2022.
  • The school drink rules, from HPB's Healthy Meals in Schools Programme.

Lesson 1: Read the drink

Outcomes 1 and 2. Materials: 10 empty drink packs, 9 kitchen scales, 3 clear plastic cups, 1 kg of sugar, teaspoons, sticky dots.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Bags away. Water bottles on the bench.
  • 5 to 15Pre-quiz on paper, alone, no help. I say it does not count for marks.
  • 15 to 22Vote. The 10 packs sit on the front bench. Each student puts a dot on the one they think has the most sugar.
  • 22 to 40Grade the drinks. Each group gets 1 or 2 packs. They find sugar and saturated fat per 100 ml, and the pack size. They grade it with the checker and fill the table on the sheet.
  • 40 to 52Sugar line-up. Three groups weigh the sugar in one pack each into a clear cup. The other groups check the sums. Cups are labelled with the drink and the grams.
  • 52 to 57Compare the vote with the results. Which drink surprised you? Why do you think it did?
  • 57 to 60Pack up. The sugar goes back into a sealed, labelled jar for lesson 2.

Check: 5 + 10 + 7 + 18 + 12 + 5 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 2: The sinking can

Outcome 3. Materials and safety are on the demo page.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Three safety rules on the board: no tasting, cans stay sealed, wipe spills at once.
  • 5 to 10Predict. Students draw where each can will sit in the tank, and write why.
  • 10 to 25Test. Three tanks, three groups at each. Lower each can in gently, three times. Then weigh each can.
  • 25 to 35Share. Groups put their masses on the board. We find the mean difference and compare it with the sugar on the label. Why is it smaller? Then we weigh the label's sugar into a cup.
  • 35 to 48Explain. Mini-lesson on density, with the diagram. Students write a three-sentence explanation on the sheet.
  • 48 to 55Discuss. Run both cans through the checker. Neither may be sold in a school canteen. Why not?
  • 55 to 60Tidy. Two people per tank. Floor wiped. Cans back to me, still sealed.

Check: 5 + 5 + 15 + 10 + 13 + 7 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 3: Who sets the choice?

Outcomes 4 and 5. Materials: a card of the national numbers, a card of the school drink rules, the canteen price board and stock list (photographed with the vendor's OK), and the shop prices students collected as homework.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Hand back lesson 2 sheets with one comment each.
  • 5 to 17The national picture. 56 g a day, 52% from drinks, and median sugar in packs down from 7.1% to 4.6%. Question: what changed the shelf?
  • 17 to 32Canteen check. Groups check the drink stall list against the rules card. Is every drink 330 ml or less? Any sweeteners? Is at least 10% calorie-free, like water?
  • 32 to 47Price map. Groups work out cents per 100 ml for water and two sweet drinks. They compare the stall, the vending machine and the minimart outside. Where is water cheapest? Where is it dearest?
  • 47 to 55Who is left out? A canteen change only reaches students who buy in the canteen. Who buys elsewhere, and why?
  • 55 to 60Exit ticket: one thing the canteen could change, and one person it would not help.

Check: 5 + 12 + 15 + 15 + 8 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 4: Plan the nudge

Outcome 5, and the post-quiz.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Read out three exit tickets from lesson 3.
  • 5 to 30Plan. Each group fills a one-page plan: the change, the evidence for it, what the vendor gains or loses, how we measure it, and who it leaves out.
  • 30 to 45Pitch. 90 seconds per group. One question from the class.
  • 45 to 48Vote. One vote each, not for your own group.
  • 48 to 58Post-quiz. Same 6 questions, new drinks in questions 1 and 2.
  • 58 to 60Pack up.

Check: 5 + 25 + 15 + 3 + 10 + 2 = 60 minutes.

Weeks 5 and 6: the campaign

The winning plan was the cheapest one. Move bottled water to eye level at the front of the fridge. Put the sweet drinks on the lower shelves. Add student-made shelf tags that show teaspoons of sugar per pack. The vendor agreed because it cost nothing and water still sells.

  1. Week 4, before the change: the vendor gives us the daily count of each drink sold. Students add them up by type.
  2. Week 5: the fridge changes on Monday. Students check the tags each morning.
  3. Week 6: we count again for five school days, the same way.
  4. Three students also run a short survey in form class: where do you buy drinks on most days?

The quiz

The same six questions before and after. Each is worth 1 mark.

  1. A drink has 6 g sugar and 0.5 g saturated fat per 100 ml. No sweetener. What is its Nutri-Grade? (C. Sugar alone puts it over 5 g.)
  2. A 250 ml pack has 10 g sugar per 100 ml. How many teaspoons of 5 g is that? (25 g, so 5 teaspoons.)
  3. Which Nutri-Grades must show the label on the pack? (C and D.)
  4. Why does a sealed can of cola sink in water when a diet cola floats? (The cola has much more dissolved sugar. Same size can, more mass, so it is denser than water.)
  5. State one rule for drinks sold in school canteens. (Any one: 330 ml or less, no sweeteners, no toppings, at least 10% calorie-free.)
  6. Name one group of students a canteen-only change would not reach, and why. (Any group with a reason, such as students who buy drinks outside school.)

For different students

  • Weaker at sums: a teaspoon strip. Ten 5 g spoons drawn in a row, so 25 g is counted, not divided.
  • Finished early: find a drink where saturated fat, not sugar, sets the grade. Milk teas are a good hunt.
  • Students fasting, or who don't buy drinks: they take the vendor and price roles. No one has to share what they buy.
  • Group roles rotate each lesson: reader, checker, recorder, safety lead.

Safety and care

  • No tasting in any lesson. Every food item stays in the food room.
  • The demo has its own safety steps.
  • Nobody is asked what they or their family eat. The survey asks only where students buy drinks, with no names.
  • We talk about drinks, not bodies. No weighing people, and no "good" or "bad" students.
