Health and Food Science: end project

Water First

A four-lesson programme for one Sec 2 class. Students read drink labels and test sugar with a sinking can. They check the canteen against the school rules. Then they run a two-week campaign to make water the easy choice.

I'm Tom. I teach sustainability and social topics through projects. Most health lessons end with "make better choices". I wanted students to ask a harder question too. Who sets the choices in the first place? The drink fridge, the price list and the shop outside the gate all decide what is easy.

The audience is one Sec 2 Food and Consumer Education (FCE) class of 36 students. We met for four 60-minute lessons in the food room, then ran the campaign in the canteen for two weeks.

Why drinks

The numbers come from Singapore's own surveys. They are about adults, but our students are the next adults.

56 g

Average sugar a Singapore adult ate each day in 2022. It was 60 g in 2018.

52%

Share of that sugar from sugar-sweetened drinks. They are the single biggest source.

50 g

WHO's limit for free sugars: under 10% of energy, or about 50 g a day. Under 5% is better still.

7.1% to 4.6%

Median sugar in packaged drinks here, from 2017 to 2023.

The last number is the one I start with. Sugar in packaged drinks fell as the rules changed what is on the shelf. MOH links this fall to the Nutri-Grade labels and the ban on grade D ads. Nobody asked people to find more willpower. That is a public health idea, and students can try it at school scale.

    The curriculum

    The full plan, with timings for each 60-minute lesson, is on the lessons page. Here is the shape of it.

    Learning outcomes

    By the end, each student can:

    1. Find sugar per 100 ml on a drink label and work out its Nutri-Grade.
    2. Turn the sugar in one pack into grams and teaspoons.
    3. Explain why a can of cola sinks in water and a diet cola floats.
    4. State two rules for drinks sold in school canteens.
    5. Plan one change to the canteen and say how they would measure it, and who it might leave out.

    Outcomes 1, 2 and 4 come from the 2024 Lower Secondary FCE syllabus. It asks students to explain "Choose water" and "Limit intake of sugar" from My Healthy Plate. It also asks them to interpret food labels. Outcome 5 links to its parts on budget and needs and wants.

    The four lessons

    • Lesson 1Read the drink. Pre-quiz. Students grade 10 real packs with the checker and spoon out the sugar of 3 of them.
    • Lesson 2The sinking can. A fair test: cola and diet cola in a tank of water. Then weigh them. Why does sugar make the can sink?
    • Lesson 3Who sets the choice? Read the national numbers. Check our canteen against the Healthy Meals in Schools rules. Map prices at the shops near school.
    • Lesson 4Plan the nudge. Groups pitch one change. The class votes. Post-quiz.
    • Weeks 4 to 6Run it. Students count drink sales for a week. Then the winning plan runs for two weeks, with the canteen vendor's agreement, and they count again.

    The food science demo

    Two sealed cans go into a tank of water. Same brand, same size. One sinks and one floats. Students then weigh both cans, and spoon out the sugar the label says is in the cola. They see the sugar before they read about it. The demo page has the method, the diagram, the safety steps and our results.

    Safety in one line each

    • No tasting. Cans stay sealed. No drinks or food leave the food room.
    • No glass. The tank is plastic. Two people carry it, and only when it is empty.
    • Spills get wiped at once. A wet floor near the sink is the real hazard.

    Assessment results

    I used three measures. A 6-question quiz before and after. A count of drink sales at the stall for one week before and one week during the campaign. And a short survey on where students buy drinks. The results page has all of it.

    How it meets the rubric

    • Accurate, current content. Every rule comes from HPB, MOH or WHO pages I checked in October 2026. The school rules are HPB's guidelines dated 30 July 2026. Each source is linked below.
    • Clear learning outcomes. Five outcomes, each tested by at least one quiz question or the campaign plan.
    • Safe, well-managed activities. No tasting in the demo, sealed cans, no glass, and a named job for spills. The canteen work only went ahead with the vendor's and the school's agreement.
    • Suits the audience. Sec 2 students buy their own drinks with their own money. So the lessons use their drinks, their canteen and their prices.

    What I would change

    The campaign moved water from 15% to 22% of stall sales. That looks good, but I can't tell how much is real. There was no second class or school to compare with. Some students may just have bought their sweet drink at the minimart instead. Fourteen students already do that most days. Next time I would also count for a week after the campaign ends. And I would ask a nearby class to keep a drink diary, as a comparison.

    I would also cut lesson 3. It tries to do the national data, the canteen rules and the price map in one hour. The price map needs its own lesson.
