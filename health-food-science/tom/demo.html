The food science demo

The sinking can

Two sealed cans of the same size go into a tank of water. The cola sinks. The diet cola floats. The difference is the sugar.

I based this on the Royal Society of Chemistry's Heavy sugar investigation, which is written for ages 9 to 11. For Sec 2, I add weighing, a mean, and a link to the label and the school rules.

You need

For each of three stations:

  • A clear plastic tank or tub, about 30 cm deep, two-thirds full of tap water.
  • One sealed can of cola and one sealed can of diet cola. Same brand, same size.
  • A kitchen scale that reads to 1 g.
  • Paper towels, and one mop for the room.

For the class: the jar of sugar from lesson 1, a teaspoon, a clear cup, and the student sheet.

Test your own cans the day before. Can sizes and recipes differ by brand and country, so check that yours really do sink and float.

Safety steps

  • No tasting. Cans stay sealed the whole lesson. Any can that is dented, bulging or leaking goes back to me.
  • No glass anywhere. Plastic tanks and cups only.
  • Fill and empty the tank at the sink. Carry it only when empty, and with two people.
  • Keep the scales and Chromebooks on a separate dry bench, away from the tanks.
  • Wipe spills at once. Each group's safety lead owns the paper towels.
  • The sugar in the cup is for looking at. It goes back into its sealed jar, and nobody eats it.

Method

  1. Predict. Draw where each can will end up. Write one reason.
  2. Read both labels. Write the pack size and the sugar per 100 ml for each.
  3. Lower the diet cola into the water, gently, on its side. Let go. Wait 10 seconds. Record float or sink.
  4. Take it out. Do the same with the cola.
  5. Repeat both twice more. Three trials each.
  6. Dry each can and weigh it. Write the mass in grams.
  7. Work out the difference in mass. Put it on the class board.

Making it a fair test

  • Change one thing: the drink, cola or diet cola.
  • Keep the same: can size and brand, the water, the tank, how you lower the can.
  • Measure: float or sink, and the mass of each can.

Why it works

A thing floats if it is less dense than the water around it. It sinks if it is denser. Density is how much mass is packed into a given space.

Both cans take up the same space. Both have the same metal and the same pocket of gas at the top. The cola also has a lot of dissolved sugar. The diet cola uses a sweetener so strong that a tiny amount does the job. So the cola can has more mass in the same space. That pushes it past the density of water, and it sinks.

The scale holds a surprise. Multiply the sugar per 100 ml by the pack size, and you get the sugar in the can. The cola can is heavier, but by much less than that. Why? Both cans hold the same 320 ml of drink. Sugar takes up room when it dissolves, so the cola can holds less water. The mass difference is the sugar minus the water it pushed out. Strong Sec 2 students can work this out. I give the rest the answer and ask them to explain it back.

The mean difference was 13 g. Our cola label said 10.6 g of sugar per 100 ml in a 320 ml can. That is about 34 g of sugar. Students expected the cans to be 34 g apart, not 13 g. That gap started the best talk of the lesson, about sugar taking up space. Then we spooned 34 g of sugar into a clear cup and put it next to the cans. Nobody said anything for a few seconds. Then someone asked how many of those they drink in a week.

The twist: neither can is allowed in school

Run both cans through the drink checker. The cola is grade D, so it must carry the label and cannot be advertised. The diet cola gets B, since a sweetener rules out A. But HPB's school guidelines ban sweeteners in school drinks, to help students get used to less sweet tastes. So neither can may be sold in a school canteen.

This is the part students argue about. Some say the diet cola should be allowed, since it has no sugar. Others say the rule is right. I let them argue, then ask: who decides what is normal to drink?

Questions for students

  1. Why must both cans be the same size and brand?
  2. The cola sank every time. Why do three trials anyway?
  3. Would a can of plain soda water float or sink? Test your prediction.
  4. The cola has 34 g of sugar but is only 13 g heavier. Explain the gap.
  5. What does this demo not tell you about how healthy a drink is?
