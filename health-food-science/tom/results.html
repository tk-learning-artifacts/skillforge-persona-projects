Did they learn it?

Three measures: a quiz before and after, drink sales at the stall, and a survey on where students buy drinks.

My class, the vendor and every number on this page are made up, like me. The quiz questions are real and are on the lessons page. Use the layout to report your own class.

What I read in it

  • Q2 was already fair before we began. Students can divide by 5. The hard part was finding the right number on the label.
  • Q4 jumped the most. Seeing a can sink, then weighing it, did more than any slide.
  • Q6 grew the least. Many wrote "people who don't care" instead of a group with a reason. That tells me lesson 3 went too fast. The price map got 15 minutes, and it needs a lesson.
  • A pre and post quiz in the same month shows recall. It does not show whether anyone drinks less sugar three months later.

Total sales hardly moved, so the vendor lost nothing. That matters. A change that costs the vendor money will not last past our two weeks.

What the count cannot tell us: whether the same students switched, or different students bought water. Whether it lasts. And whether anyone just bought their sweet drink after school instead. There was also no comparison stall, so a hot week alone could explain some of it.

3. Where students buy drinks

Three students asked one question in our form class, with no names: where do you buy a drink on most school days?

AnswerStudents
The school canteen17
A minimart or shop outside school14
I only drink what I bring from home5

Fourteen of 36 students buy outside school on most days. Our fridge change never reached them. Some told the surveyors the minimart is on the way to the bus stop. Their CCA ends after the canteen closes. That is the next campaign, and it is harder. We cannot move a shop's fridge.

What the three measures show together

  • Students learnt to read a drink label and grade it. The quiz shows that well.
  • A free change to the fridge shifted some sales toward water, with no cost to the vendor.
  • The change only reached the students who buy in the canteen. About half do not.
