What I read in it

  • Questions 3 and 7 moved most. Question 3 is one number to learn. Question 7 needs an idea. Before, 20 pupils chose "germs on the apple". After the fair test, most chose air. Doing it beat telling it.
  • Question 4 is the weakest after. It asks for two numbers, and numbers fade. I would test the habit instead.
  • Question 8 was lower than I hoped. Seven pupils still chose "everything we could". They liked testing many things at once. I would add a second, quicker fair test so they see a muddled one fail.
  • The quiz was the morning after. It shows what pupils remembered for a day, not for a term.

The handwash checklist

An adult watched every pupil wash and ticked four things: soap, 20 seconds of rubbing, backs and between fingers, and drying with a paper towel. A full wash means all four.

Full wash, all four13 of 3227 of 31

Twenty seconds was the hard one. Most pupils stopped at about 8. What fixed it was the partner with the sand timer, and the handwash checker job in session 4. Pupils hold each other to a rule more firmly than they hold themselves.

How pupils felt

The exit ticket had three faces: "I could do this", "I'm not sure" and "I'm worried". The question was the same each time: how do you feel about making food at home, with an adult?

I watch this measure most closely. A pupil who knows the danger zone but is scared of the kitchen will not use it. Two pupils were still worried at the end. One told the trainer she was afraid of knives. Next time, she gets the banana job in the first five minutes, with me beside her.

What the three measures show together

  • Pupils can name the 4Cs and plan a breakfast from My Healthy Plate. The quiz shows that.
  • The handwash habit changed, and an adult saw it change. That is stronger than a quiz answer.
  • More pupils felt ready to make food at home. I can't tell if they did. A note home asking parents would tell me.
