Back to Breakfast Cups

The four sessions

Four 60-minute sessions for 32 P5 pupils in 8 groups of 4. The questions in plum are the ones I ask, word for word.

Routines for every session

  • The stop signal is a hand up and a bell. Pupils stop, look and put food down.
  • Groups sit at the same bench each week. Each group has a tray, and the tray holds everything.
  • Nobody is asked what they ate at home. Pupils can share, but they never have to.
  • We talk about food, never bodies. No food is "bad". Some foods are "every day" and some are "sometimes".

Session 1: What's in a good breakfast?

Outcome 1. Materials: pre-quiz sheets, 8 paper plate mats split into quarters and a half, 8 sets of 12 picture cards of breakfast foods.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Introduce the stop signal and the trays.
  • 5 to 17Pre-quiz. I read each question aloud twice. Pupils circle answers alone. I tell them it is to see what they know already, and that "I don't know" is a fine answer.
  • 17 to 27Think, pair, share. "What is a breakfast that keeps you going until recess? Why do you think so?"
  • 27 to 45Plate sort. Groups place cards like kaya toast, wholemeal bread, bee hoon, egg, banana, plain yoghurt and soya milk on the mat. "Which part of the plate is still empty? What could fill it?"
  • 45 to 55Design a breakfast cup on paper: oats, yoghurt and fruit. Label which part is the wholegrain, the protein and the fruit.
  • 55 to 60Exit ticket: "One food I could add to my breakfast." Allergy letter reminder.

Check: 5 + 12 + 10 + 18 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Session 2: Why do apples go brown?

Outcome 3. All materials and safety steps are on the demo page. The apples are never eaten.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Three rules on the board: no tasting, only adults use knives, wash hands after.
  • 5 to 12Predict. "You cut an apple for your lunch box. By recess it has gone brown. What do you think happened?" Pupils write their idea.
  • 12 to 20Plan the fair test. "If we want to know if lemon juice helps, what must stay the same?" We list the same apple, the same size, the same plate, the same room.
  • 20 to 25The adult cuts 8 apples at the front bench. Each group gets 4 slices on a labelled plate.
  • 25 to 30Treat the slices. Dip one in water and one in lemon juice for 30 seconds. Wrap one in cling film. Leave one alone. Record at 0 minutes.
  • 30 to 40While we wait: the colour scale. Pupils practise scoring the picture strip from 0 to 3, so everyone scores the same way.
  • 40 to 42Score the slices at 10 minutes.
  • 42 to 50Explain. Air meets something in the apple and it turns brown. Lemon juice gets in the way. "Is the brown apple unsafe to eat? Is a white one safe?"
  • 50 to 52Score the slices at 20 minutes.
  • 52 to 57Conclude. Each pupil writes: which slice stayed whitest, and the one thing we changed.
  • 57 to 60Apples in the bin. Adults collect plates. Pupils wipe benches and wash hands.

Check: 5 + 7 + 8 + 5 + 5 + 10 + 2 + 8 + 2 + 5 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Session 3: The 4Cs

Outcomes 2 and 4. Materials: 4Cs posters, 4 sand timers, handwash checklists, and 6 planted hazards in the room's food corner. Print the job cards and checklist.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Recall last week. "What did the brown apple tell us about germs?"
  • 5 to 15The 4Cs. One poster for each, with one question. "Germs grow fastest between 5°C and 60°C. Where in this room is that warm?"
  • 15 to 30Handwash check. Groups go to the sinks in turn. A partner turns a sand timer or counts to 20 slowly. An adult ticks the checklist: soap, 20 seconds, backs and between fingers, dried with a paper towel.
  • 30 to 45Hazard hunt. Groups walk the food corner with the checklist and find the six planted hazards. Then they say which C each one breaks.
  • 45 to 55Jobs for session 4. Each group picks a timer keeper, a cooler keeper, a wiper and a handwash checker. They read their card and practise once.
  • 55 to 60Exit ticket: "My job next week is… The C it protects is…"

Check: 5 + 10 + 15 + 15 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

The six planted hazards

  1. A cup of yoghurt left on the windowsill in the sun. (Control temperature)
  2. A cooler box thermometer showing 12°C. (Control temperature)
  3. A fruit board with a paper label saying "raw chicken". (Counter cross-contamination)
  4. A damp, dirty cloth on the bench. (Clean)
  5. A drawing of a cook with long hair loose over the bowl. (Clean)
  6. A card that says "egg sandwich: egg still runny inside". (Cook well)

Session 4: Make it, eat it, clean up

Outcomes 1, 2 and 4. Before pupils arrive, an adult packs the yoghurt into the cooler box with ice packs. The thermometer must read below 5°C.

  • 0 to 5Settle. Aprons on. Long hair tied back. Jobs cards on.
  • 5 to 12Handwash, two groups per sink pair. Handwash checkers watch and tick.
  • 12 to 17Recap the 4Cs. The timer keepers write the time on their clipboard. "When must every cup be eaten by?"
  • 17 to 35Make. Pupils cut banana with plastic knives. The adult cuts apple and dips it in lemon juice. The cooler keeper hands out yoghurt only when the group is ready. Pupils layer oats, yoghurt and fruit in a cup.
  • 35 to 45Eat at the benches, with water. "Point to your wholegrain. Your protein. Your fruit. What would you change at home?"
  • 45 to 55Clean up. Wipers wipe. Adults wash knives. Pupils wash cups and spoons at the sinks, then their hands.
  • 55 to 60Exit ticket: three faces. "How do you feel about making food at home, with an adult?"

Check: 5 + 7 + 5 + 18 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes. Post-quiz: 15 minutes the next morning.

Yoghurt is out of the cooler for under 30 minutes. SFA's limit for ready-to-eat food in the danger zone is four hours. Our class rule is much stricter, because the habit matters more than the number.

For different pupils

  • Reading load: the quiz and every instruction are read aloud. Job cards have a drawing for each step.
  • A pupil who finds the sinks crowded or loud can wash first, before the class arrives at the sinks.
  • A pupil with an allergy makes the same cup with the swap their parent agreed. They are never the only one doing something different: I make one too.
  • Finished early: write the instructions for a younger sibling, in 4 steps.
