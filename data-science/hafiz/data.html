  • change-notes.txt: the notes below, as plain text.

Change notesWhat I changed, and why

I write a change note whenever I touch the data. Then anyone can check my work, or undo a choice they disagree with.

  1. Kept the raw files. I downloaded the four files on 24 October 2026. They sit in data/raw unchanged.
  2. Joined and renamed. I joined the three NEA files on the month column. I renamed total_rainfall to total_mm, maximum_rainfall_in_a_day to wettest_day_mm, and no_of_rainy_days to rain_days. Short names with units make the code easier to read.
  3. Checked for mess. No blanks. No repeated months. No month whose wettest day beats its own total. No month with more than 31 rain days. Nothing to fix, but now I know.
  4. Dropped 2026. It has only 9 months so far. A part year would look like a very dry year.
  5. Dropped 1982 and 1983. My yearly sums did not match SingStat for these two years. Totals were 167.9 mm and 87.1 mm lower than SingStat's. SingStat's footnote says its data come from Changi only from January 1984. I can't tell which station SingStat used before that. From 1984 on, both sources agree, so I start there.
  6. Left 2015 alone. My 2015 total is 1267.1 mm. SingStat says 1266.8 mm. The wettest day matches. I could not find the reason for a 0.3 mm gap. It is too small to change any result, so I kept NEA's monthly figures.
  7. Built the yearly table. For each year, total_mm is the sum of the months. wettest_day_mm is the biggest monthly maximum. rain_days is the sum. mm_per_rain_day is the total divided by rain days. Two checks passed. My 1991 to 2020 average is 2113.3 mm, the same as MSS publishes. March 2025 is 550.1 mm, the same as NEA's 2025 report.

ColumnsWhat each column means

ColumnMeaningUnit
monthYear and month, like 1984-02YYYY-MM
year, month_numThe same date, split for groupingnumber
total_mmAll the rain in that month or yearmm
wettest_day_mmRain on the single wettest daymm
rain_daysDays with 0.2 mm of rain or more, NEA's ruledays
mm_per_rain_dayTotal divided by rain days (yearly file only)mm per day

NEA counts a rain day at 0.2 mm or more at one station. MSS's report counts a wet day at 1 mm or more, averaged across stations. Same words, different rules. Check the definition before you compare two numbers.
