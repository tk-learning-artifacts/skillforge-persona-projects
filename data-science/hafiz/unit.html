Deliverable · 4-lesson plan with privacy check

Test a climate claim with open data

A 4-lesson unit for Sec 3. Pairs take a claim from the news and turn it into a question. They answer it with NEA's Changi rainfall files in Colab.

  • Class: 36 Sec 3 students, working in 18 pairs.
  • Time: 4 lessons of 60 minutes, one a week.
  • Devices: one laptop per pair, signed in to a school Google account.
  • Tools: Google Colab with pandas, and a Google Form for one anonymous poll.
  • Starter dataset: the three NEA monthly rainfall files for Changi on data.gov.sg, and my starter notebook.
  • Maths link: mean, median, quartiles, interquartile range and box-and-whisker plots. Quartiles and box plots sit in the Sec 3/4 part of MOE's G3 Mathematics syllabus.

GoalsBy the end, students can

  • Turn a claim from the news into a question one dataset can answer.
  • Load, check and clean a CSV file in pandas, and write change notes.
  • Make a fair chart, spot a misleading one, and say what the trick is.
  • Write a claim that matches the evidence, with one limit.

Before lesson 1Teacher checklist

  • Ask the school's ICT team if Colab is on for student accounts. An admin can turn it on or off for a group.
  • Run the starter notebook on a student account. Time the first run.
  • Build the prediction poll and run the privacy check below.
  • Print 18 question cards and 18 claim cards, one each per pair.

Lesson 1 · 60 minFrom a claim to a question

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Students open the prediction poll on the board's QR code. Two questions, no names.
  2. 5 to 15Show NEA's 2025 climate headline: rainfall up 92.5 mm per decade, more rain on wet days. Ask: what exactly is claimed? Where was it measured? Over which years?
  3. 15 to 25Mini lesson. A question data can answer names one place, one measure and fixed years. Show my question about Changi's wettest day.
  4. 25 to 45Pairs preview the three NEA files on data.gov.sg. Each pair writes its own question on a question card. Examples: are rain days going up? Is December always the wettest month?
  5. 45 to 55Swap cards with the next pair. Check the question against the three-part test. Rewrite if needed.
  6. 55 to 60Show the poll as totals only. Collect question cards. Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 10 + 20 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 2 · 60 minLoad, check and clean in Colab

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Sign in. Open the starter notebook from the class page and save a copy.
  2. 5 to 15Live demo of cells 1 to 3: read_csv, merge, and the four checks. Students type along.
  3. 15 to 40Pairs run the checks. They must find the part year and the SingStat mismatch. They write each change in a text cell called Change notes.
  4. 40 to 50Error clinic. Pairs read their error from the bottom line up. Most common: a misspelt file name or column name.
  5. 50 to 57Name the notebook with the pair code only, like 3E-P07. Share it with the teacher only.
  6. 57 to 60Exit ticket: one change you made, and why.

Check: 5 + 10 + 25 + 10 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 3 · 60 minSummarise and chart

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Reopen notebooks.
  2. 5 to 15Mini lesson: groupby and agg, then describe. Read one box plot together: median, quartiles, whiskers.
  3. 15 to 35Pairs summarise the data for their question and make one fair chart. They tick the chart checklist below.
  4. 35 to 45Make a misleading copy on purpose: a cherry-picked start year or a cut axis. Write a one-line trick caption.
  5. 45 to 55Swap laptops with another pair. Spot the trick in theirs. Check their fair chart against the checklist.
  6. 55 to 60Fix one thing the other pair found. Save.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 4 · 60 minClaim, evidence, limit

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Hand out claim cards.
  2. 5 to 12Model my claim card: the claim, two numbers, one chart, one limit, one thing I cannot claim.
  3. 12 to 32Pairs fill their claim card and run the shuffle test if their question is about a trend.
  4. 32 to 50Gallery walk. Nine pairs stay with their laptops and present. Nine pairs visit and score with the rubric. Swap at minute 41.
  5. 50 to 56Show the lesson 1 poll again. Did the evidence change anyone's mind? Why or why not?
  6. 56 to 60Delete the poll responses together, on the board. Pack up.

Check: 5 + 7 + 20 + 18 + 6 + 4 = 60 minutes.

Privacy checkStudents' data in this unit

The rainfall data is about weather, not people. The only data about students is the poll and their notebooks. I check both before lesson 1.

QuestionMy answer for this unit
What do we collect?Two poll answers: "Has Singapore's rain got heavier since the 1980s?" (yes, no, not sure) and "How sure are you?" (1 to 5). Nothing else.
Can anyone be identified?No name, class, index number or email question. In the form's Settings, under Responses, "Collect email addresses" is not set to Verified or Responder input. "Limit to 1 response" stays off, because it makes people sign in.
Who sees what?Only I see the raw responses. The class sees totals on the board.
Where do notebooks live?In each student's school Drive. Named by pair code. Shared with me only, never "anyone with the link".
Who could be harmed?Low risk. A student could feel silly about a wrong guess, so we never show who said what.
When is it deleted?Poll responses at the end of lesson 4, on the board. Notebooks stay with students. I delete my copies at the end of term.
Accounts and ageSchool-managed Google accounts only. No personal accounts, no sign-ups to other sites.
School rulesI check the school's own data rules and follow those where they differ from mine.

A pair that wants to run its own survey must pass this same check with me first.

Chart checklistBefore a chart leaves your notebook

  • The title says what, where and when.
  • Both axes have labels, and the y-axis has a unit, like mm.
  • A bar chart's y-axis starts at zero.
  • The years shown are all the years, or you say why not.
  • A source line names who collected the data.
  • One y-axis only. No 3D.
  • Colour is not the only clue. Labels say what each mark is.
  • A one-sentence caption says what the reader should notice.

SupportFor different students

  • Stuck on code: a copy of the notebook with blanks to fill, not empty cells.
  • Finished early: add NEA's daily Admiralty file for 2009 to 2017. Does it agree with Changi?
  • Code is a wall: do the same summary in Google Sheets with a pivot table. The thinking is the same.
  • Adult learners: I run the same unit in two 3-hour evening sessions. They argue more about the MSS claim, which is good.
