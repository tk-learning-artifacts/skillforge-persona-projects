How do we get to school?

A 4-lesson data project for a P6 class after PSLE. Pupils survey their own class on paper and clean the data. Then they chart it and compare with Singapore.

  • Class: 30 P6 pupils with a wide range of learning needs, in 6 groups of 5.
  • Time: 4 lessons of 60 minutes, two a week, in the weeks after PSLE.
  • Devices: my laptop and the projector. Pupils do not use accounts or devices.
  • Starter data: the survey slip for our own class. The made-up practice survey is on the spreadsheet page. The two census charts are on the project page.
  • Materials: printed slips, a sealed box, grid paper, number cards, sticky dots, a visual timer.
  • Maths link: bar graphs (P3), percent (P5) and average (P6).

By the end, pupils can

  • Turn "getting to school" into a question our class can answer.
  • Spot and fix mess in survey answers, and say what they changed.
  • Make a fair bar chart and spot a chart trick.
  • Say one claim with evidence, and one thing we can't claim.

Before lesson 1

  • Run the privacy check below. Check it against the school's own data rules.
  • Tell the form teacher what we will ask. Ask if any pupil may find the topic hard.
  • Print 30 slips and a few spares in large print.
  • Print the made-up practice survey, cut into 6 strips of 5 rows.

Lesson 1: What do we want to know? 60 min

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Picture cards on the board: walking, car, public bus, school bus, MRT.
  2. 5 to 15Ask: next year, how will you get to secondary school? Pairs talk. I write worries on the board, with no names.
  3. 15 to 25Turn the topic into a question together. Test it: can our class answer it? Will the answer help? We agree on: "How do we get to school now, and how long does it take?"
  4. 25 to 35Privacy talk with the two-question card. Pupils name things we must not ask: name, block, street, MRT station. We add "prefer not to say".
  5. 35 to 45Pupils fill the slip at their own pace. I or a buddy read it aloud for anyone who wants that. Slips go in the sealed box. Anyone may skip.
  6. 45 to 55Predict: sketch what you think our class bar chart will look like.
  7. 55 to 60Pack up. After class, I type the slips into the sheet and shred them.

Check: 5 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 2: Clean and count 60 min

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Show the made-up survey on the projector. Say clearly: these are not real pupils.
  2. 5 to 15Spot the mess. Groups hunt for five kinds. Look for words in a number, a range and a blank. Find a number that can't be right, and a slip typed twice.
  3. 15 to 30Each group cleans its strip of 5 rows. For every fix, they write a change note on a sticky strip.
  4. 30 to 40Our real class sheet on the projector, minutes only. The class decides each fix. I type, and a pupil writes the change log on the board.
  5. 40 to 52Average and middle value. I type AVERAGE live. Pupils line up number cards from shortest to longest to find the middle value. Compare the two.
  6. 52 to 57Which number would you tell a new pupil, and why? Say it, write it or point to it.
  7. 57 to 60Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 12 + 5 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 3: Charts that tell the truth 60 min

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Show the class counts. Small groups are already merged into "Other or not said".
  2. 5 to 15Bar graph recap. Read the chart checklist together, one line at a time.
  3. 15 to 35Groups choose. They draw the bar chart on grid paper, or build it with me in Google Sheets.
  4. 35 to 45Spot the trick. I show our chart with the axis starting at 3. What looks different? Is it fair?
  5. 45 to 55Gallery. Each group checks another group's chart with the checklist, using sticky dots for ticks.
  6. 55 to 60Fix one thing. Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 4: Compare with Singapore, and share 60 min

  1. 0 to 5Settle. Hand back the predictions from lesson 1.
  2. 5 to 15Show census chart 1 as a chart, then a table, then in words. How is our class like Singapore? How is it different?
  3. 15 to 20Show census chart 2. What changes in secondary school?
  4. 20 to 40Show what you learned, your way. Make a poster with a chart and two sentences. Or give me a one-minute talk, or fill a claim card with sentence starters.
  5. 40 to 52Share in groups of three. Listeners give one star and one question.
  6. 52 to 56Whole class: what can't we claim? I write the list.
  7. 56 to 60I delete the class sheet on the projector. We keep only the totals chart. Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 5 + 20 + 12 + 4 + 4 = 60 minutes.

Claim card sentence starters:

  • Most of our class gets to school by ______.
  • In Singapore in 2020, most primary pupils ______.
  • In secondary school, more pupils ______.
  • One thing we can't say is ______.

Privacy check

No names, no accounts, slips shredded, small groups merged, data deleted after lesson 4.

The pupil card: two questions

1. Who could this hurt?

2. What are we asking that we don't need?

I keep it to two questions on purpose. The pupils who most need to think about it skip a full page of questions.

My check as the teacher

QuestionMy answer for this project
What do we collect?Two answers on a paper slip: how you usually travel, and about how many minutes. Nothing else.
Can anyone be identified?No name, class number, address or MRT station. But a group of one or two can point to a pupil. So any group under 5 is merged into "Other or not said" before the class sees it. I never say who has the longest trip.
Who could be harmed?How a child travels can hint at family money. The census itself splits school travel by household income. A pupil driven to school, or one who travels far, may feel judged. So: "prefer not to say", no names, and no talk about who wrote what.
Accounts and ageP6 pupils are about 12. Google, for example, sets 13 as the minimum age to manage your own account in most countries. So no forms or accounts for pupils. Paper only. I type the slips on my school laptop.
Who sees what?Only I see the typed sheet. The class sees counts, with small groups merged.
Can a pupil say no?Yes. Skipping the slip is fine. Nobody checks who handed one in.
When is it deleted?Slips are shredded the day I type them. The sheet is deleted at the end of lesson 4, on the projector. We keep only the totals chart.
School rulesI follow the school's data rules where they are stricter than mine.

Chart checklist

Pupils tick each line with a sticky dot. One idea per line, short words.

  • My chart has a title that says what it shows.
  • Each bar has a name.
  • The number line starts at 0.
  • The steps on the number line are even.
  • I wrote where the data came from.
  • You can tell the bars apart without colour.
  • One sentence says what the chart shows.

4-point rubric

I mark the learning, not the format. A poster, a talk and a claim card can all reach level 4.

For different learners

  • Reading: picture slips, large-print copies, and a buddy or me to read aloud.
  • Writing: pupils can say, point or draw their answers. Sentence starters for the claim card.
  • Pace: a visual timer on the board, and the next step always written up.
  • Attention: the number-card line and the gallery get pupils out of their seats.
  • Finished early: compare our class with census chart 2. Who might need to practise a new route?
  • Anxious about the topic: they can work only with the made-up survey and the census. No one has to fill in a slip.
