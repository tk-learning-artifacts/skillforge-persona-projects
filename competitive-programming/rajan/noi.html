Entering NOI // a note for the team

The National Olympiad in Informatics is Singapore's contest for secondary and JC students. Here is what you need to know to enter. I hand this out in session 4, and it prints on one A4 page.

Facts below are from the NOI 2026 pages. They were the latest in October 2026. Our team aims at NOI 2028. Check the competition page for that year's dates before you plan around them.

Who can enter

  • Any student at a local secondary school or junior college, whatever their nationality.
  • Some Singapore citizens and PRs abroad or homeschooled can also enter. The page lists the cases.
  • Any number of students from one school can sit the preliminary contest.
  • For the final, each school sends its top five per category, Secondary or JC. It also sends its top girl. Everyone needs a score above a cut-off set after the preliminary. So up to six per category.

Languages, and what Python cannot do

  • C++ is the main language. The organisers promise every task can be solved in C++.
  • Python 3 is allowed. In 2026 it was CPython 3.8.10.
  • The organisers do not promise a full Python solution exists for every task. Python gets no extra time or memory, so it can be too slow.
  • Template files come only in C++. So Python users can only try Batch tasks: read input, print output.
  • The judge runs Python 3.8, so newer features such as match will not work.
  • Interactive tasks cannot be done in Python. So Guess the Number is good binary search practice. NOI will not give you a task like it in Python.

How you get registered

  • You cannot register yourself. A teacher signs up on the NOI website and enrols every student.
  • It is free.
  • In 2026, registration closed on 14 February at 11.59 pm.
  • I will collect names at the end of Term 4. Then nothing is rushed in January.

What the contest looks like

  • Preliminary contest: online, 5 tasks in 5 hours. In 2026 it was Saturday 21 February, 1 pm to 6 pm.
  • The day before, contestants could practise on the contest system from home.
  • Final contest: 5 tasks in 5 hours, on site at NUS School of Computing. In 2026 it was Saturday 14 March.
  • Five hours is long. Before the real thing, you will sit at least one full past paper.

Where to practise

  • CodeBreaker's NOI group has past NOI contests to submit to. The NOI practice page recommends it. You can read problems without logging in.
  • Past NOI test data and solution notes are in the sg_noi_archive on GitHub. The practice page links to it.
  • Keep going on Kattis between sessions. Aim for problems rated 1.5 to 2.5.
