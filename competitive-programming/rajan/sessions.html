Four session plans

Four Thursdays, 60 minutes each, in the computer lab. 12 students from Sec 2 and Sec 3. Every timing below adds up to 60.

  • Each timeSettle, two verdict cards, a short idea, 26 to 28 minutes of solving, the debugging log.
  • MaterialsThe problem page on the projector. One paper debugging log per student, kept in a class folder.
  • Log columnsProblem, verdict, test number, cause, fix. One row for every submission that is not Accepted.
  • Hint ruleTry for 10 minutes before you open Hint 1. Then 5 minutes between hints. Write which hint you used in the log.

Before week 1 // 28 Jun to 7 Jul

  • Monday of Term 3 week 1: a consent letter goes home. It explains Kattis, its age rule and why a parent must accept the terms.
  • Students make their accounts at home, with a parent, using their real names.
  • By Wednesday 7 July, each student emails me their Kattis username. I check the list against the consent slips.
  • I book the lab and test that the school network does not block open.kattis.com.

Session 1: Judges and verdicts // 8 Jul

Problems: Quadrant Selection, R2, Spavanac.

By the end, students can read a problem page, submit, and say what a verdict means.

  1. 0 to 5 minSettle. Log in to Kattis. Logs out of the folder.
  2. 5 to 20 minDemo on the projector. I read Quadrant Selection top to bottom: limits, input, output, samples. Then I submit three times. First with x, y = map(int, input().split()), which crashes because x and y are on separate lines: Run Time Error. Then a version that prints "Quadrant 1": Wrong Answer. Then the right one: Accepted. Each time we read which test number failed.
  3. 20 to 25 minHow to fill the debugging log. I fill in my two failed submissions as the example rows.
  4. 25 to 50 minSolve the three problems, in order. I walk the room and ask one question: "What did you expect Kattis to say?"
  5. 50 to 57 minTwo students read one log row aloud. The class names the cause before they say it.
  6. 57 to 60 minExit line on the log: what is the difference between Wrong Answer and Run Time Error? Log off.

Check: 5 + 15 + 5 + 25 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Session 2: Sets, sorting and Big O // 15 Jul

Problems: No Duplicates, Secure Doors, Shopaholic.

By the end, students can say the Big O of their solution and check it against the limits.

  1. 0 to 5 minSettle and log in.
  2. 5 to 12 minVerdict cards 1 and 2, Spavanac and No Duplicates, from the verdict lab. Hands up for each verdict before I reveal it.
  3. 12 to 22 minBig O by counting. I time 1,000 lookups in a list and in a set of 100,000 numbers, live. The set wins by a mile. Then the rule of thumb from the USACO Guide: about 108 simple steps a second, and it calls that a conservative guess. Python is slower than C++. That is why USACO gives Python twice the time.
  4. 22 to 50 minSolve the three problems. Shopaholic has up to 200,000 prices, so a loop inside a loop will not do.
  5. 50 to 57 minIn the log, students write the biggest n from each statement and the Big O of their code. Do the two fit together?
  6. 57 to 60 minLog off and file the logs.

Check: 5 + 7 + 10 + 28 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Session 3: Brute force, recursion and binary search // 22 Jul

Problems: Tri, Perket, Guess the Number.

By the end, students can try every case when the space is small, and halve it when it is big.

  1. 0 to 5 minSettle and log in.
  2. 5 to 12 minVerdict cards 3 and 5: Shopaholic, then Tri. The Tri card is about floats. Most students guess wrong, which is the point.
  3. 12 to 24 minThree short boards. Tri: there are only 8 possible equations, so try them all. Perket: I draw the "in or out" tree for 3 ingredients. It has 8 leaves; 10 ingredients give 1,024. Guess: the class guesses my number from 1 to 1,000 with higher or lower. Someone always says "pick the middle". 210 is 1,024, so 10 guesses are enough.
  4. 24 to 52 minSolve the three problems. Guess is interactive: remind them about flush=True.
  5. 52 to 57 minPairs swap screens. Each reads the other's Perket function aloud and points to the line where it stops.
  6. 57 to 60 minLog off and file the logs.

Check: 5 + 7 + 12 + 28 + 5 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Session 4: Grids and BFS // 29 Jul

Problems: Knight Jump, Counting Stars, Grid.

By the end, students can run a BFS with a deque, by hand and in code.

  1. 0 to 5 minSettle and log in.
  2. 5 to 12 minVerdict cards 4 and 7: Perket, then Knight Jump. The second one previews today's trap.
  3. 12 to 24 minBFS on a 5 by 5 grid on the whiteboard. The queue is a row of sticky notes: add at the right, take from the left. One rule I repeat: mark a cell as seen when you add it, not when you take it out. Then the same thing in code with collections.deque and popleft().
  4. 24 to 50 minSolve the three problems. Counting Stars is the one most will finish. Grid is the stretch.
  5. 50 to 57 minHand out the NOI note. Each student picks 3 Kattis problems rated 1.5 to 2.5 for the holidays and writes them in the log.
  6. 57 to 60 minLast log rows. Log off.

Check: 5 + 7 + 12 + 26 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Verdict cards 6 and 8, Counting Stars and Grid, are homework. They are on the index page, so students can do them at home.

Strong and new coders in one room

  • Finished early: solve the same problem a second way, then compare the Big O. Or start next week's first problem.
  • Stuck: use the hint ladder in order. After Hint 3, a classmate may explain the idea in words, but never type or dictate code.
  • New to reading input: they start with Hello World and Solving for Carrots before Quadrant Selection.

Club rules

  1. No AI tools during sessions. The judge tests what you understand; a chatbot's code tests the chatbot.
  2. Never paste someone else's code. Kattis keeps every submission, and so does your log.
  3. Help in words, not keystrokes. Explaining an idea teaches you too.
  4. Every wrong submission gets a log row. A row with no cause is not finished.

How I know it worked

  • Each student has at least 8 of the 12 Accepted by the end of week 4.
  • Every log row names a cause, not only "fixed it".
  • The class gets at least 6 of the 8 verdict cards right by week 4.
