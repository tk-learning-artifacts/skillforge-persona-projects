Session plans

Four sessions, one clock

Wednesdays, 3.30 to 4.30 pm, in the robotics room. 16 students in 8 pairs, one laptop per pair. Each plan adds up to 60 minutes.

The format, every week

  • Huddle, 5 minutes. Today's goal and one rule.
  • Demo, 10 minutes. I solve problem A live, mistakes and all.
  • Training, 20 minutes. Pairs solve problem B. Hint cards on the ladder.
  • Sprint, 15 minutes. Problem C, nobody has seen it, the sprint board on the projector.
  • Debrief, 7 minutes. Two pairs show their code.
  • Pack up, 3 minutes. Laptops charged and back on the shelf.
  • Pairs swap the driver every 5 minutes. I call "swap" and the keyboard moves, mid-line or not.
  • I mix pairs by level. A strong coder never drives twice in a row.
  • Submit early. A red box on test 3 is information. A perfect program that never gets submitted scores 0.

Before week 1

  • First week of Term 1: a letter goes home about USACO and the NOI plan. Students make accounts with a parent's OK.
  • I ask IT to check that the 8 laptops run Python 3 and IDLE. They must also reach usaco.org.
  • I print the 12 hint cards from the ladder page, one set per pair.

Week 1: Simulate it 13 Jan

Problems: A The Lost Cow, B Speeding Ticket, sprint The Bucket List.

  1. 0 to 5Huddle. Goal: everyone's first green box. Rule: read the input and output lines twice.
  2. 5 to 15Demo. I draw Farmer John's zigzag for x = 3 and y = 6. Then I code it. I forget the file input on purpose. Most USACO problems from before December 2020 read from a file named in the statement. I submit and get the red boxes, then fix it.
  3. 15 to 35Training: Speeding Ticket in pairs. Swap every 5 minutes.
  4. 35 to 50Sprint: The Bucket List. 15 minutes on the board. I call the time left at 5 and 1.
  5. 50 to 57Debrief. First pair to 10 of 10 shows their code. Then one pair that hit a red box explains what it was.
  6. 57 to 60Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Week 2: Try everything 20 Jan

Problems: A Milk Pails, B Diamond Collector, sprint Daisy Chains.

  1. 0 to 5Huddle. Goal: count your loops before you code. Rule: if it is small enough, try everything.
  2. 5 to 15Demo: Milk Pails with two loops. Then the maths on the board. M is at most 1,000, so at most about a million tries. The USACO Guide calls 108 simple steps a second a conservative guess. Python is slower, which is why USACO gives it 4 seconds, not 2.
  3. 15 to 35Training: Diamond Collector. Before typing, each pair writes how many steps their plan takes when N is 1,000.
  4. 35 to 50Sprint: Daisy Chains. The trap: it reads from the keyboard, not a file. The pairs who read the input section win.
  5. 50 to 57Debrief. Fastest pair shows code. I ask the room: how many photos are there when N is 100?
  6. 57 to 60Pack up. Hand out the NOI interest slip; names back next week.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Week 3: Sort and search 27 Jan

Problems: A Where Am I?, B Why Did the Cow Cross the Road III, sprint Counting Haybales.

  1. 0 to 5Huddle. Goal: know when trying everything is too slow. Collect NOI names.
  2. 5 to 15Demo: Where Am I? with a set. Then the warning for problem B: read what the input section promises, and what it does not.
  3. 15 to 35Training: Cow Cross the Road III. First, each pair writes a tiny test with the cows out of order.
  4. 35 to 50Sprint: Counting Haybales. Up to 100,000 bales and 100,000 questions. A brute force scores some tests, and that is fine: points are points. Then go for the rest with bisect.
  5. 50 to 57Debrief. I show my own brute force: on my laptop it passed 3 of the 10 official tests inside 4 seconds. Then a pair with 10 of 10 shows how they did it.
  6. 57 to 60Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Week 4: Flood the grid 3 Feb

Problems: A Bucket Brigade, B Icy Perimeter, sprint Mooyo Mooyo.

  1. 0 to 5Huddle. Goal: BFS from memory. Rule: mark a cell as seen when you add it to the queue.
  2. 5 to 15Demo: Bucket Brigade. BFS from the lake, using collections.deque. Students call out the next cell to leave the queue.
  3. 15 to 35Training: Icy Perimeter. A 1,000 by 1,000 grid. Use a queue, not recursion.
  4. 35 to 50Sprint: Mooyo Mooyo, solo this time. One laptop each from the club cupboard. This is the closest thing to a real contest.
  5. 50 to 57Debrief and the NOI note from the main page. Next stop: a past NOI preliminary on CodeBreaker, split over two Wednesdays.
  6. 57 to 60Pack up.

Check: 5 + 10 + 20 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Beginners and strong coders in one squad

  • Mixed pairs. The stronger coder explains, the newer coder types more.
  • Done early in training: start the sprint problem's slow version on paper. Do not submit it.
  • Stuck: flip the Nudge card first, then the Push card. Hints cost nothing in training.
  • Strong coders who finish the sprint early: make the solution faster, or try the next rung.

Squad rules

  1. We train under contest rules. USACO says code is written from scratch, and only language references are allowed. So: Python docs yes, AI tools and copied code no.
  2. Help in words. In a pair, only the driver touches the keyboard.
  3. Every red box gets one line in your pair's log: which test, which letter, what you changed.
  4. Win or lose a sprint, you shake hands. Same as robotics.

How I know it worked

  • Every pair scores in every sprint, even if only the sample.
  • By week 4, half the squad or more scores 10 of 10 in a sprint.
  • At least 8 names on the NOI list.
