Sprint to NOI

Four weeks. Twelve contest problems. Every session ends on the clock, with a scoreboard. By February, nobody on my squad has ever sat a timed contest for the first time.

I'm Marcus. I coach a robotics team, and we win by testing under pressure, not by reading about it. Contest programming is the same sport. So this programme trains it like one: reps, a timer and a live ranking. This is my end project for Competitive Programming Basics.

The squad

  • Who: 16 students from Sec 2 to Sec 4. My robotics squad, plus anyone who passed the club's Python check. Everyone can write loops, lists and functions.
  • Where: the robotics room. 8 school laptops with Python 3 and a browser, one per pair.
  • When: Wednesdays, 3.30 to 4.30 pm, in Term 1 of 2027: 13, 20 and 27 January, and 3 February.
  • Judge: USACO. Past problems stay open for submission in "analysis mode" once you log in.
  • Target: the NOI preliminary contest in February.

Why USACO and not another judge

  • It is free and takes Python 3.
  • It marks every test case in its own box, green or red. Pass 6 of 10 and you see it. That is how NOI feels too, with points for each subtask.
  • The first test is the sample, and you must pass it before you see the rest. That stops blind submitting.
  • Old contests publish their official test data. So my squad can test at home, and so could I. Every solution here passes every official test.

USACO accounts ask for your real name, email, school and graduation year. I could not find a minimum age on USACO's site. My squad is all 13 or older anyway. A letter goes home first, and students sign up with a parent's OK. No Sec 1s this round.

The four weeks

  1. Simulate it

    Do exactly what the story says, step by step.

    Sprint: The Bucket List

  2. Try everything

    Complete search. Count the loops first.

    Sprint: Daisy Chains

  3. Sort and search

    Sets, sorting, then binary search.

    Sprint: Counting Haybales

  4. Flood the grid

    BFS for shortest paths and blobs.

    Sprint: Mooyo Mooyo

Each session runs the same: I solve one problem live, pairs train on the second, then the third is the sprint. Full timings are in the session plans.

Why complete search first? It wins points in week one, and it is always right when the input is small. Greedy ideas come later. New coders guess a rule and never test it. So in week 3, I make them check a clever idea against a slow, sure one.

      Entering NOI

      This is the note I give the squad in week 4. The facts are from the NOI 2026 pages, the latest when I wrote this. Check the competition page for 2027 dates.

      Who and how

      • Any student at a local secondary school or JC can enter, whatever their nationality.
      • You cannot sign up yourself. A teacher registers on the NOI website and enrols everyone. It is free.
      • In 2026, registration closed on 14 February. So I collect names after week 2, not after week 4.

      Race format

      • Preliminary: online, 5 tasks in 5 hours. In 2026: Saturday 21 February, 1 pm to 6 pm.
      • Final: 5 tasks in 5 hours at NUS School of Computing. In 2026: Saturday 14 March.
      • Each school sends its top five in each category, Secondary or JC, plus its top girl, to the final. All need a score above a cut-off.

      Python rules that change your plan

      • C++ is the main language. Python 3 is allowed, judged with Python 3.8.10.
      • Python gets no extra time. USACO gives Python more time; NOI does not. Fast code matters more there.
      • Python users can only try Batch tasks. Interactive tasks need C++.
      • A full Python solution is not promised for every task. Take the subtasks you can.

      Where to train next

      • Past NOI contests on CodeBreaker, which the NOI practice page recommends.
      • Keep climbing USACO Bronze, then Silver, with the USACO Guide.

      What I would change

      The sprint rewards speed, and that is the point. But it can bury a quiet student who solves things slowly and well. In my plan, the fastest pair shows their code first. Next time I would also show the cleanest code, picked by me, so slow and careful gets airtime.

      The ladder is also all USACO, with farm stories and cows. Some students tune out of the stories. NOI problems read differently. A fifth week with one past NOI preliminary task would close that gap.

      Pairs share one laptop for three weeks, and only the week 4 sprint is solo. In a real contest, you are alone for five hours. One solo sprint is not enough practice for that. I would make the week 3 sprint solo too.
