Cards in a cardboard phone

A 60-minute makerspace lesson for Sec 2. Groups turn their user flow into 3 paper screens and test them on other groups. Then they pin them up for critique.

  • Class: 32 Sec 2 students in 8 groups of 4.
  • Time: 60 minutes, in the makerspace.
  • Before this lesson: each group interviewed 3 people about one school task. They drew a persona and a user flow of 8 steps or fewer. Ideas so far: borrowing a ball, finding a CCA venue, topping up a canteen card.
  • This lesson covers: wireframes, a test and critique. Next lesson they fix and test again.
  • No devices and no accounts. Cardboard, cards and pencils.

1By the end, students can

  • Draw 3 screens with one main action each, using size and space, not colour.
  • Run a paper test as "computer", facilitator or note-taker, without helping the tester.
  • Give critique about the screen, not the person, with a sentence starter.

2Success criteria

  1. Squint at each card. The main action is the first thing you see.
  2. Your note-taker wrote what testers did, not what they said they liked.
  3. Every sticky note you wrote starts with a starter and points at one card.
  4. Your group chose one fix and can name the note behind it.

3What to prepare

  • 8 cardboard phone frames. I make them once and reuse them with every class. The cutting diagram is below.
  • Blank A6 cards, 12 per group. Cut A4 card into quarters.
  • Pencils, erasers, one thick marker per group. No coloured pens today.
  • Task cards: each group writes their own 2 tasks.
  • Sticky notes, 6 per student. Blu-tack and wall space for 8 groups.
  • A timer on the projector.

4Run sheet

  1. 0 to 4Settle. Groups sit with their user flow. Frames and cards are already on each table. Quick safety reminder: today is scissors only. The craft knives stay locked.
  2. 4 to 10Watch me get it wrong. A volunteer tests my paper Laser Slot cards. I play "computer" and swap cards. The class tallies every time I help. Then I show my grey slots and ask: "What did grey mean?"
  3. 10 to 28Draw 3 cards. Pick the 3 most important screens in your flow. One card each. Board rules: 1 main action, 3 text sizes at most, space between groups, no colour. At 24 minutes, every group squints at their cards and fixes one.
  4. 28 to 32Write 2 tasks and pick roles. Tasks start "Find…" or "Do…". Roles: computer, facilitator, note-taker and traveller.
  5. 32 to 46Test carousel. Each traveller moves one group clockwise and becomes the tester. The facilitator reads one task, then stays silent. The computer swaps cards. The note-taker writes what happened and tallies interruptions. Round 1 runs 6 minutes. At 38 minutes, travellers move on again. Round 2 runs from 39 to 45. Travellers return at 45.
  6. 46 to 56Critique wall. Each group pins its 3 cards up. Everyone writes 2 sticky notes for 2 other groups, using the starters. Designers read their notes in silence.
  7. 56 to 60Pick one fix, tidy up. Each group circles one sticky note or test note. They write the fix on the back of the card. Frames go back on the shelf.

Check: 4 + 6 + 18 + 4 + 14 + 10 + 4 = 60 minutes.

5Critique rules

  • I saw…
    What the tester did. "I saw him tap the title twice."
  • I tapped … because I thought…
    For testers. "I tapped grey because I thought it was empty."
  • I wonder if…
    A question. "I wonder if the button could go higher."
  • Try…
    One idea for the next test. "Try the word Free on the box."

Stick it on the card, not the person. Say "this button" or "card 2". Never "you forgot" or "your design is bad". Each note names one card and one thing.

Designers read their notes in silence. If they disagree with a note, they test it next lesson.

Any sticky note colour works. The starter is written on the note, so colour never carries the meaning.

6For different students

  • Hates drawing: use a ruler and boxes only. Wireframes are meant to look plain.
  • Quiet in groups: be the note-taker. Your notes decide the fix.
  • Takes over: be the facilitator. Your job is to stay silent, and the tally shows it.
  • Finished early: draw the error screen. What happens when the user does it wrong?

7How I check learning

During the carousel I walk with a clipboard. I tick criterion 2 when a note-taker writes an action, like "tapped the logo". I read the critique wall for criterion 3. The back of each card shows criterion 4. I squint at every card myself for criterion 1.

Each group meets only 2 testers today. Over the next two lessons they test again, until each design has met 5. NN/g's article on testing with 5 users explains why. Small rounds, with fixes in between, work.

8Cutting diagram: the cardboard phone frame

An A6 card slides in from the top and shows through a window. Swap cards to "change screens".

How to make it

  1. Mark all five pieces on corrugated cardboard with a pencil and steel ruler.
  2. Cut them out. The window and the notch go in the front piece only.
  3. Glue the side strips along the long edges of the back. Glue the bottom strip across the bottom.
  4. Glue the front on top. The open top is the slot.
  5. Slide in an A6 card. It drops onto the bottom strip and shows through the window.

Why these sizes: an A6 card is 105 by 148 mm. The gap between the strips is 120 mm wide and 200 mm deep. The card slides in easily. The window is 90 by 135 mm and starts 60 mm from the top. The card covers it even when it slides sideways. Its edges stay hidden.

Safety when cutting cardboard. I cut the frames myself, before class, with a craft knife. Put a cutting mat under the cardboard. Cut along a safety ruler with a raised edge. Keep your hands out of the blade's path. Cut to the side of your body, not towards it. Put the blade away whenever you stop cutting. Students who make frames later follow the same rules, with supervision.
