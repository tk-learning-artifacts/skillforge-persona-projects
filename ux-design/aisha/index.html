Laser Slot

Our makerspace books the laser cutter through one long online form. Students double-book, turn up untrained, or bring plastic we can't cut. I redesigned that one task. I tested it on paper with 5 students, then built the fixed version.

I'm Aisha. I teach design and making in a school makerspace. I don't trust a design until someone has tapped it with a finger. So this project is mostly paper, cardboard and sticky notes. A working site comes at the end. Then my Sec 2 class does the same steps on their own app ideas.

1The old form

Students book on a single-page form. Every answer is a free text box. After you press Submit, a teacher reads it and emails you back. Sometimes.

I matched each problem to one of Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics.

  1. No safety check. Anyone can book, trained or not. That breaks "Error Prevention".
  2. Free slots are hidden. You guess a day and hope. That breaks "Visibility of System Status".
  3. Any material can be typed. Someone typed "pvc". PVC must never go in a laser cutter. That breaks "Error Prevention" too, and it is a safety problem.
  4. "Your response has been recorded." Booked? Not booked? When? That breaks "Visibility of System Status" again.

2Interviews

I interviewed 3 students and our lab technician, for about 10 minutes each. We stood at the laser cutter. I asked about the last time they booked it.

  • Sec 2 student, keychain project
    "I booked Tuesday. So did two other people. I went home."
    Pain: no way to see who else booked.
  • Sec 3 student, robotics CCA
    She cuts acrylic parts weekly. "Why does it ask my class again?" Last month she found someone else on her slot.
    Pain: long form, and clashes.
  • Sec 4 student, project parts
    He booked before his safety session. The technician sent him away at the door.
    Pain: found out too late.
  • Lab technician
    "Once a month someone brings a vinyl sticker sheet." He checks every material by hand.
    Pain: unsafe material reaches the machine.

The surprise: I thought the problem was the long form. Students hated the clash more. Two of three had turned up to find the machine taken.

4Three wireframes, on index cards

I drew each screen in pencil on an A6 card. Boxes and labels only. One main action each, three text sizes, no colour. Then I slid the cards into a cardboard phone frame and tested them. You can make the frame from the cutting diagram in the lesson plan.

6Test notes from 5 students

I tested the paper cards with 5 students, from Sec 2 to Sec 4. One at a time, at a makerspace bench, about 6 minutes each. I played "computer" and swapped the cards. A Sec 3 helper took notes and tallied my interruptions. I interrupted twice, so I dropped those two moments from the notes.

Each student had 3 tasks, read aloud from a card:

  • Task A: "Book the laser cutter after school on Thursday."
  • Task B: "You want to cut a clear acrylic sheet. Is it allowed?"
  • Task C: "You are sick on Thursday. Cancel your booking."

Everything we saw

  1. Task A. Student 1 tapped a grey box first. "Grey is free, right? Like empty?" Grey meant taken.
  2. Task A. Student 2's job needs about 45 minutes. One slot is 30. She booked one and said, "I'll just go fast."
  3. Task A. Student 3 had not done the safety session. He reached card 3, saw the tick box, and stopped. "So I did all that for nothing?"
  4. Task B. Student 4 read the list twice. "Acrylic isn't here. What's PMMA?" PMMA is acrylic, but nobody calls it that.
  5. Task A. Student 5 finished, looked at "Done" and asked, "Done what? Which day did I get?"
  6. Task C. Students 1 and 5 couldn't find a way to cancel. It was hidden in a menu card I hadn't shown.

The top 3 fixes

I ranked by safety first, then by how many students got stuck.

  • N3 The safety question moved to the very start. "Not yet" shows the next safety sessions. You never pick a slot you can't use.
  • N1 Every slot now says "Free" or "Taken" in words. Taken slots are striped and can't be tapped. Colour is no longer the only clue.
  • N5, N6 After booking you get a booking card: day, time, room, what to bring. It stays on the first screen, with a "Cancel booking" button.

Smaller fixes

  • N4 "Acrylic 3 mm (PMMA, also sold as Perspex)". Students' word first.
  • N2 Not fixed. Longer jobs need two slots in a row. The technician and I have to agree on that rule first.

7The lesson

My Sec 2 class does the same steps on their own app ideas. They use cardboard phone frames and index cards. In one 60-minute lesson they build 3 cards and test them in a carousel. Then they pin them up for critique.

8What I would change

N2 bugs me. A student who needs 45 minutes will still book 30 and rush. Rushing at a laser cutter is exactly what we don't want. Next I'd add "How long is your cut?" and offer two slots in a row.

I also tested only the paper version. The coloured site has not met a real student yet. The paper test found the big problems. A second round of 5 on the site would catch the small ones. And I only tested with students. Teachers book the cutter too, for whole classes, and they need something different.
