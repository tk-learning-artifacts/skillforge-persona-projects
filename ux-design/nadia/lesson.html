One screen, one test, kind words

A 60-minute lesson for a P5 class. Pupils sketch one screen, test it silently on a partner, and give kind, specific critique.

  • Class: 30 P5 pupils with a wide range of needs. 7 groups of 4, plus one pair who critique with me.
  • Time: 60 minutes.
  • Before this lesson, each pupil interviewed 2 classmates. They asked about the last time something in school confused them. They wrote 3 notes. Ideas so far: the canteen queue, the library loan screen, the CCA notice board.
  • This lesson covers: wireframe, test and critique. The next lesson is for fixing.
  • No devices. No accounts. Paper only.

By the end, pupils can

  • Sketch one screen with one main action that stands out.
  • Watch a tester in silence and write what they saw.
  • Give feedback about the screen, not the person, using a sentence starter.

Success criteria

  1. When you squint, the main action is the first thing you see.
  2. You wrote at least 2 things you saw your tester do.
  3. You gave one piece of feedback that starts with a sentence starter.
  4. You chose one fix and can point to the note behind it.

Materials

  • 30 A4 phone outlines, plus 5 on A3 for pupils who need bigger lines.
  • 10 bags of pre-cut paper pieces: a heading, 3 buttons, 2 picture boxes and a list.
  • Thick black markers and pencils. Glue sticks.
  • 30 "I saw" sheets: 4 rows, each with a picture prompt and a line.
  • 8 sets of talk cards (print them below). A visual timer on the board.

I cut the paper pieces myself before the lesson, so pupils don't need scissors. If pupils trim pieces, give round-tip scissors only.

Run sheet

  1. 0 to 5Settle and hook. Show the old Homework Board on the screen. Ask: "Where would you look first?" Take three answers. Point out that everyone looked at a different place.
  2. 5 to 12Model. Show my persona, Wei Jie, and my "Tonight" wireframe. Think aloud: "His main job is to pick a task. So the task cards are biggest." Then I show the three rules: 1 main action, 3 text sizes at most, no colour yet.
  3. 12 to 27Make one screen. Pupils pick one way to make it. Draw it on the outline. Or build it with the paper pieces. Or tell a partner, who draws while you point. All three count the same. At 22 minutes, everyone does the squint test on their own screen.
  4. 27 to 30Write one task. On a strip: "Find…" or "Show me how to…". I read examples aloud. Pupils can copy one.
  5. 30 to 42Silent test, in pairs. The tester reads the task and says what they think as they point. The designer stays silent and fills the "I saw" sheet. Each interruption gets a tally mark. Swap at 36 minutes.
  6. 42 to 54Kind critique, in groups of 4. 3 minutes per screen. Each pupil gives one comment, spoken or with a talk card. The designer only says "thank you", then asks one question at the end.
  7. 54 to 58Choose a fix. Circle one note on your "I saw" sheet. On a sticky note, draw or write the one change you will make.
  8. 58 to 60Pack up. Screens and sticky notes go in the group folder.

Check: 5 + 7 + 15 + 3 + 12 + 12 + 4 + 2 = 60 minutes.

Critique rules

Use a sentence starter

  • "I noticed…" Something you saw, like "I noticed the button is at the bottom."
  • "I got stuck when…" Where you, as the tester, stopped.
  • "I wonder if…" A question, like "I wonder if the title could be bigger."
  • "What if you tried…" One idea to test next.

Talk to the screen, not the person. Point at the paper. Say "the button" or "this heading". Never say "you did it wrong".

Designers say only "thank you" until everyone has spoken. Then they ask one question.

For different pupils

  • Reading load: I read every task and starter aloud. The starters are also on the talk cards, with a shape for each.
  • Not ready to speak: lay a talk card on the part of the screen. Point to show where. That counts as a full comment.
  • Needs to move: be the group's timekeeper. You hold the timer and call "swap".
  • Low vision: A3 outlines and thick markers. Sit near the board.
  • Spelling worries: spelling does not count today. Pictures and arrows are fine.
  • Finished early: sketch the next screen in your flow.

How I check learning

I walk the room during the test with a class list. I tick criterion 2 when I see notes on an "I saw" sheet. I tick criterion 3 during critique, spoken or with a card. The sticky note is criterion 4. Criterion 1 I check myself, by squinting at each screen at the end.

Why it is built this way

The test is silent because helping changes what testers do. Jakob Nielsen's think-aloud article warns that interruptions "can very easily change user behavior". The tally makes pupils notice their own interruptions.

Each pupil tests one partner today. That is not 5 users. Over the next two lessons each screen meets 4 more testers. NN/g's article on 5 users explains why small, repeated rounds work.
