The Two-Minute Handover

A phone screen that tells a cover trainer where a class really stopped.

Priya Nair, curriculum lead
Users: 12 trainers, P4 and P5 micro:bit programme

Empathise3 interviews

I assumed our lesson plans were too thin. Three trainers told me about their last cover lesson. The plans were fine. What failed was knowing where the class really was, and what kit was broken.

"The plan said week 4. The class had never finished week 3. I found out 20 minutes in." Trainer A

Regular trainers wrote handovers later, "on the MRT home", from memory. The only free time is the two minutes of pack-up.

Define2 statements, 1 question

A cover trainer needs to know where the class really stopped and what will trip them up, because the plan shows where the class should be, not where it is.

A regular trainer needs to hand over in the time it takes pupils to pack up, because after the lesson the details are gone.

How might we pass on the state of a class in the two minutes of pack-up?

Ideate20 ideas

Six of us, 15 minutes, four themes: say it, paper, screen, change the lesson. Picked: a three-tap exit screen (reaches the cover trainer the night before) and a card in the kit box (next loop).

Assumption to test: trainers will hand over during pack-up if it takes under two minutes.

Prototype and test3 trainers, twice

Version 1, a paper phone with nine fields. Mock pack-ups, timed, me silent.

  • Nobody finished in two minutes.
  • "Where did you stop?" got "halfway". The reader asked, "Through which step?"
  • Two trainers wrote pupils' names. My form asked for them.
  • The reader read two fields of nine.

Critique: "I wish I could tap." "What if it knew the steps?"

Version 2, a working web page: tap the last step finished, give a number of pupils behind (no names), tap kit problems. The cover trainer gets one card: "Start at step 5".

A v1 4:10 B v1 5:30 D v1 3:50 A v2 0:55 B v2 1:10 D v2 1:40

The reader started at the right step 3 times out of 3.

What I learnedand what's next

  • Trainers asked for a better plan. They needed the state of the class.
  • Version 2 needs a step list for every lesson. That's about 80 steps for my team to write into the scheme of work.
  • Three testers find only some problems: Nielsen (2000) says 5 users find about 85%. Next: the kit box card beside the phone, then four real cover lessons.

Trainers, quotes and times are made up.
