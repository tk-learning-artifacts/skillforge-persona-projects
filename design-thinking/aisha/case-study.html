Design thinking case study · one loop · Sec 1 makerspace

The Cool-Down Dock

Aisha Rahman, design and making teacher. A cardboard and micro:bit V2 stand. It shows when a glue gun has had time to cool.

1 Empathise

I interviewed our technician and two Sec 1 students about the last pack-up. I expected guns left switched on. They were unplugged. The technician still finds two or three hot guns in the tray after most lessons.

"I hold my hand near the tip to feel if it's cold. It's hard to tell." Student A

Cool-melt guns don't fix it: Bostik lists its cool-melt gun at 125 to 135°C.

2 Define

Sec 1 makers packing up need to see when a glue gun is safe to put away, because a hot gun looks the same as a cold one.

How might we make a glue gun's heat visible at pack-up?

3 Ideate

20 sticky notes in 15 minutes, in four themes: routines, materials, signals, wild ones. We picked two. Unplug 10 minutes before the bell (free, starts now). A dock that counts down the cooling time (the one to prototype). Assumption to test: if students can see the time left, they will wait.

Idea 14, measuring the tip, died fast. The micro:bit's temperature sensor is inside its processor and reads roughly the air. So we time the cooling instead.

What the loop changed

  • The problem. From "students leave guns on" to "hot and cold guns look the same".
  • The signal. From a number hidden under the gun to lights you read at a glance.
  • The trigger. From light, which anything can change, to the gun's own weight.
  • My habit. I now ask about the last pack-up, not what students would like.

4 Prototype and 5 Test

Version 1, 90 minutes: the micro:bit under a hole in a card cradle. Darkness starts a 10-minute count.

Three testers, one new to the project. I only watched.

  • "Where's the timer?" The gun covered the display.
  • A hand, or the lights going off, started the empty dock.
  • Lifting early gave no warning.

Version 2, from the notes: the gun's weight presses two foil pads. Hands and lights can't start it. The display moved to the front. 25 lights drain like a battery, then a tick and a beep. Lifting early: a cross and two low beeps.

Retest, same three people: all waited for the tick. The technician could read it at 2 m, not from the door.

Next loop

Docks send their state by radio to one micro:bit at the door. First, test whether early unplugging alone is enough. Then test version 2 in a real pack-up with 20 students.
