Part 3

Business plan

How Second Round can run three times a year and cover its costs. And how it can outlive the class that started it.

The costs, prices and counts here are made up for this example, like me. They are close to what a school would pay, but check your own suppliers. The rules from CCS, ScamShield and raiSE are real and linked.

Key resources

  • A cupboard near the office
  • A trained team of 6
  • Check cards, size chart
  • The request slip

Value proposition

The right size, fast, without being seen. $2 an item, or swap one for one. Every item stays out of the bin a little longer.

Customer relationships

Private and light. Numbers, not names. One feedback card per visit.

Channels

  • Form teachers and slips
  • Numbered bags at the office
  • After-school try-on room
  • Posters

Customer segments

  • Students who grow mid-year
  • Families just above the help line
  • Anyone who lost kit
  • Donors: families with outgrown items

Cost structure

Bags, labels, stain remover and posters: about $18 a window. Start-up: $18. Hidden cost: about 15 hours of people's time a window.

Impact we count

Items reused. Requests filled. Large sizes filled. Kilograms kept from the bin.

Revenue streams

$2 an item. Swaps bring in stock, not money. Any surplus goes to the student welfare fund, with the principal's agreement.

Is it a social enterprise?

Not by raiSE's standard. raiSE, Singapore's centre for social enterprise, takes ACRA-registered businesses as members. We are a school project. But its three principles still guide us:

  • Intentionality: the social aim is built into what we sell. Our value proposition is access, not profit.
  • Additionality: systems and steps back up the impact. That is why the canvas has an impact box.
  • Proportionality: a real share of what we earn goes to the people we serve. All of our surplus goes to the welfare fund.

02Prices, costs and revenue

The price

Second Round

$2 an item

Or swap: bring one clean uniform item, take one. No charge for a swap.

Up to 3 items a visit. No other charges.

  • One price for every item. No extra charges, so the price on the sign is the price you pay.
  • No "was $10" claims. We have never sold at $10, so it would be a fake discount.
  • The swap condition sits on the same sign, in the same size text. Singapore's price transparency guidelines say a "free" claim must not mislead. Its conditions must be clear and prominent.
  • Why $2? Nine sales cover a window's costs. It is one coin. It is a price nobody has tested yet, which is the point of January.

Some things are not in the table, because no cash changes hands. These are the cupboard, the office's time and the float. The float is $30 in coins. The finance office lends it, and we return it after each window.

The biggest cost is time. A window takes about 15 hours. That is 6 students for 2 hours each, and 3 hours of mine.

Why these numbers? Swap week had 88 items in and 71 usable (81%). The expected case sits just below that. The sold share is a guess, because nobody has paid yet. Swap week gave away 52 of 71 usable items, or 73%. Paying will lower that, so I used 55%.

A year has three windows. In the expected case, 3 × $52 = $156, less the $18 start-up. That is $138 for the welfare fund. It is a small sum, and I tell my students so. Per hour of work, it is about $3.50. As a wage that is poor. As a club activity that pays its own way, it is fine. The real return is 140 or so uniforms a year that skip the bin.

04Handling the money safely

  • The float is $30 in coins, signed out from the finance office. Two people count it at the start and the end, and both sign the sheet.
  • A teacher holds the cash box. A student never holds it alone.
  • At the try-on room we take cash only. Goods go over after the coin is in the box.
  • For request bags, the general office takes the $2. It does this like any small school payment.
  • No student takes a transfer on their own phone. ScamShield warns that scammers send sellers fake screenshots as "proof of payment".
  • The cash is counted, signed for and handed to the finance office the same day.

05Growth plan

Growth here means lasting and being copied, not getting big. One school's cupboard can't serve the island. A two-page handbook can travel.

  1. Term 4, 2026 (now to 20 November)

    Hand over and stock up

    My Sec 3s become Sec 4s next year, with national exams ahead. The Green Club's Sec 2s take over. Three of my students train them in two sessions.

    Run an XL drive with Sec 4 leavers. Agree with the general office to pass on unclaimed PE shirts each year end.

    Gate: 20 or more L and XL items in the cupboard by 20 November

  2. Term 1, 2027, week 2

    First paid window: the price test

    Bags and the try-on room, at $2 or a swap. Principal's approval and the float are arranged in Term 4.

    Gate: 25 or more items sold. Keep $2. Fewer than 25 sold, but 25 or more requests: try $1. Fewer than 15 requests: go swap-only, once a year.

  3. Term 3, 2027, week 2

    Mid-year window, for the growth spurts

    The request slip stays open all year. The Green Club runs the window alone. I only hold the key and the cash box.

    Gate: the club runs it with no more than 1 hour of teacher time

  4. Term 4, 2027

    Write it down and share it

    The club writes a two-page handbook. It has the check card, the slip, the money rules and the numbers. We offer it to two nearby secondary schools. Each school has its own uniform, so each runs its own swap.

    Gate: one other school agrees to try it

What we will not do

  • Sell online. Students would deal with strangers and payment scams.
  • Take shoes or socks. Hygiene, and we can't check them well.
  • Sell near the uniform shop in December, as Mr Koh asked.
