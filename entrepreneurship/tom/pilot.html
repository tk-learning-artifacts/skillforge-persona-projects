Part 2

The service and its test

Our MVP was a service, not a product: one week, one table, one cupboard. We changed it halfway through the week.

The people, counts and quotes on this page are made up, like me.

01What we tested

Swap week ran from Monday 17 to Friday 21 August, week 8 of Term 3. My principal agreed to it in week 3.

  • No money changed hands. Every item was free during the test. The module's rule is clear: take no money while you test.
  • We only gave out items that passed our check.
  • Students handled the items. I kept the request slips and the key to the cupboard.

So this test answers one question: will people come and take the right size? It cannot answer whether they will pay. That question waits for the first sale in 2027.

03How the service works

The five-point check card

  1. Washed, with no smell.
  2. No stain you can see at arm's length.
  3. No tears. Hems and seams hold.
  4. Zips and buttons work.
  5. Our school's crest and current design.

Two students check each item. If they disagree, it fails. Stained and torn items go to the art room's rag bag. Items from other schools go back to the family.

04Two versions in one week

Version 1: a recess table (Monday and Tuesday)

A table at the canteen, both recesses. Requests were collected at the table too. Students watched and counted.

  • 9 items taken in two days. 5 of them were requests.
  • The counting team saw 14 students stop, look around, and walk away.
  • A group of Sec 1 boys joked about "used pants" near the table. Two girls left mid-choice.
  • Two students asked if they could come after school instead.

This was finding 3 from the interviews, happening in front of us. Jun Hao had told us in week 2.

Version 2: bags and an after-school room (Wednesday to Friday)

  • Requests were packed in plain paper bags with a number, not a name.
  • Form teachers passed each student a slip with their number. They collected the bag at the general office, during office hours.
  • The table moved to a classroom near the office, from 2.30 to 3.30 pm. It had a mirror, a tape measure and a size chart.
  • We dropped the recess table.

Feedback cards were collected for the whole week, so they are not split by version. "Large" means L and XL. All 8 unfilled requests were L or XL: 4 pants and 4 PE shorts.

  • Every packed bag was collected by Friday. Two needed a reminder slip through form teachers.
  • Seven people came back to the after-school room a second time.
  • 19 items were left, 13 of them small school shirts. They are in the cupboard for the next window.
  • We weighed 20 items: 4.1 kg in all. So the 52 items came to about 10 kg.

Ten kilograms, next to the 231,000 tonnes in NEA's 2025 figures, is nothing. My students worked that out themselves. We agreed the point is habits and access, not tonnes. A project should say what it cannot do.

06What the test changed in the plan

  • Bags by number become the main channel. The table is only for trying on.
  • An XL drive comes before every window. We ask Sec 4 leavers first.
  • The request slip stays open all term, not only before a window.
  • The cap of 3 items stays, so one family cannot clear a size.
  • We still don't know if people will pay. See the price test.

Who it still leaves out

The parent who came because her son would not. Bags helped, but some students still won't use it. And anyone outside a size we stock. I don't have a fix for the first group yet. Maybe the best fix is time: when enough students use it, it stops being strange.
