01The idea

Teenagers grow fast. A Sec 1 shirt can be too small by June. The old one sits in a cupboard for years, then goes in the bin.

Second Round collects outgrown uniforms and PE kit. Students check each item, tag its size and pass it on. In the plan, an item costs $2. Or you bring one clean item and take one, at no charge. Any money left after costs goes to the school's student welfare fund.

I picked a uniform swap for a reason. My students see it every day. It has a money side, a waste side and a fairness side. Each side leads to a bigger question about the city they live in.

02Who it helps, and who it leaves out

I ask this about every project. A business that helps one group can quietly shut out another.

It helps

  • Students who grow mid-year. The parents we interviewed bought uniforms once a year, in December. A growth spurt in June is the gap.
  • Families just above the line for school financial help. Families on the MOE Financial Assistance Scheme can get free school attire. It is given on a need basis. Families who earn a little more get no help.
  • The bin. In 2025, Singapore recycled only 3% of its textile and leather waste (NEA).

It can leave out

  • Students who feel shy to be seen taking second-hand. Version 1 lost them at the recess table. Version 2 packs requests in plain, numbered bags.
  • Students in large sizes. Donations were mostly small and medium. All 8 requests we could not fill were L or XL.
  • The uniform shop. Its staff earn a living from our families too. We talked to the owner before the test. Read what he said in interview 11.

05What I would change

We never tested the price. School rules said no money during the test, and that was right. But 41 requests for free items do not prove 41 people will pay $2. So the first sale in 2027 is the real price test. The growth plan sets the number that decides what happens next.

I would also start the XL drive on day one. We found the size gap in week 8. A tally by size in week 5 would have shown it three weeks sooner.
