The deck and its tests

Version 1 took one morning and flopped with the little ones. Version 2 took one more morning. Here are both, the test results, and every card to print.

The children, the tests and the counts are made up, like me. The cards are real.

Version 1 and version 2

Test 1: version 1 on Wednesday

The Testers team took the deck next door. Six children from Ms Farah's camp, aged 5 to 7, played for 20 minutes. The Testers had one job: watch, don't help, and tally. Then 4 parents tried it at pick-up, 2 minutes each.

Games played in 20 minutes11
Cards nobody chose7 of 30
Parents who read a rule aloud and played3 of 4

Notes from the Testers

  • Note 1. Nur, 5, flipped through all 30 cards and gave them back. "Where are the pictures?"
  • Note 2. The 7 cards nobody chose all had 4 or more lines of words.
  • Note 3. Three cards needed a pen and paper. Nobody had one.
  • Note 4. A parent said, "Too many words. I'm holding a tray."
  • Note 5. Mr Farid said the paper bent after one day in his bag.
  • Note 6. Arjun, 12, finished his cards early. He wrote harder "level 2" versions. The little ones ignored them.

What changed for version 2

  • From notes 1 and 4: a picture on every card. Rules cut to two short lines, for the grown-up.
  • From note 2: the 7 unpicked cards went.
  • From note 3: the 3 pen-and-paper cards went too. 30 cards became 20.
  • From note 5: printed on thick card, with rounded corners.
  • From the interviews: each card shows its type by colour, icon and word. Look, Talk, Move or Listen. Colour is never the only clue.
  • From Kai's interview: three cards have a timer.
  • From note 6: Arjun's level 2 ideas became 3 blank cards. Older siblings make their own games.

Print the deck

One deck is 24 cards on 2 sheets of A4. That is 20 games, a how-to card and 3 blank cards. Each card is 64 by 70 mm. An optional third sheet prints the backs.

  • I printed on thick card at a print shop. The shop cut the cards on its guillotine.
  • At camp, campers only used a corner-rounder punch and rubber bands. No craft knives.
  • At home: print on the thickest card your printer takes. An adult cuts with a paper trimmer.
