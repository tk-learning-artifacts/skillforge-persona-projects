Stall Day

Run our showcase sale yourself. Pick a price. Decide whether to ask first. Print some decks. Then open the stall and see who comes.

My Shop team played this on Thursday, before I printed anything. It turned the money plan into a game. They argued about it for half an hour. That is what I wanted.

The families here are pretend. How they choose comes from a made-up model, set to land near our camp's numbers. It is a game for thinking, not a forecast.

1. Pick a price for one deck

Each deck costs $1.10 to print and pack. Set-up costs $14, whatever you sell.

Challenges

  • Beat our team's $74.80

  • Make a profit without a pre-order sheet
  • Find the price with the best 20-day average

For the one who finishes early: which price is best over 20 days? Is it the same price that wins a single day?

How the pretend families choose

  • There are 38 families, as at our camp. 4 to 10 visitors from the centre walk past too.
  • The chance a family wants a deck drops as the price goes up. It is about 9 in 10 at $2, 6 in 10 at $4 and 3 in 10 at $6.
  • About 1 in 4 buying families wants 2 decks, one for each child.
  • With a sheet, 9 in 10 families who want one sign it. Some forget and turn up on the day.
  • Pre-orders are always kept. A family who misses Friday collects later.
  • Walk-past visitors are half as keen as camp families.

Our real Friday: $4, a sheet, 26 pre-orders and 6 spares. We sold 31 and kept 1 as a sample. The model is not our camp. It is close enough to argue with.

What my Shop team took from it

  • Without a sheet, they either ran out or had a pile left. The sticker pack pile, again.
  • $6 felt rich, but fewer families bought. The best days were at $4 or $5.
  • One great day proves nothing. Twenty days show you the risk.

Wen asked the best question: "Why do we pay for the spare decks if nobody buys them?" That is the whole lesson about stock.
