Queue Quest made at camp

A pocket deck of 20 screen-free games for little kids stuck waiting. My holiday campers found the problem, tested the deck and sold 31 of them.

I'm Sofia. I run holiday camps for mixed ages. In the June holidays my campers made 40 sticker packs and sold 17. Nobody asked a buyer first. So in September we had one rule: ask first, make second. This is my end project for the Entrepreneurship and Innovation module.

My camp, the children and the parents are made up, like me. So is every number from the week. The deck is real: you can play it below and print it. The rules and dates I quote are real and linked.

How the camp week ran

Five mornings in the September holidays, 9 am to 1 pm. Monday 7 to Friday 11 September. 18 campers aged 8 to 12, in three mixed-age teams.

  • Makers write and draw the cards.
  • Testers run the games with the little ones.
  • Shop runs the pre-order sheet, the price and the money.

Next door, my colleague Ms Farah ran a camp for 22 children, aged 5 to 7. They were our users. Their parents, and ours, were our customers: 38 families in all.

Monday

  • Problem hunt: 10 problems
  • Pick one, write a How Might We
  • Practise interviews in pairs
  • 3 interviews at pick-up

Tuesday

  • 7 more interviews
  • Sort what we heard
  • Make version 1: 30 cards, words only

Wednesday

  • Test version 1 with 6 little ones
  • Test with 4 parents
  • Pre-order sheet goes up at pick-up

Thursday

  • Make version 2: 20 cards, pictures
  • Test it with 6 little ones
  • Sheet closes. I print 32 decks after camp.

Friday

  • Pack decks
  • Practise the cash table
  • Showcase and sale, 12 to 1 pm
  • Count up, vote on the profit

The 2026 September school holidays ran from 5 to 13 September.

What I would change

We only ever showed one price: $4. So I know 26 decks sold at $4. I don't know if 40 would sell at $3. In December, one camp's sheet will show $3 instead of $4. Then we compare.

Also, the deck is still too hard for four-year-olds. 8 of the 20 cards need counting, letters or rhymes. A "tiny ones" mini-deck of 8 picture-only cards would fix that. One of my quiet campers, Wen, spotted this on Thursday. I wish I had listened on Thursday instead of Friday.
