Price and costs

Queue Quest

$4 a deck

That's the whole price. No extra charges.

  • Why $4? Parents told us their last small buys. Most cost $3 to $5. Then 26 decks were ordered at $4.
  • Two decks cost $8. We had no "buy one, get one free" deals. Singapore's price transparency guidelines say a "free" claim must not mislead. A discount must be real. A camper's first idea was to say "was $6, now $4". We had never sold at $6, so that would have been a fake discount.

Break-even, two ways

  • If you print only what you sell, each deck leaves $2.90 after its own cost. $14 ÷ $2.90 is 4.8. So the 5th deck covers set-up.
  • But we printed 32 before selling any. That cost $35.20, plus $14 of set-up: $49.20. At $4 a deck, we needed 13 sales to cover it.

The second way is the real one. Every deck you print costs money, sold or not. That is why the sheet mattered.

Handling the money

  • The float was $20 in $1 coins and $2 notes, from the camp's cash box. Two adults counted it at the start and at the end. Both signed the count sheet.
  • An adult held the cash box all hour. Campers took coins and gave change with an adult beside them. Good maths practice, too.
  • Parents could pay cash, or PayNow to the camp's own account. An adult checked the camp's banking app for the money. Only then did a camper hand over the deck. We never accepted a screenshot. ScamShield warns that scammers send sellers fake screenshots as "proof of payment".
  • No camper used their own phone, or took money for a friend.
  • The count sheet: the $20 float at the start. At the end, the float plus $116 taken in cash and PayNow, for 29 decks. The last $8 came in at the front desk the next week.

Plan for the December holidays

The 2026 year-end school holidays run from 21 November to 31 December. I run two camps in that time. Growth here means small steps I can check, not a factory.

  1. From 9 November: ask first, again

    A pre-order sheet at the centre's front desk, for September families and December sign-ups. The rule for printing: pre-orders plus 20%.

    Gate: 15 decks ordered by 20 November, or no print run

  2. First December camp: a Tiny Ones mini-deck

    New campers make 8 picture-only cards for ages 3 and 4. No counting, no letters. Tested twice with Ms Farah's youngest, the same way as before. Price: $2 a mini-deck.

    Gate: 4 of 6 four-year-olds start a game alone

  3. Second December camp: test a second price

    The sheet shows $3 at this camp. Each camp sees one price. No "was" prices. At $3 a deck leaves $1.90, not $2.90. So $3 needs about 1.5 times the orders to earn the same.

    Gate: 1.5 times the orders per family, or $4 stays

  4. By 31 December: a front desk stand

    Ten decks at the front desk, with the centre manager's agreement. Desk staff take the payment.

    Gate: 10 sold by 31 December, or the stand goes

What we won't do

  • Sell online. Campers would deal with strangers, and scammers target sellers.
  • Print before the sheet closes.
  • Add "free" or "was" offers.
  • Let campers use craft knives. The print shop cuts. Campers use the corner punch only.
