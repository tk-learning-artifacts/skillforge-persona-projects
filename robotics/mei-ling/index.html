Kotak, the shy robot

A shoebox robot that wanders around a pen. When something is close, it stops, backs away, turns and carries on. Nobody steers it.

I'm Mei Ling. I run an after-school coding club for upper primary pupils. "Kotak" means box in Malay, and that is what Kotak is: a shoebox on two wheels, with a micro:bit for a brain.

The brief was one autonomous behaviour, with notes on how we tuned it. I picked obstacle avoidance over line following. It needs one sensor and one rule: "Is it close? Then turn." A 10-year-old can act that rule out with a partner before they code it. A line follower needs two sensors and a threshold, which is a lot for a 90-minute club.

My club and pupils are made up, like me. The test numbers on this page come from the simulator below, which runs the same steps as Kotak's code. Press the same buttons and you get the same numbers.

    Reflection

    The biggest surprise was version 1. It had a bug, and the bug made it look safe. When no echo came back, it got a negative number. Negative is less than 10, so it turned. It hardly ever bumped anything. But it stopped for nothing 39 times in 2 minutes. My pupils called it "scaredy-bot".

    Fixing the bug made the bumps go up. That upset two of my pupils, who wanted to put the bug back. It was a good argument to have. We settled on this: a robot that stops for nothing is broken too, just more politely.

    Shouting three times was the real fix. One wild number can't stop Kotak now. Stops for nothing fell from about 25 to under 3 per run.

    The trim test was the best 15 minutes of the whole project. Pupils typed in 76, 77, 78 and watched a wheel creep, stop, then creep the other way. They understood "calibrate" after that.

    What still goes wrong, and what I would change

    • Chairs. Put the chair in and Kotak bumps the legs again and again. Once it stayed stuck for 48 seconds. The legs are too thin to echo well. In club, I just keep chairs out of the pen. That is me simplifying again. A real classroom has chairs.
    • The fix I would try: a "bored" timer. If Kotak drives forward for 4 seconds with nothing changing, it backs away and turns anyway.
    • The sound beam is narrower than the robot. Kotak can brush a box with its side and never see it. A second sensor, angled out, would help, but that is a lot more wiring for P5.
    • Soft things soak up sound. The school bag rarely echoes back. I told pupils "Kotak can't hear soft things." That is true, but it is not the whole story. Slanted things bounce the sound away too.

    Sources

    • Sensor: Adafruit: RCWL-1601 ultrasonic distance sensor. Runs on 3 V to 5.5 V, and its logic level matches its power.
    • Sensor timing: HC-SR04 user manual. The RCWL-1601 is built to be compatible with it. It gives a 15 degree angle, sound at 340 m/s (so about microseconds ÷ 58 = cm), and over 60 ms between pings.
    • Servos: Adafruit: FS90R continuous rotation servo. A 1.5 ms pulse stops it, and it may need trimming.
    • Timing an echo: micro:bit MicroPython: machine. time_pulse_us returns a negative number on a timeout.
    • Servo signals: micro:bit MicroPython: pins, for set_analog_period and write_analog.
