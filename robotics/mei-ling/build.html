Building Kotak

A shoebox, two servos, one sensor and a lot of cable ties. Built by pairs of P5 pupils over three 90-minute club sessions.

Parts, for one robot

  • 1 shoebox with its lid, about 30 by 18 cm. Bring your own from home.
  • 1 micro:bit V2 on an edge-connector breakout board.
  • 1 RCWL-1601 ultrasonic sensor. It runs on 3 V.
  • 2 FS90R continuous rotation servos, with wheels to fit.
  • 1 plastic bottle cap, glued under the front as a skid.
  • 1 pack of 2 AA cells with the micro:bit's JST plug.
  • 1 pack of 4 rechargeable AA cells (NiMH, about 4.8 V) with a switch, for the servos.
  • 1 mini breadboard as a power strip, jumper wires, 8 cable ties, and Velcro squares.

Building it, session by session

Each club session is 90 minutes, with a 10-minute snack break. Pupils work in pairs: one builder, one checker, swapping at the break.

Session 1: box and wheels

  1. Before club, I cut two holes in the front of each box for the sensor's "eyes", and two slots for the servos. A craft knife is for the teacher only, on a cutting mat, cutting away from the body.
  2. Pupils run the trim test on each servo (see tuning notes) and write the numbers on masking tape on the servo.
  3. Push each servo through its slot. Glue it with a low-temperature glue gun, at the glue station I watch. Hold it for 30 seconds, and don't touch the nozzle or fresh glue.
  4. Add a cable tie round each servo, through two holes poked with a sharp pencil. Glue alone lets go after a few bumps.
  5. Push the wheels on. Glue the bottle cap under the front.

Session 2: wiring

  1. Switch off the servo pack. Unplug the micro:bit pack.
  2. Velcro the breakout, the breadboard and both battery packs inside the box. The heaviest thing, the 4-cell pack, goes in the middle, over the wheels.
  3. Push the sensor into its holes from inside. A blob of glue on each corner, never on the round "eyes".
  4. Wire it following the diagram. The checker reads each wire aloud from the table below and ticks it.
  5. Run the wiring checks below. Then I flash version 1.

Session 3: test and film

  1. Build the pen with books on their sides. Add the shoebox, a bag and a slanted file.
  2. Run 2-minute tests. One pupil counts bumps, one counts stops for nothing.
  3. Change one top number, test again, and fill in the tuning card.
  4. Last 15 minutes: film the one-minute demo on the club iPad.

P1 and P2 are not shared with the screen or buttons. P8 and P16 can do PWM and are free too.

Wiring checks

Every pair does these before every test. The checker reads them out.

  1. Is the servo pack's + going only to the breadboard + rail, never to a micro:bit pin?
  2. Is every − joined: sensor, servos, servo pack and micro:bit?
  3. Tug test: pull each wire gently. If it slides out, push it in further or swap the wire.
  4. Are the wires cable-tied to the box, with nothing near a wheel?
  5. Shake test: hold the box and shake it gently. Switch on. Is the sleepy face still showing?
  6. Hold Kotak up and press A. Do both wheels turn forward? If one turns backwards, tell the teacher.

Safety in club

  • Craft knives are for the teacher only. Pupils poke holes with a sharp pencil into a lump of Blu Tack.
  • Low-temperature glue guns only, at one table I can watch. A wet cloth sits next to it. Glue on skin: cool it under the tap and tell me.
  • Switch off the servo pack before touching any wire.
  • The rechargeable cells are charged by me, in sight, with their own charger. Pupils never charge them.
  • A hot, swollen or leaking battery: stop and tell me. Nobody touches it.
  • Never put batteries in a general bin or a blue recycling bin. Take them to a battery or e-waste bin. Tape the ends of rechargeable ones first.
  • Take the batteries out before the robots go into the cupboard.
