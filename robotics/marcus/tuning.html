Tuning notes

Four versions. Each one faced the same test. Each change points to the result that caused it.

The rooms and the sensor readings are made up, shaped like a real team's logs. Every test result below comes from the simulator on the main page. Pick the same version and room, press "Run 20 tests of 3 laps", and you get the same numbers.

01The test

  • A loop of 19 mm black tape: a long straight, corners C1 to C4, and an S-bend. The tightest bend has a 25 cm radius.
  • One test is 20 runs. Each run is 3 laps from a standing start.
  • Each run gets slightly different batteries, a slightly crooked start, and sensor noise. Like real life.
  • We log: runs finished, average lap time, fastest to slowest, and wobble.
  • Wobble is how far the robot points away from the tape, on average, in degrees. Lower is smoother.
  • The rule: change one thing, then run the whole test again.

Why 20 runs? Because one good lap proves nothing. Version 2 finished its first 8 runs. It still failed 4 of 20.

02Sensor readings by room

Before any code, we put the robot on the floor and on the tape and read the raw numbers off the screen. 0 to 1023, where black reads high.

Look at the last column. No single threshold works in all three rooms. A threshold of 512 calls the hall's tape "floor" and the dim corner's floor "tape".

Sensor height matters too. The TCRT5000 datasheet gives a peak operating distance of 2.5 mm. We mount ours a few millimetres off the floor and check it with a coin as a spacer. Too high and the gap between tape and floor shrinks.

Version 1: does it go round at all?

BASE 700 and TURN 700, so the inside wheel stops dead on every turn. Threshold 512, halfway up the scale. A guess.

  • CCA room: 20 of 20. It works, but it twitches. Wobble 2.9 degrees.
  • Hall: 0 of 20. The tape reads about 463, below 512, so the robot never sees it. It drifts off on the first straight.
  • Dim corner: 0 of 20. The floor reads about 553, above 512, so both sensors nearly always see "tape". The robot keeps its last command. It sits still, or crawls off.

Change for version 2: halve TURN to 350, to stop the twitching. And take the threshold from our CCA readings, 543, not a guess.

Version 2: smooth, but not reliable

  • CCA room: wobble fell from 2.9 to 2.2 degrees. It looked great. Then runs 9, 12, 13 and 16 left the track. Three went at the S-bend, one at C3. A gentle turn can't get round a tight bend in time.
  • Hall and dim corner: 0 of 20 again. Taking the threshold from one room's log only fixes that room.

Change for version 3: two changes, and I broke my own rule. Calibrate at the start of every run. And TURN back up to 525, halfway between the two. I allowed it because the two problems never touched: one was rooms, one was corners. The test shows each fix did its job.

Version 3: works everywhere

  • 20 of 20 in all three rooms. The thresholds it found: about 530 in the CCA room, 270 in the hall and 730 in the dim corner.
  • Lap about 13.7 s. Reliable. Also slow for a competition.

Change for version 4: speed. A speed sweep, below.

What the grid says:

  • The faster you go, the bigger the turn you need. At 1000, only the hardest turns get round, and not every time.
  • A harder turn than you need costs time and smoothness. At 900, TURN 800 is 0.19 s slower than 700 and wobbles more.
  • The fastest box with 20 of 20 is BASE 900, TURN 700. That became version 4.

Version 4: final

  • 59 of 60 runs across the three rooms. About 11 s a lap, 2.6 s faster than version 3.
  • The one miss: hall, run 13, at C4.
  • Wobble went up to 3.8 degrees. Faster means twitchier. It still holds the line.

The trade-off is real. Version 3 never missed. Version 4 is about 20% faster and missed once in 60. In a timed competition round I take version 4. For a first demo in front of parents, I would run version 3.
