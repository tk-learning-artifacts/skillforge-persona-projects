Build and wiring

What the robot is made of, where every wire goes, and the checks that stop it falling apart mid-run.

A loose wire looks exactly like a code bug. The robot twitches, stops, or one wheel dies. My team lost a whole practice to a jumper wire that only came loose on corners. So wiring is checked before code, every session.

01Parts

  • 1 micro:bit V2, on an edge-connector breakout board with pin headers.
  • 2 TCRT5000 reflectance sensor boards with an analog output (A0).
  • 1 DRV8833 dual motor driver board.
  • 2 small yellow gear motors with wheels, and 1 ball caster at the front.
  • 1 pack for 4 AA cells, with an on/off switch, for the motors. About 6 V.
  • 1 pack for 2 AA cells, with the micro:bit's JST plug. About 3 V.
  • A kit chassis plate, M3 screws and stand-offs, cable ties, and jumper wires with female ends.
  • A roll of 19 mm black electrical tape for the track.

02Wiring diagram

micro:bit pinGoes toName in the code
P1Left sensor A0LEFT_EYE
P2Right sensor A0RIGHT_EYE
P8DRV8833 AIN1LEFT_PWM
P13DRV8833 AIN2LEFT_DIR
P16DRV8833 BIN1RIGHT_PWM
P14DRV8833 BIN2RIGHT_DIR
3VBoth sensors' VCC
GNDBoth sensors, the driver, and the 6 V pack's minus

Why these pins: P1 and P2 can read analog values and are not shared with the screen or buttons. P8, P13, P14 and P16 are free too. We keep off P3, P4 and P10 (the screen), P5 and P11 (the buttons), and P12, which is kept for accessibility features.

Check your driver board's guide before you wire. Some DRV8833 boards have a sleep pin that must be pulled high before the motors will turn.

03Secure wiring: the checks

Every pair runs these before the first test of the session. The captain signs the run log.

  1. Motor pack switched off, and the 2 AA pack unplugged. Nothing is live while you work.
  2. Trace every wire against the table above with a finger. Say each pin out loud.
  3. Check that power never goes into a micro:bit pin. Only the 2 AA pack feeds the micro:bit, through its JST socket.
  4. Tug test: pull each jumper gently. If it slides off, crimp or replace it.
  5. Bundle the wires with cable ties to the chassis, with a little slack at each end. Wheels must not reach any wire.
  6. Sensors screwed to a bracket, not taped. A few millimetres off the floor. A coin under the bracket checks the height.
  7. Shake test: hold the robot over the table and shake it, gently. Switch on. The screen should still show "A".
  8. Wheel test: hold the robot up and run a one-line program that drives both wheels forward. If one spins backwards, swap its two motor wires at the driver.

04Safety

  • Switch off the motor pack before you touch any wire.
  • If a chip gets hot or smells, switch off at once. Let it cool. Look for a wire in the wrong place.
  • Use the battery type the pack says. Never mix old and new cells.
  • Keep loose cells away from keys and coins. They can short.
  • Take batteries out before the robot goes into the store room.
  • A hot, swollen or leaking battery: stop and tell the teacher. Do not use it.
  • Never put used batteries in a general bin or blue recycling bin. Use a battery or e-waste bin. Tape the ends of rechargeable ones, and seal leaking ones in a bag first.
  • Long hair tied back and sleeves clear of the wheels when testing.
