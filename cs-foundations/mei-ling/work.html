Session 1: three exit tickets

Question 1: The 8, 2 and 1 cards are up. What number is that? Question 2: Show 19. Which cards are up?

Pupil B

1. 11 ✓

2. 16 and 3 ✗

What went wrong: there is no 3 card. B found 19 = 16 + 3 and stopped. B can read cards but has not used the big-to-small steps yet.

What I wrote: "16 fits, 3 left. Does 8 fit in 3? Does 4? Does 2? Then 1 left." Next week, B makes 3 numbers with me before the laptops.

Pupil C

1. 821 ✗

2. 16, 2, 1 ✓

What went wrong: C wrote the card names side by side. She read them as hundreds, tens and ones. That is place value from maths, used in the wrong place.

What I did: I asked C first. Then I put "821" on the board next to "8 + 2 + 1 = 11". We talked about what each place is worth. It was the best 5 minutes of the session.

What the samples tell me

  • Reading cards is easy for most. Making a number needs the algorithm, and some pupils skip it.
  • The place value mistake is the most useful one. I now plan for it in session 1.
  • In Scratch, copied scripts are where the bugs live. I now say "change both numbers" before pupils copy.
