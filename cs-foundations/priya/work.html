The marking guide

All three trainers use this guide in all six classes. Each criterion gets one level.

Swap-test notes from Group B

All 5 right. No problems.

Different from the model key, and it works. We used it in class to show "many ways of classifying". The trainer asked: "Does your first question split the objects well?" It only removes one object. Starting with conductivity splits them 2 and 3. Their reason is still a real reason, so justification stays secure.

Swap-test notes from Group C

Our marble is blue inside, it is not see-through. So we got ice-cream stick. Also spoon and wire both shiny, we couldn't finish.

Two bugs, both found by the swap test, which is the point. "Shiny" ends with two objects. "See-through" depends on which marble you got. "Made of metal" names a material, not a property you test. The group changed question 1 to "Does it conduct electricity?" in the debug time.

Moderation: Trainer 2 first marked this secure. The key worked on the group's own tray. We agreed on developing, because it failed the swap test. The guide says "another group follows it".

The argument in the notes is the best evidence on this page. It is exactly why a key needs testable questions. The group saw it and fixed question 1. The trainer gave them the question bank for lesson 2, and their Scratch bot worked.

Lesson 2: one pair's Scratch bug

Pair 7, coding Group A's key

Their first version. Each question says (y/n), but each check looks for "yes".

Their test log:

ObjectBot saidRight?
rubber bandglass marbleNo
copper wireglass marbleNo
steel spoonglass marbleNo
ice-cream stickglass marbleNo
glass marbleglass marbleYes

Their explanation

The tester typed y. Our code checks for yes. y is not yes so it always goes to the no side, and the last no is glass marble.

Their fix: they changed "yes" to "y" in all four checks.

This is the bug in the trainer briefing, found on their own. The explanation traces the path all the way to the marble. That is a secure answer. They used "say for 2 seconds" instead of a guess variable. That works fine, and it was their choice.

Two exit tickets

Student X, lesson 1

1. Why is "Is it heavy?" a weak question for a key?

Because heavy is different for different people. A key has to work the same for everyone.

2. Put a cork through your key. Where does it end up? What question would you add?

It goes to ice-cream stick. Add: is it squishy?

Question 1 is secure. Question 2 finds the right place, then makes the same mistake as "heavy". The trainer wrote: "How would two people test squishy the same way?"

Student Y, lesson 2

1. Which block is the input, and which block makes the decision?

Ask and wait is the input. If else decides.

2. A tester typed "yes", and the bot went down the "no" path. Why?

The computer doesn't know yes means y.

Question 1 is secure. Question 2 is developing. It is true, but it doesn't say how the bot decides. It compares two pieces of text, and they must match.
