Build a key, then break it

Groups write a yes/no key to sort 5 everyday objects. Then another group tries to follow it exactly, and finds the bugs.

  • Sec 1 G2 or G3 Science
  • 60 minutes
  • About 36 students, 9 groups of 4
  • Science lab or classroom, no devices

Learning goals

  • Science Classify everyday objects by their physical properties. See that there is more than one way to do it.
  • CS Write an algorithm that someone else can follow exactly and get the same result.

Success criteria

  1. Every question in our key can be answered yes or no by testing or looking.
  2. Every object ends on its own, at the end of a branch.
  3. Another group followed our key and got every object right.
  4. We can say why we chose our first question.

Materials

  • 9 trays. Each holds a steel spoon, 15 cm of copper wire and a rubber band. It also holds a glass marble and a wooden ice-cream stick.
  • 9 tubs of water, each on a paper towel, and spare towels.
  • Each class's results from the earlier practical on which materials conduct electricity.
  • 9 A3 key templates and a question bank. Print them below.
  • A cork in an envelope for each tray, for the extension.

Safety

No circuits in this lesson: conductivity comes from last week's results. Keep water tubs away from sockets and laptops. Wipe spills at once, so nobody slips. Tape both ends of each copper wire. If a marble chips, students tell the trainer and do not touch it.

Trainer briefing

  • Do not give the model key. Groups must choose their own questions.
  • A group asks "Is this question OK?" Reply: "Would two people always give the same answer?"
  • The swap test is the lesson. Protect its 13 minutes, even if building runs late.
  • Collect the keys at the end. The science teacher needs them for density.

The lesson, minute by minute

TimeWhat happensTrainer says or does
0 to 5Settle into groups of 4. Trays and tubs out. Roles: reader, tester, writer, checker."Don't touch the objects yet. Look first."
5 to 12Hook. The trainer hides one object. The class may ask only yes/no questions to find it. Count the questions."A sorting machine can only ask yes/no questions. Today you write its questions. That list is an algorithm."
12 to 27Build. Each group writes a key on the A3 template. One property per question. Test floating in the tub. Use the practical results for conductivity.Circulate and ask: "Would two people always give the same answer?" Push groups past colour and size.
27 to 40Swap test. Groups pass their key and tray to the next group. The tester picks an object in secret. The reader follows the key out loud, word for word. The writer notes every place they got stuck."Follow it exactly, even if you know the answer. If it's wrong, it's their bug, not your mistake."
40 to 50Debug and share. Keys come home with the notes. Each group fixes one question. Two groups with different working keys show them side by side."Both keys work. Which one is right?" Draw out the syllabus idea. There are many ways to classify the same objects.
50 to 57Exit ticket, on your own: the 2 check questions.Collect tickets as students leave their seats.
57 to 60Pack up. Empty tubs into the sink, dry the trays, keys to the trainer.Count marbles back into the trays.

Check: 5 + 7 + 15 + 13 + 10 + 7 + 3 = 60 minutes.

Check questions

  1. Why is "Is it heavy?" a weak question for a key? (Two people can answer it differently. A key must give the same result for anyone.)
  2. Put a cork through your key. Where does it end up? What question would you add? (In the model key, it goes to the ice-cream stick. It does not conduct or stretch, and it floats. You need one more question to split cork from wood.)

For every student

  • Students who struggle: start with 3 objects, then add the others. Use the question bank, which gives the property and the test for each question.
  • Students who race ahead: add the cork. Then make the key as short as possible. With 5 objects, can any key finish in 2 questions on every branch? (No. 2 questions split at most 4 objects.)
  • Students still learning English: the template shows each object. The question bank gives the starter "Does it ...?"
  • G2 classes: the trainer writes the first question with the class, then groups carry on.
  • Quiet students: every group has a checker. Their job is to ask "Are you sure?"
