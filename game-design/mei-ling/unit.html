Paper games before Scratch

In three club sessions, P5 pupils design a maths card game. They playtest it twice and fix it. No laptops needed.

  • Who: 20 P5 pupils in my after-school coding club. 5 groups of 4.
  • Time: 3 sessions of 90 minutes, one a week. Each has a 5-minute break.
  • Why paper: rules are quick to change on paper. In week 4 we build the best ones in Scratch.
  • Per group: 30 blank index cards, 2 dice and 20 counters. Also markers, a zip bag and round-tip scissors.
  • Printouts: 5 sets of Make One cards, and 8 feedback sheets per group.
  • Safety: only round-tip scissors. I cut the card stock before the club.

By the end, pupils can

  • Say the goal, the rules and the feedback of a game.
  • Make a game where knowing the maths helps you win.
  • Watch someone play in silence, then make 2 changes from what they saw.

Success criteria, in pupil words

  1. A new player can tell me the goal after reading our rules.
  2. You need maths to win. Guessing does not work.
  3. Players can see how they are doing, with a score, a pile or a strip.
  4. We changed 2 things because of what our players did.

Session 1: Play, pull apart, plan

  1. 0 to 10 min. Arrive, attendance, water. I tell them: no laptops for three weeks, and why.
  2. 10 to 30 min. Play Make One in groups of 4.
  3. 30 to 40 min. Talk. What is the goal? Name 2 rules. How did you know you were winning?
  4. 40 to 45 min. Break.
  5. 45 to 55 min. Bingo or Make One? We play one quick round of fraction bingo. Then I ask: did the best adder win? In Make One, did they?
  6. 55 to 80 min. Plan. Each group picks a maths skill they know, like times tables or fractions. They fill a planning card: goal, 3 rules, and how a wrong answer costs.
  7. 80 to 87 min. Each group reads out one rule. Others ask one question.
  8. 87 to 90 min. Pack up. Planning cards go in the zip bag.

Check: 10 + 20 + 10 + 5 + 10 + 25 + 7 + 3 = 90 minutes.

Session 2: Make it, test it once

  1. 0 to 5 min. Arrive. Bags out.
  2. 5 to 40 min. Make. Write the cards and a rule sheet of 6 lines or fewer. Draw a picture for each step.
  3. 40 to 45 min. Break.
  4. 45 to 55 min. The silent rule. I act as a maker who keeps explaining. Pupils spot what went wrong. Then we practise zipped lips.
  5. 55 to 75 min. Round 1. Two makers stay as silent watchers. The other two go to play the next group's game. Play for 15 minutes, then fill the sheet for 5.
  6. 75 to 85 min. Groups read their sheets. They choose 2 changes and write them down.
  7. 85 to 90 min. Pack up. Everything in the bag, with the group name.

Check: 5 + 35 + 5 + 10 + 20 + 10 + 5 = 90 minutes.

Session 3: Fix it, test again, share

  1. 0 to 5 min. Arrive. Bags out.
  2. 5 to 25 min. Make the 2 changes. Write the new rules in a new colour.
  3. 25 to 45 min. Round 2. Swap jobs: last week's players now watch. Players go two groups along. Play for 15 minutes, sheets for 5.
  4. 45 to 50 min. Break.
  5. 50 to 60 min. Read the new sheets. Did the changes work?
  6. 60 to 80 min. Games fair. All 5 games are out. Pupils play any game but their own.
  7. 80 to 87 min. Sticky note: "We changed ___ because ___."
  8. 87 to 90 min. Pack up. I keep the bags for next week's Scratch build.

Check: 5 + 20 + 20 + 5 + 10 + 20 + 7 + 3 = 90 minutes.

For different pupils

  • Stuck for an idea: change one rule of Make One. Try a new target or a new card.
  • Done early: add a second way to win, then test it on me.
  • A pupil who loses badly: the next round, they are the rule-checker. It is an important job.
  • A pupil who takes over: each group has 4 jobs. Card holder, writer, timekeeper and checker. Jobs swap each session.

How I check it

I play every game once myself, by guessing. If I can win by guessing, criterion 2 is "not yet". Each criterion gets a tick, a "nearly", or a "not yet". The sticky notes show me criterion 4.
