Make One

A card game for 2 to 4 players. Take turns adding fraction cards to a cup. Make exactly 1 whole and the cup is yours.

I'm Mei Ling. I run an after-school coding club for upper primary pupils. My P5s still trip over fractions with different denominators. That is a P4 skill, so this game is revision for them. It works on paper, so it still runs when the laptops won't log in.

    Rule sheet

    You need

    The 40 cards and the fraction strip mat from the print page. 2 to 4 players. About 15 minutes.

    Goal

    Win the most cards. You win cards by filling the cup to exactly 1 whole.

    Set up

    Shuffle. Give everyone 4 cards. Put the rest face down. The cup is the middle of the table. It starts empty.

    On your turn

    1. Play 1 card into the cup. Say what the cup holds now, like "seven twelfths".
    2. The player on your left checks with the strip mat. If you are wrong, take your card back. Your turn ends.
    3. You can't take the cup over 1. If your card would, take it back. Your turn ends.
    4. Can't play, or don't want to? Draw 1 card and pass.
    5. After you play, draw back up to 4 cards.

    Win a cup

    Make exactly 1 whole and you win the cup. Keep all its cards face down. Each card is 1 point.

    Challenges

    Think someone said the wrong total? Say "check". If you are right, they take the card back. If you are wrong, you miss your next turn.

    Stuck

    If nobody can play for a whole round, clear the cup. Nobody scores it.

    End

    When the pile is gone and nobody can play, count your cards. Most cards wins.

    The feedback is on the table. The strip mat shows each total. A cup that hits 1 whole gets a cheer and a pile of cards.

    Why you need fractions to win

    • You must say each total. A wrong total costs your turn.
    • Going over 1 costs your turn too. So you must add before you play.
    • A big cup is worth more points. Do you add a small card to grow it? The next player might finish it.
    • Knowing the gap wins cups. If the cup holds 2/3, you need 1/3, or 1/6 and 1/6 over two turns.

    Luck still decides what you draw. But a pupil who adds well wins more cups. In my old fraction bingo, the pupils who knew most did not win more.

    Playtest notes from 2 rounds

    My club and pupils are made up, like me. The card counts and fractions are real.

    Round 1: four P5 pupils, version 1

    Version 1 had eighths too. Going over 1 just spilled the cup, with no cost. A won cup was 1 point, however many cards it held. I watched and said nothing.

    • Note 1. Pupils slammed down their biggest card every turn. Going over cost nothing.
    • Note 2. Nobody said the totals. Two pupils argued: does 1/2 and 3/8 make 1?
    • Note 3. 1/3 and 1/8 stopped the game for 2 minutes. The answer is 11/24.
    • Note 4. The game took 24 minutes. I planned 15.
    • Note 5. A cup of 1 card scored the same as a cup of 6.

    Changes for version 2

    • From note 1: you can't go over 1. If you try, the card comes back and your turn ends.
    • From note 2: the "say it" rule, and the strip mat to check.
    • From notes 3 and 4: no more eighths. Every card is in halves, thirds, quarters, sixths or twelfths. All of them fit into twelfths.
    • From note 5: each card in a won cup is 1 point.

    Round 2: four other P5 pupils, version 2

    • Note 1. The game took 16 minutes. Close enough.
    • Note 2. Pupil J sat across the table. She could not read 5/12 upside down. The numbers were small.
    • Note 3. Pupil K is quiet. She never checked a total. Later she told me she saw a wrong one.
    • Note 4. Pupil L said "check" on every turn, to slow the others down.
    • Note 5. Pupil M found a trick. Leave a gap of 7/12, and no single card can fill it.

    Changes for version 3, the final game

    • From note 2: big numbers at both ends of each card. Words and a strip too.
    • From note 3: the player on your left always checks. Everyone gets a turn to check.
    • From note 4: a wrong challenge means you miss your next turn.
    • From note 5: nothing. That is a real choice, and I want pupils to find it.

    Accessibility check

    I checked the cards and online game against 8 Basic Game Accessibility Guidelines.

    • "Use an easily readable default font size." Fixed: big numbers at both ends.
    • "Ensure no essential information is conveyed by a fixed colour alone." Fixed: number, word and strip.
    • "Use simple clear language." Met: the rule sheet uses short lines.
    • "Provide high contrast between text/UI and background." Met: black print on white card.
    • "Allow players to progress through text prompts at their own pace." Met: no timer.
    • "Ensure no essential information is conveyed by sounds alone." Met: no sound.
    • "Ensure interactive elements / virtual controls are large and well spaced." Met: cards 72 px or wider.
    • "Offer a wide choice of difficulty levels." Partly met. There is a starter deck of halves and quarters, and the full deck. Two levels is not a wide choice.

    What I would change

    Luck still matters a lot in a 2-player game. A hand full of 2/3 and 3/4 cards can stall you for three turns. With 4 players it evens out. I would also like a middle deck with thirds and sixths. And the game only practises adding. Taking away never comes up.
