Make a maths arcade game

A 3-lesson unit for a Sec 1 class. Pairs design, build and playtest a game where winning needs the maths.

  • Class: 32 Sec 1 students, working in 16 pairs.
  • Time: 3 lessons of 60 minutes, one a week.
  • Devices: one laptop per pair, with a browser. No sign-in needed.
  • Tool: MakeCode Arcade, in blocks.
  • Maths: any Sec 1 topic the class has learnt. Primes, integers and ratio work well.
  • Printouts: 1 A4 design card per pair, and 4 feedback sheets per pair.

By the end, students can

  • Name the goal, rules, feedback and core loop of a game.
  • Build a game where a wrong maths answer costs the player.
  • Run a silent playtest and make one change from what they saw.

Success criteria

  1. A new player can say the goal after reading the rule screen.
  2. A player who guesses does worse than one who knows the maths.
  3. The game answers each press with a sound, a move or words.
  4. The pair made one change and can point to the note behind it.

Lesson 1: Play it, pull it apart, plan yours

  1. 0 to 5 min. Settle. Pairs open Prime Smash version 1 on one laptop.
  2. 5 to 15 min. Play. Version 1 for 4 minutes, then version 3 for 6 minutes.
  3. 15 to 25 min. Talk. What changed? Which version needs the maths? Write goal, rules and feedback on the board.
  4. 25 to 33 min. Mini lesson. Show the button-masher score from version 1. Ask: does guessing win in your game?
  5. 33 to 53 min. Plan. Each pair picks a topic and fills the A4 design card. It asks for the goal, 3 rules and the core loop. It also asks what a wrong answer costs.
  6. 53 to 58 min. Exit ticket. One line: what does your player do again and again?
  7. 58 to 60 min. Pack up. Design cards go in the class tray.

Check: 5 + 10 + 10 + 8 + 20 + 5 + 2 = 60 minutes.

Lesson 2: Build the core loop

  1. 0 to 5 min. Settle. Hand back design cards. Open MakeCode Arcade.
  2. 5 to 15 min. Demo. I build the falling block, a number variable and the A button check, live. Students copy step by step.
  3. 15 to 45 min. Build. Pairs swap the driver every 10 minutes. Goal for today: one block, one check, one sound.
  4. 45 to 50 min. Quick try. Each pair plays the next pair's game for 2 minutes, then swaps back.
  5. 50 to 55 min. Save. Each pair clicks Share and pastes the link in the class sheet.
  6. 55 to 60 min. Pack up and tidy cables.

Check: 5 + 10 + 30 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 60 minutes.

MakeCode saves projects in the browser on that laptop. Clearing the cache can remove them, so the share link is the backup. A share link made without sign-in can't be removed, except by reporting it. So: no names, faces or school details in any game.

Lesson 3: Silent playtest, change, test again

  1. 0 to 5 min. Settle. Open your game from the class sheet.
  2. 5 to 10 min. Brief. Show the feedback sheet. The rule: the makers stay silent and only write.
  3. 10 to 18 min. Round 1. Pairs swap laptops. One pair plays, the other watches and notes.
  4. 18 to 22 min. Players fill the feedback sheet. Makers read it.
  5. 22 to 34 min. Change. Each pair makes one change from one note. They write the note and the change on the back of the sheet.
  6. 34 to 42 min. Round 2. Swap with a new pair. Same silent rule.
  7. 42 to 46 min. Players fill a second sheet.
  8. 46 to 54 min. Show. Three pairs show their change and the note behind it.
  9. 54 to 58 min. Reflect. Write one line: did your change work? How do you know?
  10. 58 to 60 min. Pack up. Final share links go in the class sheet.

Check: 5 + 5 + 8 + 4 + 12 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 4 + 2 = 60 minutes.

For different students

  • Stuck on code: give them my starter with the falling block done. They add the maths check.
  • Finished early: add a second level, or a score that rewards no mistakes.
  • Not keen on screens: make the game with cards and dice. Playtest it the same way.
  • A student who takes over: drivers swap every 10 minutes, always. I keep a timer on the board.

How I mark it

I use the four success criteria above. Each gets "yes", "nearly" or "not yet", with one line of comment. Criterion 2 matters most. I test it myself by guessing.
