Classroom management plan

Lab 2, Sec 1 coding: my plan

Grace Chua. For a class of 36. One 60-minute double period a week, in a lab of 40 laptops. Written so a new trainer could take the class over.

1. What I believe

Students behave well when they know what to do and believe I'm on their side. They also need to see that I notice. So I put most of my effort into the first six weeks. I teach routines like I teach code. I show it, they try it, I give feedback. When something goes wrong, I use the smallest response that works. I correct the behaviour, never the person. Every lesson starts fresh.

This draws on four ideas. PBIS asks for 3 to 5 positive expectations. They are taught, and students get specific praise for meeting them. Responsive Classroom's Interactive Modeling gives me the steps for teaching a routine. Kounin's withitness reminds me that students need to see that I see. The Marzanos' research puts relationships and procedures side by side.

2. The room

Desks run along three walls, so every screen faces into the room. From the middle, I can see all of them. Bags and PLDs (personal learning devices) go on the middle table, zipped. We use the lab computers, so PLDs stay away unless I ask for them. We hold short talks at the middle table, away from screens.

3. Our three expectations

  • Be ready. Log in and start the Do Now. Lids down when I say so.
  • Be kind. Take turns at the keyboard. Use helpful words with your partner. Try the stuck ladder before calling me.
  • Look after the lab. No food or drink near the computers. Report damage straight away. Leave your desk as you found it.

Each is on a poster. I teach one per lesson in Weeks 1 to 3. Each gets a "looks like, sounds like" talk.

4. Six routines

Coming in

I stand at the door and greet each student by name. Bags and PLDs go on the middle table. Students sit, log in and start the Do Now on the board. It's a 4-minute task they can do alone. I take attendance while they work.

Lids down

I raise one hand and say "Lids down". Students push their laptop lids halfway down. They take their hands off the mouse. They turn their chairs to face me and look up. I count down from 5 on my fingers, silently. I never talk over screens.

Getting help: the stuck ladder

  1. Read the step card again, slowly.
  2. Ask your partner.
  3. Check the help sheet on the wall.
  4. Put the red card on top of your screen. Carry on with the next step. I come to red cards in order.

Pairs

I set the pairs in a seating plan, mixed by confidence. I change them every three weeks. Pairs use one computer. The driver types and the navigator reads the step card. They swap when the board timer rings, every 10 minutes.

Packing up

Packing up starts at 5 minutes to the end. Save, share the link in the class sheet, then log out. Headphones go on the hook and chairs go in. Students collect their bags and wait. I check desks and dismiss by row.

Emergency stop

For anything unsafe, I say "Stop. Hands up." Everyone lifts both hands off the desk and faces me. We practise this in Week 1, so it works when it matters. For a fire alarm, we follow the school drill and leave the computers on.

5. Noticing what goes right

I praise the behaviour, specifically and quietly: "You tried the stuck ladder before calling me. That's why you're on Step 5." I aim for much more praise than correction. Once a week, I tally both on my clipboard for one lesson to check. I don't use a points app. I haven't yet seen one hold up with Sec 1s past Term 1.

6. When things go wrong: the response ladder

I start at the bottom. I go up one step only if the step before didn't work. Anything unsafe skips the ladder and gets the emergency stop.

  1. Scan and keep teaching. I make sure the student can see I've noticed.
  2. Move closer. I keep teaching from beside their desk.
  3. The look, or a signal. Eye contact for a second, or a lids-down hand.
  4. A quiet word. Name, task and time: "Ravi, Step 3 please. I'll check back in two minutes." Then I walk away.
  5. An I-statement and a choice. Thomas Gordon's three parts: the behaviour, its effect and my feeling. "When the game is open, I can't see your code. I'm worried you'll fall behind. Close it now, or work at the front computer."
  6. A talk after class. Two minutes at the door, never in front of the class. We agree on one thing for next week. If it happens three times in two weeks, I talk to the form teacher.

7. Supporting different learners

  • Reading load: step cards have one step per line. Each has a picture of the block.
  • Attention: students who drift sit near my walking loop, not at the back.
  • Noise: headphones are allowed during solo work, one ear free.
  • Special educational needs: I ask the school's SEN Officer and form teacher what has worked. Then I add that to the seating plan and step cards.
  • Extra support: some students get a quick check-in at the door. They also get a target card for one behaviour. This is the PBIS idea of extra support for some students, in small form.
  • Fast finishers: a challenge card for each step. Being done never means free time on games.

8. Tracking and review

I keep A-B-C notes on my clipboard. A is what happened before. B is the behaviour as I saw it. C is what happened after. I write facts, not guesses about feelings. Every Friday, I look for patterns, such as the same task or time. Notes go into the school's own system, never my phone. At Week 6, I review the plan. I keep what worked and change one routine that didn't.

9. The first six weeks

  • Week 1: coming in, lids down, emergency stop, Be ready.
  • Week 2: the stuck ladder, Be kind.
  • Week 3: pairs and swapping, Look after the lab. New seating plan.
  • Week 4: packing up with share links.
  • Weeks 5 and 6: practise everything without reminders. Review.

Week 1 lesson: 60 minutes

  1. 0 to 5 min. Greet at the door. Seats from the plan. Log in.
  2. 5 to 10 min. Who I am. What this class will make this term.
  3. 10 to 22 min. Teach lids down with Interactive Modeling. I model it, they notice, one student models, all practise three times.
  4. 22 to 30 min. Teach the emergency stop the same way.
  5. 30 to 50 min. First coding task, in pairs. I praise lids down and the stuck ladder when I see them.
  6. 50 to 55 min. Talk at the middle table: what does "Be ready" look like here?
  7. 55 to 60 min. Pack up, first time with me talking it through.

Check: 5 + 5 + 12 + 8 + 20 + 5 + 5 = 60 minutes.
