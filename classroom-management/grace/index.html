Classroom Management: end project

Lab 2, first six weeks

A classroom management plan for a Sec 1 coding class in a computer lab. It sets out the routines, three expectations and my steps when a lesson wobbles.

I'm Grace. I taught for twenty years, and now I mentor new trainers. I wrote this plan for my own class. I also wrote it so a new trainer could use it on Monday. The class meets once a week for a 60-minute double period. There are 36 students and 40 laptops. The class, the students and their names are made up, like me.

What the plan rests on

Most behaviour problems in a lab start with an unclear first five minutes. So most of my plan is about what happens before anything goes wrong. Students come in the same way every week. They know where help comes from. They know what "lids down" means, because we practised it.

When something does go wrong, I use the smallest response that works. Then I go up one step at a time. Opening a game and throwing a mouse should not get the same response.

Four ideas I lean on

  • Teach expectations like content. PBIS asks schools for 3 to 5 positive expectations. They must be taught as well as posted. I have three.
  • Show routines, then practise them. I use Responsive Classroom's Interactive Modeling: I model, students notice, a student models, everyone practises.
  • Let them see that you see. Kounin called this withitness. In a lab, that means a layout where I can see every screen.
  • Relationships first, routines soon after. The Marzanos' book gives a chapter to each: rules and procedures, and teacher-student relationships. A warm teacher with no routines still loses the room.

Why an animation and not a recording

This page can't show a real class. I wouldn't film students for a public page anyway. So the video is a moving floor plan. You see where I stand, where I look and what I say. That is what a recording would teach you.

Film your own, safely

  1. Ask two colleagues to play the students. Give each a card: "open a game" or "keep chatting".
  2. Check your school's rules on filming before you film any real class.
  3. Film from a back corner, so you get the whole room and no faces.
  4. Run it three times. Each time, try to say fewer words.
  5. Watch it back with the sound off. Could you tell who was off task, and when you saw it?

What I would change

The plan leans on me walking the room. With desks along the walls, I reach anyone in a few steps. In a lab with fixed rows, moving closer is slower. It is also easier to read as a threat. That room would need a seating change before this plan works.

I also run every routine myself for now. The Marzanos' book has a chapter on the student's responsibility for management. I'd like to hand some of this over. Lab captains could check desks at pack-up. I'll wait until Week 6, when the routines hold without me.
