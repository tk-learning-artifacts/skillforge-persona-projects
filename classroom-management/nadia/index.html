Many ways in

A classroom management plan for a P5 micro:bit unit. Every routine comes as a picture, a word, and a sound or a gesture. No pupil has to rely on just one of them.

I'm Nadia. I teach pupils with a wide range of learning needs. This plan is for a P5 class of 30 in our own form classroom. Pairs code micro:bits on laptops from the trolley. Lessons are 60 minutes: two periods back to back. The class and pupils here are made up, like me.

Who is in the room

  • Two pupils with dyslexia. Long written steps slow them down.
  • One pupil with ADHD who does best with short tasks and a job to do.
  • One autistic pupil who finds noise and sudden changes hard.
  • A few pupils still building their English.
  • Several fast finishers who drift once they're done.
  • The school's SEN Officer joins one lesson a week.

These notes are for planning only. No labels go on posters, seating plans or the board.

  • The written plan

    Three A4 pages: beliefs, routines, breaks, support steps and tracking.

  • Four posters

    Class promises, desk cards, stuck steps and the calm corner.

  • The video

    The break card, from "I need a break" to back at work.

  • Now and Next board

    A visual schedule and timer for the class screen. Ready to use.

Why many ways in

One rule for everyone sounds fair. It isn't fair if some pupils can't read the rule. So I plan for the whole range from the start. I don't wait to fix things for one pupil later.

This idea is called Universal Design for Learning (UDL). CAST's UDL Guidelines have three principles: engagement, representation, and action and expression. In plain words: ways to get interested, to take in information, and to show what you learned.

In my room, each routine has a picture card, a short phrase and a sound or gesture. Pupils can show their learning by demo, drawing, telling me or writing. Visual supports have strong research behind them for autistic learners. They include schedules, First, Then boards and choice boards. A 2020 review by the National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice counted them as evidence-based. I use them with the whole class. Then nobody stands out for having a card.

A is Aliya. Me is the teacher. Desk cards: green circle means working, orange square means help, blue triangle means break. About 1 in 12 men have trouble telling some colours apart. So each card has its own shape, and colour is never the only clue.

Read the transcript

  2. 0:06 Pairs are coding a micro:bit step counter. The room is getting loud. I'm helping a pair at the first table. Their orange square asked for help.
  3. 0:18 Aliya turns her desk card to the blue triangle. It means "I need a break". She doesn't need to say anything or put up her hand.
  4. 0:24 I see it from where I am. I give her a thumbs-up. That's the whole answer. No questions, and no "are you sure?"
  5. 0:34 She walks to the calm corner. Her partner keeps coding. She turns the five-minute timer and puts on headphones.
  6. 0:47 I don't follow her. Hovering would turn a break into a talk. The timer shows her how long is left.

  8. 1:06 One small step to restart, not the whole task again. She turns her card back to the green circle.
  9. 1:13 I write down the time of the break. Patterns tell me what to change.

Why an animation

I won't put a real pupil's break on a public page. A break is private, and that is part of why it works. The animation shows what a recording would: where I stand, what I don't say, and how long it takes.

Film your own role-play

  1. Ask a colleague to play the pupil. Give them the break card and nothing else.
  2. Check your school's rules on filming. Film adults only.
  3. Film from the side of the room so you see both the desk and the calm corner.
  4. Watch it back. Count your words from the card going up to the pupil sitting down. My target is zero.
  5. Add captions before you share it with your team.

What I would change

The calm corner can turn into a way out of the hard part. In week 3, two pupils used it every time testing started. I don't want to ask for a reason. That breaks the routine for the pupils who need it. For now, I look at my break notes and change the task, such as shorter test steps. I haven't solved this yet.

The plan also needs a corner. Our form classroom has one. The computer lab doesn't, so this plan would need a rethink there.
